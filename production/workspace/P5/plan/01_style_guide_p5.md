# Style Guide — "TheOneApp, Explained" · Primary 5 series

This guide carries over **every** rule of the P3 style guide (`P3/plan/01_style_guide_p3.md`, which itself carries every P4 rule) and the teacher's standing rules, then adds the P5 adjustments for 10–11-year-olds and the new **real-photo** convention. Where a P5 rule changes a P3 rule, the P5 rule wins for P5 episodes. The rules scripts lean on most are repeated here so nobody has to open three files.

---

## 1. Voice

- A warm, curious teacher talking to ONE 10-year-old who is about to meet fair tests, tables and line graphs properly for the first time: "you", short sentences, one idea per sentence, but **the science words arrive sooner** than in P3. Name the thing, show it, then use the term for the rest of the episode.
- Spoken English, not textbook English. No Singlish in the narration (both countries watch).
- **Cause, then effect, every time:** "The water gains heat from the flame, so it boils." "The ice loses heat to the air… no: the air is warmer, so the ice gains heat from the air and melts." Say **gains heat from / loses heat to**; never "cold gets in", never "the cold makes it".
- Every claim is accurate to the P5 syllabus. Simplify, but never say something a P6 teacher would have to unteach. Never: particles, molecules, atoms, *energy* (the P6 word in this app: say "gains heat"), xylem / phloem (say **water-carrying tubes** / **food-carrying tubes**), voltage, "current" as a noun on screen (say "electricity flows"), "cold flows".
- Numbers are real and friendly: 0 °C, 100 °C, three batteries, five seeds, 1 ml of water, 10 minutes. Never invent a measured value: use the Activity Book's set-ups or say "say, ten grams".
- Hedge words stay where the source has them (*most*, *usually*, *may*).

## 2. Positive language (unchanged)

No "cheat", "trap", "stupid", "kill", "dead", "die", "gross", "bad". Swaps as in P3: "Exam tip", "stop living", "harmful", "a medicine that stops harmful bacteria from growing". Human reproduction is taught **plainly and clinically** with the syllabus words (sperm, egg, testes, ovaries, fertilisation, fallopian tube, uterus, womb) and nothing else; no jokes, no euphemisms, no slang. Science words that only sound negative are fine (extinct, repel, pollution, waste substances, overcrowding, competition).

## 3. Pacing

- Kit timings unchanged (0.55 s between lines, 1.0 s between scenes). P5 segments may run three or four sentences; one visual change every 2–3 s.
- **Voice 3** (Heart, expressive) stays the series voice. Speed 0.92 statements, 0.86 questions.
- Three touches per idea: say it, show it, say it again in the Exam corner.

## 4. Narrator (unchanged)

Heart, voice 3 (Kokoro `af_heart`, en-us), one voice start to finish. Neutral English ("your parents"). Numbers as words where a cue is needed ("experiment two point four", "one hundred degrees Celsius"). Every word in cmudict or `pron.txt`. P5 watch-list words: *anther, filament, stigma, style, ovary, ovule, pistil, stamen, pollen, pollination, fertilisation, germination, fallopian, uterus, testes, ovaries, sperm, heredity, offspring, evaporation, condensation, vapour, humidity, retort, Bunsen, limewater, gaseous, windpipe, conductor, insulator, parallel, NEWater, Marina, reservoir, Le Le (panda), Kai Kai, Jia Jia*.

## 5. Structure (3:30–4:45, about 480–640 authored spoken words)

Same skeleton as P3/P4: Hook (≤ 35 words) → kit welcome with **three goals** → body beats (one spoken Singapore anchor and one spoken US bridge inside named beats) → Try-it (School Lab card + spoken line, or a safe home test) → **Pause and predict** (`quiz1`/`quiz2`, the 4-second silent "Think!" countdown) → **Exam corner** (at most three full definitions) → atomic habit (≤ 30 words) → mind map on the TheOneApp phone (narration names only the middle word and the branches) → close with ONE offline ask → spoken goodbye.

P5 changes:
- **Four quiz options (A, B, C, D)**, as the Activity Book's MCQs have four. The quiz is still a **prediction on a new situation**, never recall of the previous beat; at least one wrong option is a real misconception from the brief; the beat before `quiz1` must not state the answer.
- **One "Think like a scientist" beat per investigation episode:** name the variable changed, the variables kept the same and the variable measured (the P5 variables chips: *changed · kept the same · measured*). Every fair-test mention says all three.
- **Explain-answer frame for exam-style explanations:** cause → process → result, in the teacher's trigger-bank wording ("Warmer water vapour from the surroundings loses heat to the cooler surface of the can and condenses into water droplets"). The Exam corner may show one such explanation in place of a definition.
- **Tail length:** exam + habit1 + habit2 ≤ 130 spoken words. Hook ≤ 35 words. Whole episode ≤ 640 authored words.
- Close: one offline ask that fits an older child ("Explain it to someone at home using the words gains heat and loses heat", "Try the fair test with two towels at home"). Never "tell me in the comments".

## 6. The teacher's standing rules (checked before every render)

1. **Definition accuracy.** Every definition shown or spoken uses the FULL wording from `definitions_p5.md`. Prefer, in this order: the teacher's recitation table / trigger bank → the syllabus outcome → the Activity Book's "Let's Conclude" line → standard P5 wording (flagged "textbook unverified" until the teacher checks the textbook, which this plan could not read: the P5 textbook scans hold no text).
2. **Plain label lines**, no arrowheads, no numbered markers. Arrows only for motion, flow or direction (water rising in the stem, blood round the body, electricity round a circuit, the water cycle loop, pollen carried to a stigma).
3. **Wrong statements:** ✗ at the start only, no strikeout, only on statements that are actually false.
4. **Positive language.**
5. **Realism** (section 10), now including photos (section 11).
6. **Every named skill or term becomes a key word, with its full name** ("WATER-CARRYING TUBES", "RATE OF EVAPORATION", "EXPOSED SURFACE AREA", "CLOSED CIRCUIT", "ELECTRICAL CONDUCTOR", "SEED DISPERSAL", "FAIR TEST", "VARIABLE CHANGED").
7. **The 4-second silent "Think!" countdown** before every quiz answer; the question stays on screen during the countdown.
8. **School Lab card plus the spoken link** wherever a P5 lab matches: "You can try it in The One App. Open the School Lab, experiment two point four, and press, Let's test it now!" (letters spoken: "one point five A"). The teaser names what the bench really holds (`labs_p5.json`).
9. **Mind-map habit with the real app steps**, chapter names from `app_p5.md` §4 (the P5 Mind Lab posters: Reproduction in Plants, The Water Cycle, Plant System, Circulatory System, Electrical System). Mind-map wording matches the poster.
10. **Bigger objects filling the empty space.**
11. **Welcome with three goals and a spoken goodbye.**
12. **Split files over about 14 MB** for the review board; free tools only.

## 7. P5 adjustments for 10–11-year-olds

- Sentences of one idea, up to about 16 words. Two clauses are fine when the second is the effect.
- **Tables and graphs on screen** when the source has them: a 3–5-row results table, a line graph with labelled axes and units (temperature against time with the flat part during melting or boiling; mass of towel against minutes; brightness against number of batteries). Read the graph aloud: "The line stays flat here. The temperature stays at zero while the ice melts."
- **The three variables chips** appear on every investigation: *Changed* · *Kept the same* · *Measured*, filled in before the test runs.
- **Compare beats use a two-column table** (similarities / differences): human vs flowering-plant reproduction, evaporation vs boiling, series vs parallel, plant transport vs human circulation, plant vs fish vs human gaseous exchange.
- **Exam language is practised, not just shown:** each Exam corner line is a sentence a P5 pupil can write in a structured question, and one "Exam tip" beat per episode names the mix-up the trigger bank targets.
- **Concrete Singapore examples:** Le Le the panda cub, the fertility-rate graph, the school garden flower walk, rambutan and durian seeds, saga seeds, the wet floor after rain at the void deck, a cold can of drink at the hawker centre, foggy spectacles leaving an air-conditioned mall, NEWater and Marina Reservoir, dengue and stagnant water, the MRT cabin's ventilation, the HDB kitchen's circuit breaker.
- **US bridges:** Grade 5, a dandelion clock, maple "helicopter" seeds, a bald-eagle nest, the science fair, a cold soda can in July, a dehumidifier, a humid Florida afternoon, the Grand Canyon's dry air, a string of Christmas lights (series: one bulb out, all out), a Thanksgiving kitchen's breaker.
- **Characters:** never the textbook's cast names (Ali, Belle, Chen, Devi, Mr Bala, Mrs Lim). Use "a girl in class", "your friend", "you".
- **Safety line and "Ask an adult" badge** on: boiling water and the Bunsen burner (teacher's demonstration only; goggles), hot water for condensation, the table lamp (hot), any home circuit test (batteries only, never the mains; never poke anything into a wall socket), the electricity-safety episode (wet hands, overloaded sockets, frayed wires), seed handling ("wash your hands"), the flower dissection ("tell an adult if you are allergic to pollen").
- **No cells drawn with organelles**, no nucleus names: "a cell is the basic unit of life", seen as a repeating pattern under a microscope, is the whole P5 idea. (Confirm with the teacher: the lab brief 1.1 says the teacher ruled cells outside this level for the School Lab; the 2023 syllabus lists "recognise that a cell is a basic unit of life" for P5.)
- **Human reproduction:** diagrams are plain, labelled outlines (male: testes; female: ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus/womb, vagina as the syllabus names them). Fertilisation is "a sperm cell fuses with an egg cell". No foetal development, no umbilical-cord mechanism (syllabus excludes). The baby "develops in the uterus (womb)"; the mother's blood passes oxygen and digested food to the baby's blood and takes carbon dioxide and waste away (trigger bank p. 13–14).
- **Plant reproduction exclusions:** no pollen-tube formation, no self-/cross-pollination terms, no vegetative propagation (stem cuttings were P4).

## 8. On-screen conventions (kit-built, unchanged)

Bright, colourful look; a new background per scene; Fredoka font; cartoon outlines; key-word pills to the ⭐ KEY WORDS shelf (aim for 8–12 per episode); word-by-word captions; the episode tag "🔍 TheOneApp, Explained - <topic>"; chalkboard Exam corner with green ✓ lines; temperatures °C first with °F small underneath; 1920 × 1080, 60 fps.

## 9. Text layout rules (unchanged)

Text never spills or overlaps; reserved zones (caption band y > 950, keyword shelf x > 1480, tag top left); run the layout audit after prep and fix every flag of 0.5 s or more.

## 10. Drawing quality and realism (P5 additions in bold)

All P3/P4 rules hold (connected parts, level liquids, blue water, pink/maroon organs, red blood, off-white bones, arrows only for motion). P5 additions: **a flower's anther sits on top of its filament (together the stamen), the stigma on top of the style above the ovary (together the pistil), ovules inside the ovary; pollen grains are tiny dots; a wind-pollinated flower is small, dull and has feathery stigmas and dangling anthers, an insect-pollinated flower is large, bright and often scented; dispersal: wind seeds have wings or hairs, water fruits have fibrous husks or air spaces and float, animal-dispersed fruits are fleshy or hooked, splitting pods are dry; a germinating bean shows the root first, then the shoot; melting ice sits in water at 0 °C with the thermometer reading 0; boiling water has bubbles forming throughout the liquid, not only on top, at 100 °C; condensation droplets form on the OUTSIDE of a cold can and are clear water (never coloured, even when the water inside is coloured: AB 2.3); celery dye climbs through the water-carrying tubes, which show as coloured dots on the cut stalk; the heart is drawn with two sides and the lungs beside it, blood vessels red (away from the heart) and blue-red (toward the heart) only if the brief says so, otherwise all red; the windpipe branches into two lungs; a circuit's wires join battery terminals to the bulb's two contacts, a switch opens a visible gap, series bulbs sit in ONE loop, parallel bulbs each sit in their OWN loop; a brighter bulb has bigger rays; one removed bulb in series leaves a gap so all go out.**

## 11. Real photos (new for P5)

The teacher asked for real-life images in the P5 series because the pupils are older and the science is closer to real investigations. Rule: **photos for the real thing, drawings for the mechanism.**

- **Where a photo earns its place:** anything a pupil will meet in a real exam picture or a real lab: cells under a microscope (onion skin, waterweed leaf), a dissected flower, insect- vs wind-pollinated flowers, seeds and fruits (saga, rambutan, maple, dandelion, coconut, balsam pod), germinating beans, crushed ice with a thermometer, boiling water with bubbles, droplets on a cold can, wet clothes on a line in sun and wind, clouds and rain over the sea, Marina Barrage, a celery stalk stained with dye, a split stem, a lung model or X-ray outline, a real battery-bulb-wire circuit, a switch, a circuit breaker, a string of lights.
- **Where a drawing stays:** the process itself: pollen moving to the stigma, the fusing of sperm and egg, the inside of the ovary, water climbing the tubes, the blood loop, electricity flowing round a loop, the water cycle loop, every labelled diagram (labels with plain lines go on top of photos too).
- **Sources, in order of preference:**
  1. **The teacher's own photos and video stills** (classroom set-ups, school garden flowers, kitchen experiments). No licence questions, Singapore-real, and they match the School Lab. The teacher puts them in a Google Drive folder named **"P5 video photos"** with one sub-folder per episode (`EP07`), and the build reads them through the Drive connector.
  2. **Public-domain or CC0 photos** (Wikimedia Commons CC0 / PD, NASA, USGS, Pixabay). No credit required, but we still list the source in the description.
  3. **CC BY / CC BY-SA photos** (Wikimedia Commons): allowed with a credit. The credit goes **on screen as a small chip** ("Photo: <author>, CC BY 4.0") for the frames that show it, **and** in the video description under "Photo credits".
  - **Never:** textbook or activity-book pictures, Dreamstime/iStock/Shutterstock/Getty images, Google Images results of unknown licence, pupils' faces, the school's name or crest.
- **Network note:** the cloud session cannot reach Wikimedia, Unsplash, Pexels or Pixabay (the environment's network policy denies them). Either the teacher adds `commons.wikimedia.org` and `upload.wikimedia.org` to the environment's Allowed domains, or the photos come through Drive (option 1). Drive is open today.
- **How the kit shows a photo:** a `photo(stage, src, {x, y, w, h, radius, kenburns, credit})` helper draws the image inside a rounded frame on the SVG stage, with an optional slow push-in (Ken Burns) over the beat, and a credit chip bottom-left of the frame. Labels, ✓/✗ lines, chips and key-word pops are drawn over it as usual. Photos are JPEG, long edge 1600 px, under 400 KB, stored in `R/epN/photos/`. The still is pre-cropped to the frame's ratio so nothing important is cut.
- **Each brief carries a "Photo shot list":** segment id → subject → what must be visible → preferred source (teacher / Commons search phrase) → fallback drawing if no photo arrives. The script is written so that it works with the fallback drawing too; a missing photo never blocks a render.
- **Mixing rules:** at most one photo on screen at a time unless the beat compares two things (then two frames side by side, same size); keep the cartoon background and chips so the series still looks like one series; no photo under the Exam corner, the quiz options, the mind map or the end card.

## 12. Two audiences, one video

One Singapore anchor and one US bridge spoken inside named beats (section 7 lists them); extra ones in the description.

## 13. Kit conventions (unchanged from `P3/kit/README.md`)

Three authored files per episode (`epNN_segments.json`, `epNN.json`, `scenes_epN.js`); first id `hook`; `quiz1` then `quiz2` in scene `quiz`; `habit2` in scene `mindmap` saying "save" and "tap"; cue words stripped to a–z and `'`; `kw` 1–14; `lab` config or `null`; `mm` phone shot; `end` lines; `output` name `P5_EPNN_<Name>.mp4`. New config key for P5: `"photos": {"<segId>": [{"src": "photos/<file>.jpg", "box": [x, y, w, h], "credit": "…"}]}` (added to the kit with the `photo()` helper before the first P5 render).

## 14. Pre-render checklist (P5)

1. Every definition line matches `definitions_p5.md` word for word; lines flagged "textbook unverified" were checked by the teacher against the textbook.
2. Cause-then-effect wording with "gains heat from / loses heat to"; no particles, molecules, energy, xylem, phloem, voltage, current-as-a-noun.
3. Variables chips on every investigation; the fair test names changed, kept the same, measured.
4. No numbered stages, no arrowheads on label lines, no strikeout, ✗ only on false lines.
5. Positive language sweep; human reproduction plain and clinical.
6. Key words with full names; cue words in stripped form.
7. Three goals, welcome, four-option prediction quiz passing the four rules, lab card + spoken line, mind map matching the Mind Lab poster, one offline ask, goodbye.
8. Realism sweep (section 10) and photo sweep (section 11): every photo has a source and a licence; CC BY credits on screen and in the description; no textbook or stock-agency images.
9. Layout audit clean.
10. Safety badge where needed (section 7).
11. The lab on the card is signed off (status `ready`) before the video goes public and does not contradict the video.
12. Tail length ≤ 130 words; hook ≤ 35; episode ≤ 640 authored words.
13. One Singapore anchor and one US bridge spoken in a named beat.
