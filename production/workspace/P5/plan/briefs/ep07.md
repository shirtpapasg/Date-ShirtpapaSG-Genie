# EP 07 — No fertilisation, no fruit: what happens after pollination

## Big idea
Fertilisation happens when the male reproductive cell from the pollen fuses with the egg cell in the ovule; the ovule develops into a seed and the ovary into a fruit; a flower that forms no fruit was not fertilised.

## Misconceptions to bust
- Pollination and fertilisation are the same → pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma; fertilisation is the fusing of the male reproductive cell with the egg cell in the ovule (BRIEF-P5 §4; misconception M-30; `app_p5.md` §5 gap note). → the `tip` beat.
- A seed starts growing the moment pollen lands → fertilisation must happen first (lab 1.5D prediction reason "A seed starts growing the moment pollen lands").
- The fruit comes from the ovule and the seed from the ovary → fruit from the OVARY, seeds from the OVULES (Mix-up buster #46).
- Every part of the flower is needed to make a fruit → the petals and the anthers can go if pollen comes from another flower; the stigma and the ovary are needed (CfU Q2; row 1.52 note). → wrong quiz options A, C and D.
- "Pollen joins the ovule" → the male reproductive cell from pollen fuses with the egg cell in the ovule (row 1.48 note).

## Exam wording box

## Pause and predict
- **Q:** Before pollination, a gardener removes two parts from a flower. Bees still bring pollen from other flowers. Which two parts can she remove, and the flower can still form a fruit?
- **A** the stigma and the ovary · **B** the petals and the anthers · **C** the style and the stigma · **D** the ovary and the ovules
- **Answer: B.** Pollen from other flowers can still reach the stigma, so the flower needs neither its own anthers nor its petals; the stigma must receive the pollen, the ovules hold the egg cells and the ovary becomes the fruit.
- Checks: a new situation (a text version of the CfU Q2 idea, with new wording and options); the question does not contain "fuses", "develops into a fruit" or "fertilisation"; the `tip` before it contrasts pollination and fertilisation and does not say which parts can go; A, C and D each remove a part the fruit needs (the "every part is needed" and fruit-from-ovule mix-ups).

## Realism notes
- Fertilisation is drawn as a diagram, never a photo: the male reproductive cell reaches the ovule and fuses with the egg cell inside it; **no pollen tube** (syllabus exclude).
- After fertilisation: petals, stamens, stigma and style wither and drop; the ovary swells into the fruit; each ovule becomes one seed, so the number of seeds matches the number of ovules shown before.
- Chilli: a superior ovary, so the whole red pod is the fruit and the seeds sit on the pale centre; tomato: seeds in jelly in chambers. Avoid apple, strawberry and rambutan as "ovary becomes fruit" examples (their eaten part does not come only from the ovary).
- A flower with no fruit: the ovary stays small, yellows and drops; nothing grim.
- Labels on plain lines; growth and flow arrows only for change over time (allowed motion/direction arrows); no numbered stages; ✗ only on the false statement in `tip`.
- The quiz flower shows the parts clearly separate: petals, anthers on filaments, stigma, style, ovary with ovules.
