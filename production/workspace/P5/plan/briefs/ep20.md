## Big idea
Plants, fish and humans all take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide, but through different parts: tiny openings on leaves, gills in fish, lungs in humans.

## Misconceptions to bust
- Plants do not take in oxygen → plants take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide all the time, through tiny openings (content map Ch 4; definitions 4.13 note; misconception M-23 named but its P6 fix is not quoted). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- All animals have lungs / fish breathe air with lungs → fish use gills to take in oxygen dissolved in water (lab 4.3 Challenge; definitions 4.14). The quiz turns it round: **wrong quiz option A** is "gills, like a fish" for a whale (every animal in the sea has gills: false).
- Gills work in both water and air → gills take in oxygen dissolved in water (lab 4.3 Part (a) prediction reason).
- Oxygen stays at the gills or in the lungs → it travels further in to the parts that need it (lab 4.3 Part (b)).
- Lungs for all three → each living thing uses its own part (lab 4.3 Part (a)). **Wrong quiz option C** ("tiny openings on its skin") borrows the plant's part.
