# EP 05 — A flower is a reproduction machine: the six parts

## Big idea
A flower has male parts (anther and filament: the stamen, where pollen grains with the male reproductive cells are made) and female parts (stigma, style, ovary with ovules holding the egg cells: the pistil).

## Misconceptions to bust
- The petals make pollen → pollen grains are made in the anther; petals attract animals (lab 1.5A mix-up "The petals are the parts that make pollen"; rows 1.38, 1.41). → the `tip` beat.
- The stigma (or the ovary) is a male part → the stigma, style and ovary are the female parts; the anther and filament are the male parts (rows 1.35–1.36; lab 1.5A Challenge mix-up "The pistil, made of the stigma, style and ovary" offered as the male part).
- Pollen grains are kept in the ovary → the ovary contains ovules, which contain egg cells (row 1.40). → wrong quiz option A.
- The anther catches pollen → the stigma receives pollen grains (lab 1.5A Challenge mix-up "The anther"; row 1.41).
- Write "egg" → write "egg cell" (Mix-up buster #46).

## Exam wording box
1. The anther produces pollen grains, which contain male reproductive cells.
2. The ovary contains ovules, which contain egg cells.
3. Anther, stigma, ovary and filament are reproductive parts of a flower.

## Pause and predict
- **Q:** An insect eats away the ovary at the base of a flower. Which parts are lost with it?
- **A** the pollen grains · **B** the ovules and their egg cells · **C** the anthers · **D** the filaments
- **Answer: B.** The ovary contains ovules, and the ovules contain the egg cells; pollen grains, anthers and filaments are male parts and stay.
- Checks: a new situation (a damaged ovary was never shown); the question does not contain "contains ovules" or "egg cells"; the `tip` before it is about petals and pollen, not about the ovary; A is the "pollen is kept in the ovary" mix-up.

## Realism notes
- Style guide §10: the anther sits **on top of** the filament (together the stamen); the stigma sits on top of the style above the ovary (together the pistil); ovules sit **inside** the ovary; pollen grains are tiny dots.
- Use a lily (or another flower with separate stamens) for drawings and the photo: six stamens around one pistil; a three-part stigma; the ovary at the base. ⚠ A hibiscus has its filaments joined in a column around the style and an orchid has its parts fused: avoid both for the labelling.
- The lengthwise half-cut shows the ovary opened with the ovules visible as small round beads in rows.
- Labels on plain lines with no arrowheads; male labels blue, female labels black (AB 1.5A step 2); no numbered markers.
- Tweezers, magnifying glass and white tile are real lab items; the blade is shown only in the teacher's hand, never the pupil's.
- In the quiz drawing the beetle is small and the damage is a neat hollow; nothing gory.
