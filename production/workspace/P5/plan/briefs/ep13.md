## 2. Big idea
Water vapour in the surrounding air loses heat to a cooler surface and condenses into water droplets; the droplets come from the air, not from inside the can; a warm surface collects few or no droplets. (The last point is taught through the Pause and predict, not in the body, so the quiz stays a real prediction.)

## 7. Misconceptions to bust
- "The droplets come from the drink inside / leak through the glass" → they come from water vapour in the surrounding air; the droplets are clear even when the water inside is coloured (AB 2.3; bench 2.3 prediction reason "The droplets are water that leaks from inside the beaker"; Mix-up buster #8). **This is the `tip` beat** (and the ✗ line in `where`).
- "The cold can gives out cold" → the water vapour loses heat to the cooler can (Mix-up buster #10; style guide §1 "never cold gets in"). **`tip` beat.**
- "Warmth helps water vapour form droplets" → a warm surface is not cool enough, so little or no droplets form (bench 2.3 prediction reason; Mix-up buster #12; definitions 2.29). **Wrong quiz option A.**
- "The droplets are coloured like the drink" → condensed droplets are clear water (AB 2.3). **Wrong quiz option C.**
- "Water vapour turns into ice on the mirror" → condensation is gas to liquid (bench 2.3 do-not-accept list). **Wrong quiz option D.**
- "The white mist is water vapour" → water vapour cannot be seen; mist is tiny water droplets (bench 2.3 "The trap"; taught in EP 12's tip, repeated here in `mist`).

## 9. Pause and predict
- **Question:** You breathe on a cold bathroom mirror, and it fogs up. Then you breathe on a mirror that a hairdryer has made warm. What do you see on the warm mirror?
- **A** More droplets than on the cold one. · **B** Little or no droplets. · **C** Coloured droplets. · **D** A thin layer of ice.
- **Answer: B.**
- **Reason:** The warm mirror is not cool enough for the warm water vapour from your breath to lose heat and condense, so little or no water droplets form (definitions 2.29).
- Checks: a new situation (no warm surface appears in the body; `hot` has a cooler cover); the question does not contain the definition words; the `tip` beat before `quiz1` is about the source of the water. ## 13. Realism notes
- Droplets form on the OUTSIDE of the cold beaker and the can, and are clear water, never coloured, even over blue or red water (AB 2.3).
- In the hot set-up the droplets form on the UNDERSIDE of the cover; the hot water has no bubbles (it is hot, not boiling); the coloured water's surface is level.
- Water vapour is never drawn as a visible cloud: only faint dashed outlines with a plain-line label "cannot be seen".
- Heat arrows go from the water vapour INTO the cooler surface (wavy motion arrows); there are no "cold" arrows anywhere.
- Fogged glasses: the lenses mist evenly at the edges first; the person's face is not shown (glasses on a table or held by a hand).
- Mist: low, white, over water or grass at sunrise; not dark smoke.
- No brand names or logos on the can (turn the label away or blur it).
