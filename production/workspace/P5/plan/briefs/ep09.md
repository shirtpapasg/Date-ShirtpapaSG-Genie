## Misconceptions to bust
- Seeds need light to germinate → light is not needed for the germination of seeds (lab 1.5C Challenge tagged "The trap": "Light is the first thing a seed needs, before water or warmth"; Mix-up buster #47; row 1.70). → the `tip` beat.
- Cold keeps seeds fresh, so they germinate faster → in a refrigerator the temperature is too low; the seed does not have enough warmth to germinate (lab 1.5C prediction reason; row 1.71).
- Seeds need soil to germinate → green beans germinate on wet cotton wool (AB 1.5C set-up; lab 1.5C mix-up "Light, warmth and soil").
- Changing two things at once is still a fair test → change only one variable and keep the others the same (AB 1.5C step 2; CfU Q4 idea). → wrong quiz options A and D.
- Any pair that changes one thing tests what you want → the pair must change the variable you are testing (Q and R change the place, so they test warmth, not water). → wrong quiz option C.

## Pause and predict
- **Q:** A girl wants to find out if seeds need water to germinate. Set-up P: 10 ml of water, on a classroom table. Q: no water, in a refrigerator. R: no water, on a classroom table. S: 10 ml of water, in a refrigerator. Which two set-ups make a fair test?
- **A** P and Q · **B** P and R · **C** Q and R · **D** R and S
- **Answer: B.** P and R differ only in water; the place (warmth) is kept the same. Q and R change only the place, so they test warmth, not water; A and D change two variables.
- Checks: a new situation (a water test with four set-ups, new letters and a new pairing; CfU Q4 asked about warmth with A–D set-ups, and its wording is not reused); the question does not contain "change only one variable" or "kept the same"; the `tip` before it is about light, not about fair-test pairs; A and D are the "two changes at once" mix-up, C is the "wrong variable" mix-up. Only one pair changes exactly the water and keeps the place, so the answer is unique.
