## Big idea
A simple circuit has a battery, wires, a bulb and often a switch; the bulb lights only when the circuit is closed (an unbroken path from one battery terminal through the bulb to the other terminal); a switch opens or closes the gap.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "The bulb lights as long as it is joined to the battery." → It lights only in a closed circuit: no gaps, so electricity flows through the bulb (lab 5.2 prediction reason "Being joined to the battery is enough on its own"; Mix-up buster #22 "say the circuit is closed AND what that does"). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- "Electricity can jump across a small gap by itself." → A gap stops the flow; the circuit is open (lab 5.1 trap). **Wrong quiz option C.**
- "One clipped wire is enough." → A bulb in its holder lights only when its two contacts are joined to both battery terminals, making one unbroken loop (content_map Ch 5 common mistakes "one wire is enough"; 5.9).
- "Both clips on the bulb is enough, wherever they touch." → Both clips on the same contact miss the filament (lab 5.1 Part (a) set-up; 5.8).
- "A fused bulb is still a closed circuit." → A broken filament is a gap, so the circuit is open (content_map Ch 5 common mistakes; 5.8 note). Used as the quiz.
- "The battery being new decides everything." → A new battery cannot light a bulb across a gap (quiz option D; content_map Ch 5).
- Kept out of the video: "the bulb uses up electricity" (the lab's counter-line 5.10 is flagged ⚠⚠ loose).

## Pause and predict
**Q:** A toy's switch is closed and its battery is new, but the filament inside its bulb has broken. What happens?
- A. The bulb lights, because the switch is closed.
- B. The bulb does not light, because there is now a gap.
- C. The bulb lights dimly, because electricity jumps across. (lab 5.1 trap)
- D. The bulb lights, because the battery is new.

**Answer: B.** Reason: a broken filament is a gap inside the bulb, so the circuit is open and electricity cannot flow, even with the switch closed.
Rule check: a new situation (a broken filament has not been shown; the `bulb` beat shows a whole filament); the question does not use the words "open", "gap" or "closed circuit" in its stem; the `tip` beat before `quiz1` talks about "joined is not enough" and never mentions a broken filament.

## Realism notes
- Battery: a D-size battery with the raised bump at the positive (+) end and a flat negative (−) end; "+" and "−" printed on it. Never call or label it a "cell".
- Wires: crocodile-clip wires with a metal clip at each end and a coloured plastic covering; a clip either grips a terminal or a contact, or visibly sits apart from it (a clear gap).
- Bulb: a small torch bulb in a holder; two contacts drawn correctly: the side (screw) contact and the tip contact at the bottom, with a thin filament between two support wires inside the glass; in the loop set-up, one wire goes to each contact.
- Lit bulb has yellow rays; a dark bulb is grey with no rays. A brighter bulb would have bigger rays (not needed here: one bulb, one battery throughout).
- Flow arrows are motion arrows along the wires only (allowed); every label line is plain, with no arrowheads; no numbered markers (the four set-ups are on cards, not numbered).
- A switch opens a visible gap: the metal bar lifts clear of its contact; closed, it lies flat on both contacts.
- The broken filament in the quiz shows two loose ends with a clear gap; the glass is whole (the bulb is not smashed).
- Never draw a wall socket being touched or a bare wire joining the battery's two ends.
