# EP 18 — What goes up and what comes down: two tube networks

**Topic tag:** Two Tube Networks · **Icon:** 🌳 · **Chapter:** Ch 3 Plant Transport System, AB pp. 87–88 and 93–100 · Activities 3.3 What goes up and what comes down?, 3.5 (title only in our sources) + Check for Your Understanding (p. 97) · **Mind Lab chapter:** Plant System · **School Lab:** 3.3 Two Tube Networks · Part (c): Trace the routes (in review: sign off before release) · **NGSS:** 5-LS1-1 · **Narrator:** Heart voice 3 · **Target:** 3:45–4:30, 612 authored words (hook 31, tail 116) · **Output:** `TwoTubeNetworks` → `R/P5_EP18_TwoTubeNetworks.mp4`

## Big idea
Plants have two kinds of tubes: water-carrying tubes carry water and mineral salts from the roots up, food-carrying tubes carry food made in the leaves to the rest of the plant; cutting a ring of bark removes food-carrying tubes, so food cannot pass that point (the technical tube names are not required).

## Misconceptions to bust
- Roots take in food from the soil → roots absorb water and dissolved mineral salts; the plant makes its food in the leaves (misconception M-20; Mix-up buster #38). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- Cutting the bark stops water reaching the leaves → a thin ring removes the food-carrying tubes, not the water-carrying tubes (misconception M-22; lab 3.5 Challenge). **Wrong quiz option A.**
- The dye travels in the food-carrying tubes → coloured water travels in the water-carrying tubes (content map Ch 3; lab 3.2 check question). **Wrong quiz option D.**
- Food and water travel together in the same tube → two kinds of tubes, each with its own job (lab 3.3 Challenge).
- Food goes up from the roots → food starts in the leaves and moves up and down (content map Ch 3; lab 3.3 Part (c)).
- A bigger cut means bigger fruit → a deep cut removes the water-carrying tubes and the leaves above it wilt (lab 3.5 Part (c)).
- Plants digest food → plants make food; say "food", never "digested food", for plants (content map Ch 3).

## Pause and predict
Q: A leafy stem stands in red water. An adult removes a thin ring of its outer layer, halfway up. A day later, what do the leaves above the ring show? A, no red, because the ring cut every tube. B, red, just as without the ring. C, red only in the leaves below the ring. D, no red, because red water travels in the food-carrying tubes. **Answer: B.** Reason: a thin ring removes only the outer layer with the food-carrying tubes; the water-carrying tubes are further in, so the red water still climbs to every leaf.
