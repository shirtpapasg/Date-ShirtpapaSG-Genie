## Big idea
Sexual reproduction in humans needs a sperm cell (from the testes) to fuse with an egg cell (from the ovaries); fertilisation happens in the fallopian tube and the baby develops in the uterus (womb).

## Misconceptions to bust
- A baby develops in the mother's stomach → the baby develops in the uterus (rows 1.16, 1.25; AB 1.4 cast idea; lab 1.4 mix-up "In the stomach"). → the `tip` beat and wrong quiz option C.
- An egg is fertilised by many sperms → only one sperm cell fuses with one egg cell (rows 1.16, 1.24 ⚠; lab 1.4 mix-up "Many sperm are needed to fertilise one egg"). → wrong quiz option C.
- The ovaries make sperm and the testes make eggs → the testes produce sperm cells; the ovaries produce egg cells (lab 1.4 mix-up; rows 1.19–1.20).
- Blocked fallopian tubes stop the ovaries working → the ovaries still release egg cells (row 1.28). → wrong quiz option A.
- Fertilisation happens in the uterus or the ovary → fertilisation occurs in the fallopian tube (row 1.23). → wrong quiz option D.

## Pause and predict
- **Q:** In a woman, both fallopian tubes are blocked. What is most likely to happen?
- **A** her ovaries stop producing egg cells · **B** the sperm cell may not meet the egg cell, so fertilisation may not occur · **C** many sperm cells fuse with the egg cell in the stomach · **D** fertilisation happens in the ovary instead
- **Answer: B.** "The sperm cell may not meet the egg cell, so fertilisation may not occur." The ovaries still release egg cells, and fertilisation occurs in the fallopian tube, one sperm cell with one egg cell.
