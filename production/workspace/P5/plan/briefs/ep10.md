## Big idea
Sexual reproduction in flowering plants runs pollination → fertilisation → seed dispersal → germination; humans and flowering plants both need fertilisation (fusion of reproductive cells) but differ in where it happens and how the young develop; a new fast-spreading plant can crowd out the plants already there.

## Misconceptions to bust
- Humans go through pollination before fertilisation → only flowering plants need pollination; both have fertilisation (lab 1.6 prediction reason; row 1.74). → the `tip` beat.
- Seeds are scattered before they have even formed → seed dispersal comes after fertilisation (lab 1.5D prediction reason "Seeds are scattered before they have even formed"; lab 1.5D typed question). → the `order` beat and wrong quiz option B.
- Pollination and fertilisation are the same → pollination moves pollen grains; fertilisation fuses the cells (M-30). → wrong quiz option C.
- Egg cells are made in the ovary of a flower → in the ovule of a flower; in the ovary of a human (CfU Q1; row 1.40). → the `compare` beat.
- Plants always help each other to grow when they stand close together → a fast-spreading plant competes with the others for light, water and space, so their numbers fall (lab 1.7 Challenge tagged "The trap"; row 1.75). → the `newplant` beat.

## Pause and predict
- **Q:** A gardener covers an insect-pollinated plant with a fine net, so no insects can reach its flowers. Which process is the first that cannot happen?
- **A** germination · **B** seed dispersal · **C** fertilisation · **D** pollination
- **Answer: D.** No insects can carry pollen grains from the anther to the stigma, so pollination cannot happen; without it there is no fertilisation, no seeds to disperse and nothing to germinate.
- Checks: a new situation (a netted plant is never shown); the question does not contain "transfer", "anther" or "stigma"; the `tip` before it is about humans and pollination, not about a plant that cannot be pollinated; C is the pollination-vs-fertilisation mix-up, B the order mix-up.
