## Big idea
Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma; insect-pollinated flowers are large, bright and often scented, wind-pollinated flowers are small and dull with feathery stigmas and dangling anthers.

## Misconceptions to bust
- Pollination is when pollen touches the stigma → pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma (Mix-up buster #45; row 1.42; `app_p5.md` §4a note on the poster's "Pollen lands on the stigma"). → the `tip` beat.
- Pollination and fertilisation are the same → pollination moves pollen; fertilisation (EP 07) is the fusing of reproductive cells (BRIEF-P5 §4; misconception M-30).
- Wind needs bright petals to carry pollen well → wind-pollinated flowers are small and dull; bright petals attract animals (lab 1.5A prediction reason).
- A flower that is not brightly coloured must be wind-pollinated → read all the clues: size, scent, nectar and sticky pollen point to animals (row 1.45 "usually"). → wrong quiz option A.
- Flowers can only be pollinated in daylight → animals such as bats and moths visit at night ⚠ check. → wrong quiz options C and D.

## Realism notes
- Insect-pollinated flower: large, bright petals, nectar at the base, anthers and stigma inside the flower; the bee is hairy with pollen dots stuck to it, six legs, two pairs of wings.
- Wind-pollinated flower (grass): small and dull, anthers dangling outside on long thin filaments, feathery stigmas, pollen as a puff of tiny light dots (style guide §10).
- Ixora: clusters of small red tubular flowers with four petals each, butterflies with long mouthparts. Corn: tassels at the top of the plant, pollen as a fine yellow dust.
- Pollen moves from an anther to a stigma along curved **motion** arrows (allowed); label lines plain with no arrowheads; no numbered markers.
- The night flower is large and pale cream (not bright), with scent swirls; the bat in `quiz2` is a small fruit bat, friendly-looking.
- Never draw a pollen tube (syllabus exclude).
