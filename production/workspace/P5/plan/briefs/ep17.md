## Big idea
A plant without water droops; water and mineral salts travel from the roots up the stem to the leaves inside water-carrying tubes; coloured water shows the path; a fair test can change one variable (say, wind) and measure how much water the plant takes in.

## Misconceptions to bust
- Coloured water soaks through the whole stalk like a sponge → it travels only in the water-carrying tubes, seen as dots and lines (lab 3.2 Challenge; definitions 3.11). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- A cut stalk or flower cannot take in water → a cut stalk still draws water up its tubes (lab 3.2 Part (a) prediction reason). **Wrong quiz option B.**
- Liquids inside a plant mix, like water in a cup → each tube carries its own water; the colours do not mix (lab 3.2 Part (b); definitions 3.11). **Wrong quiz option D.**
- Once a plant droops, it can never recover → watered, it stands upright again within hours (lab 3.1 Part (b)).
- Leaves drink water from the air → water enters at the roots and travels up the stem (lab 3.1 Part (c); app_p5 §5).
- Plants only droop in the dark → a plant droops when it does not get enough water (lab 3.1 Part (a)).
- Changing two things at once is still fair → a fair test changes only one variable (lab 3.4 Part (c)).

## Pause and predict
Q: A white carnation's stem is split in two. One half stands in blue water, the other in plain water. How will the petals look next day? A, all blue. B, no change: a cut flower cannot take in water. C, part blue, part white. D, pale blue all over, because the waters mix. **Answer: C.** Reason: the blue water climbs only in the water-carrying tubes on its own side, so only the petals those tubes reach turn blue; the plain water keeps the other petals white. ## Realism notes
- Celery cross-section: a U-shaped (curved) stalk outline with the coloured dots in a curved row near the outer, ribbed side, not scattered evenly and not a solid colour; dots are small, separate, evenly spaced.
- Length-cut: coloured lines run continuously from the cut end up into the leaf stalks; the colour is darkest near the bottom.
- Dyed water is clearly blue (or red) and level in the glass; the stalk's cut end sits under the water surface.
- The split stalk: both halves stay joined above the split; each half reaches into its own cup; top cut shows red dots on one side, blue on the other, no purple.
- Wilting plant: the stem bends and leaves sag (not fall off); the "a few hours later" clock card sits between watering and straightening, so watering is never shown as instant.
- Think set-up: two identical plants (same size, same number of leaves) with roots in two identical measuring cylinders, water levels marked at the same height; the fan is beside one plant only; same lamp over both.
- Arrows only for water moving up the tubes; plain label lines with no arrowheads; no numbered markers; ✗ only at the start of the false sponge line.
