# EP 12 — Boiling is not evaporation: bubbles all the way through

## 2. Big idea
Water gains heat and boils at 100 °C (its boiling point), changing to water vapour throughout the liquid; evaporation also changes liquid water to water vapour but at the surface, at any temperature, when water gains heat from the surroundings.

## 7. Misconceptions to bust
- "The white mist above a kettle is water vapour" → water vapour cannot be seen; the mist is tiny water droplets formed when hot water vapour loses heat to the cooler air and condenses (bench 2.3 "The trap"; Mix-up buster #11; definitions 2.33). **This is the `tip` beat.**
- "Water can only evaporate once it reaches its boiling point" → evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature (bench 2.4 "The trap"; definitions 2.24). Busted in `evap` + `compare`.
- "A stronger flame makes water boil hotter than 100 °C" → a stronger flame only gets the water to 100 °C sooner (bench 2.2 "The trap"). Busted in `think`.
- "More heat always means a higher number / the temperature keeps rising while it boils" → it stays at 100 °C while boiling (bench 2.2 prediction reason). **Wrong quiz option C.**
- "Once it stops being heated it stays at 100 °C" / "it stays flat to the end" → with the flame gone it loses heat and cools. **Wrong quiz option B.**
- "Boiling and evaporation are the same thing" → two differences: where (surface vs throughout) and temperature (any vs the boiling point).

## 8. Exam wording box
1. Boiling is the change of liquid water to water vapour throughout the liquid when heated to its boiling point.
2. Evaporation is the change of liquid water to water vapour at the surface when heat is gained.
3. Evaporation occurs at the surface of the liquid and can happen at any temperature. Boiling occurs throughout the liquid at its boiling point.

## 9. Pause and predict
- **Question:** Water at 30 °C is heated. At 10 minutes it starts to boil. It boils for 10 more minutes, then the flame is taken away and the water cools. Which graph is right? (CfU Q3's situation, not shown earlier in the video.)
- **A** Up, flat, then down. · **B** Up, then flat to the very end. · **C** Up and up, past 100 °C. · **D** Up to 100 °C, then straight down.

- **Reason:** The water gains heat until it reaches 100 °C and stays at 100 °C while it boils; once the flame is removed, it loses heat to the cooler air and its temperature falls towards room temperature.
