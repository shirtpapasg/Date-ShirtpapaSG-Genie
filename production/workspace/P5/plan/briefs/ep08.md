## Big idea
Seeds are dispersed away from the parent plant by wind, water, animals and splitting; dispersal reduces competition for light, water and space and prevents overcrowding.

## Misconceptions to bust
- The plant is dispersed → it is the seed (or the fruit) that is dispersed (Mix-up buster #51). → the `tip` beat.
- A pod flings its seed hard, so it goes furthest → splitting sends seeds only a short way from the parent plant (lab 1.5B prediction reason; lab Challenge "They are flung out and land a short way from the parent plant"). → wrong quiz option A.
- Hooks help a seed float further on water → hooks attach to animal fur (lab 1.5B prediction reason).
- A coconut floats because it is "waterproof" → the fibrous husk traps air (Mix-up buster #49; bench big idea says "waterproof").
- Seeds are dispersed so the parent plant has more space → dispersal reduces competition between parent and young plants and prevents overcrowding (rows 1.57–1.58).
- Wind always spreads seeds in every direction → seeds travel in the direction of the wind (AB 1.5B step 4 diagram, "Direction of wind"). → wrong quiz option C.

## Realism notes
- Style guide §10: wind seeds have wings or hairs and are light; water fruits have fibrous husks or air spaces and float; animal-dispersed fruits are fleshy or hooked; splitting pods are **dry**.
- Angsana pod: a flat, round, papery wing around a central seed case. Maple: paired winged seeds that spin. Coconut: a thick fibrous husk inside a smooth outer skin, floating on a level blue water surface. Love grass: tiny pointed seeds with barbs that catch on socks. Rubber-tree fruit: a dry three-part capsule that splits and flings out mottled seeds a short way.
- Seeds land a short way from a splitting pod, further in the wind's direction, along the water's flow for water, and anywhere an animal goes.
