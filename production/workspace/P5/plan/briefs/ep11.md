## 2. Big idea
Water exists as solid (ice), liquid (water) and gas (water vapour); ice gains heat and melts at 0 °C (its melting point), water loses heat and freezes at 0 °C, and during melting or freezing the temperature stays the same because the heat is used for the change of state.

## 7. Misconceptions to bust
- "The ice loses its cold / the cold comes out of the ice" → the ice gains heat from the warmer surroundings and melts (Mix-up buster #9; bench 2.1 Challenge mix-up "It loses heat to the warmer room"). **This is the `tip` beat.**
- "Gaining heat (or losing heat) always makes the reading keep changing" → during melting or freezing the temperature stays the same, because the heat is used for the change of state (Trigger bank p. 5; bench 2.1 prediction reason "Gaining heat always makes the number keep climbing"). **Wrong quiz option B** (the freezing mirror of it).
- "Once it reaches zero it stays at zero for ever" → once all the water has frozen, the ice keeps losing heat and its temperature falls (bench 2.1 Challenge "It stays at 0 °C for ever"). **Wrong quiz option C.**
- "Ice has no temperature until it turns into water" → ice has a temperature; melting ice reads 0 °C (bench 2.1 prediction reason).
- "A bigger heap of ice melts at a higher temperature" → it takes longer but still melts at 0 °C (bench 2.1 Part (b) takeaway).

## 8. Exam wording box
1. Melting is the change of state from solid to liquid when heat is gained.
2. Freezing is the change of state from liquid to solid when heat is lost.
3. Heat gained or lost is used for the change of state, not to change the temperature.

## 9. Pause and predict
- **Question:** Warm water goes into a freezer much colder than zero. Its temperature is read every minute, until well after it is solid ice. Which graph is right?
- **A** It falls, stays flat at 0 °C, then falls again. · **B** It falls the whole time without stopping. · **C** It falls to 0 °C and stays there for ever. · **D** It rises while the ice forms.
- **Answer: A.**
- **Reason:** The water loses heat to the colder freezer and cools to 0 °C; while it freezes, the heat lost is used for the change of state, so the temperature stays at 0 °C; once it is all ice, the ice keeps losing heat and its temperature falls.
- Checks: a new situation (the body only graphs melting; `freeze` deliberately shows no graph and never says the temperature stays the same while water freezes); the question does not contain the definition words; the `tip` beat before `quiz1` is about "gains heat", not the freezing graph. ⚠ check: graphs A and C go below 0 °C after freezing; confirm with the teacher that a reading below zero is fine for P5 (Trigger bank p. 5 "Below melting point: solid" supports it).
