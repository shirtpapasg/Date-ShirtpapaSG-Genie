## 2. The chain of questions, the spine and the split

**The chain (each answer opens the next question; SERIES_FORMAT 1c):**
1. *EP21's dare:* Pip strokes an iron nail with Basil's magnet, again and again, always the same way, then holds it near some steel paper clips. What happens? → (Momo: nothing, the nail is just a nail again once the magnet goes; Pip: the nail attracts them, because iron nails always attract steel clips; Basil: the nail attracts them, like the hardware shop uncle's screwdriver.)
2. *Be the iron nail:* → **you are stroked from your head to your tip, again and again, always the same way; you are carried to the clips, and they jump up and cling to your tip.** Answer: 2. Basil: that is the stroke method.
3. *But was it a magnet already? Iron nails always attract steel clips!* (Pip) → **A new nail from the same bag attracts no clips. A magnet pulls it, because iron is a magnetic material, but it is not a magnet. Pip's stroked nail attracts the clips with the magnet shut away: it has been magnetised.** (Right card, wrong reason: no point.)
4. *Basil's one-stroke hook: "Look, the clips jump up!"* (HOLD ON!) → **The clips are on his magnet, not his nail. Put the magnet away first, then test the nail near some steel clips.**
5. *Can we stroke anything into a magnet, if we stroke it enough? Even a wooden chopstick?* (Momo) → **Copper wire, aluminium foil and a wooden chopstick, stroked the same way: no clips. Only the steel screwdriver. Only a magnetic object can be magnetised.**
6. *Basil's fast way: back and forth, with both ends. Will his hook be the strongest?* (Part 1's cliffhanger) → Part 2, first as a game: **the same number of strokes: Basil's nail hardly pulls a clip; Pip's one-way nail pulls a clip up, and a friend with it** (be a steel paper clip). **Going back, or switching ends, undoes the stroke before.**
7. *So how do I get back to the head, without rubbing the wrong way?* (Basil) → **One pole on the head, slide to the tip, lift up and away in a big curve, back to the head: the same direction every time.**
8. *Pip's poster: "Halfway, turn the magnet round and use the blue end too, for more strength"?* (Showdown) → **No: switching ends undoes the strokes. Stroke with the same pole.** *Then how do we make a hook strong enough for the big fish?* (Momo) → **Three nails, the same, ten, twenty and thirty strokes: the more strokes, the more clips. A stronger magnet. The thirty-stroke hook lifts the big fish.**
9. *Momo coils a wire round a new iron nail and joins it to a battery; it attracts clips. She takes the battery away. Do the clips stay, or drop off?* → EP23's dare (the electrical method).

**The spine:** a build-and-reveal. The clip-fishing game for Friday's class party needs three hooks, and there is only one magnet. Saturday morning at the hardware shop (Part 1): the nails are bought, the first hook is magnetised and tested, the list of what can be magnetised is filled. Monday after school in the maker space (Part 2): the right way to stroke, the same pole, and more strokes for a stronger hook; the big gold fish is lifted out of the pond (the reveal), *READY FOR FRIDAY!*.

**Why the split is here:** the plan's three goals and p. 114's order. Part 1 is "what magnetised means" (7.1) and what the stroke method can do (7.2's first sentence), with the test (step 4) and what can be magnetised (the brief's "only" tip), which answers EP21's dare fully. Part 2 is how to do it well: the steps (7.2's steps 1 to 3) and more strokes (7.3). Part 1 ends on Basil's wrong way, the exact question that opens Part 2's Round 1.

- **Facts to check (no web check in this session; how sure I am of each, as worded):**
  1. **Only magnetic materials can be magnetised by stroking** (iron and steel in P3; also nickel and cobalt, not named). Copper wire, aluminium foil and wood stay non-magnetic however many times they are stroked. **Very sure.** EP19 taught copper and aluminium as non-magnetic metals.
  2. **A stroked nail becomes a weak magnet that picks up a few steel clips:** very sure in general (it is the book's own activity and School Lab 7.1A's). How many clips depends on the nail, the magnet and the clips, so **no number is ever said or written**; the bunches are drawn. Most "iron nails" sold today are low-carbon (mild) steel; the book calls them iron nails, and so do we.
  3. **How long it stays a magnet:** a stroked nail keeps some magnetism after the magnet goes away (very sure: that is why the test works with the magnet put away), but it is weak and does not last for ever: it fades over hours to days, faster if the nail is dropped, knocked or heated (very sure as a direction; how fast varies a lot: soft iron loses most of it quickly, hardened steel such as a screwdriver or a sewing needle keeps it longer). **The script never says how long, never says "for ever" or "permanent",** and the fishing game is used within the scene. Momo's cut line `m806m` ("Pip's nail stayed a magnet") is true in the scene. For the teacher sheet only: a magnet can be "demagnetised" (made weaker) by dropping or heating; the word is NOT used in the video.
  4. **A new nail attracts no clips:** usually true (about 90%): a nail can carry a little magnetism from the factory or from lying near a magnet. The script says "a new nail from the same bag. Nobody has stroked it." and the realism note keeps the bag away from the magnet; a pupil who finds a new nail that lifts a clip has found a nail that was magnetised by accident, which is the same idea.
  5. **A magnet attracts the new nail, but the new nail is not a magnet:** very sure (magnetic material vs magnet: EP19, EP20 6.13; the monograph's alternative conception «all magnetic materials are magnets»).
  6. **Back and forth, or switching ends, gives a weak magnet or none, weaker than one-way strokes with one pole:** very sure in principle (each stroke the other way, or with the other pole, magnetises the nail the opposite way and undoes the one before; what is left depends mostly on the last strokes). So Basil's nail is drawn and said to "hardly pull" (it may hold no clip or one), and Round 1 asks which nail attracts MORE clips, an answer that holds even if his nail keeps a little magnetism. "Twice the rubbing, twice as strong" is wrong (very sure).
  7. **Lift away between strokes:** sliding the pole back along the nail is a back stroke (very sure); lifting the magnet well clear and returning to the head is the method in the book's picture and in the brief's realism note (very sure).
  8. **More strokes make a stronger magnet, up to a point:** very sure as the book states it (7.3); School Lab 7.1A Part (b): "The more strokes, the stronger the magnet, up to a point. 40 strokes was only a little better than 30." Ten, twenty and thirty strokes are the drawn test (about 85% sure a real nail and bar magnet show a visible difference at these counts; the clip bunches are drawn, not counted). Never said: "it gets stronger for ever".
  9. **The screwdriver:** a steel screwdriver can be magnetised by stroking its shaft with a magnet, and it then holds small steel screws (very sure; people do this, and some screwdrivers are sold already magnetised: so the look-closer's screwdriver is NEW and checked first). Basil's "the hardware shop uncle strokes his screwdriver with a magnet" is a realistic Singapore scene, not a claim about a real person.
  10. **Steel paper clips:** ordinary paper clips are steel wire (very sure); the drawings use plain silver clips. **Stainless steel** (spoons, many kitchen tools) is often NOT magnetic (very sure for the common 18/8 kind), so no stainless steel object is ever used as a "steel" example.
  11. **The bar magnet is not weakened by stroking** a nail a few dozen times (very sure; the brief's quiz option C is wrong).
  12. **The woodpecker fact:** "A woodpecker pecks again and again, many times every second, like super-fast strokes! And its tongue is so long, it wraps round the back of its head!" Woodpeckers drum many times a second (up to about 20 beats a second in some species; very sure for "many times every second" as drumming); in woodpeckers the tongue's long supporting bones (the hyoid) curve round the back of the skull (very sure for woodpeckers in general; about 85% for the Sunda pygmy woodpecker in particular, a small species with a shorter tongue: the line says "a woodpecker", not "this woodpecker"). **Not said:** that the tongue works as a "seat belt" or shock absorber (debated by scientists). The Sunda pygmy woodpecker is common in Singapore's parks, gardens and housing estates (very sure).
  13. **The ant fact:** "When an ant finds food, it leaves a smell path on its way home. More ants follow the same path, again and again, and the smell gets stronger!" Many ants lay a trail pheromone on the way back from food; ants that follow it and also find food add more, so a busy trail gets stronger (very sure for trail-laying ants, including common house ants such as pharaoh ants and ghost ants; about 85% for "the ant" as drawn, a generic small black house ant). **The EP23 dare:** with a NEW iron nail, when the battery is taken away most clips drop at once (very sure); a clip may cling for a moment if the nail keeps a trace of magnetism (likely with mild-steel nails), so EP23 should show and say "they drop off" with a new nail, and its teacher sheet can mention "one may cling for a moment".
  14. **The safety lines:** a coil joined straight to a battery is close to a short circuit: the wire and the battery can get hot within a short time (very sure; the book's warning box 7.5), hence the dare's small print and "held only for a moment" in the realism note.
  15. **"The red North pole":** the red half is the North pole in this series' magnets (EP19 to EP21's convention; many school bar magnets are painted this way, very sure; some are not).
  16. **Which end of the stroked nail becomes which pole** depends on the stroking pole and the direction (the end where the stroke leaves the nail becomes the opposite pole to the stroking pole). It is beyond P3 and the script never names the nail's poles.

## 11. Choices I made

1. **The dare is answered by being the nail:** the child is stroked again and again the same way (the magnet lifting away each time, which Part 2 names), then carried to the clips and feels them jump up. "Again and again, always the same way" from EP21's dare is lived before it is explained.
2. **Pip's "right card, wrong reason" is the heart of "magnetised":** iron is a magnetic material (EP19), so a child easily thinks an iron nail must attract clips. The new nail over still clips, and the magnet pulling that same nail, separate "attracted to a magnet" from "is a magnet" (the monograph's alternative conception), and the game's no-point rule makes the difference matter.
3. **The HOLD ON! is the book's own step 4, done badly:** testing with the magnet still close is a real fair-test mistake and a link to EP24 (fair tests), without the word. It also keeps "is one stroke enough?" open for Part 2.
4. **"What can be magnetised" uses things a hardware shop sells** (copper wire, foil, a chopstick, a screwdriver), checked first so the test is fair; it matches School Lab 7.1A Part (a) (iron nail, copper, wood), and EP19's non-magnetic metals come back as a reason, not a repeat.
5. **Basil's back-and-forth way is the brief's quiz made into a story** (Part 1's cliffhanger, Part 2's Round 1). The question asks which nail attracts MORE clips, so it stays true even if a back-and-forth nail keeps a little magnetism ("Facts to check" 6).
6. **Part 2's journey is a clip, not the nail again:** the child feels the two nails' pull from below (one hardly, one strongly), which answers Round 1 and starts "more clips" for look-closer 2.
7. **The three steps are split where a child meets them** (the test in Part 1's HOLD ON!, one pole and the same direction in Part 2's look-closer 1, the same pole in the Showdown), each with its book sentence; together they are 7.2 word for word.
8. **"Same pole" is a mistake that sounds clever** ("both ends, for more strength"); Basil, who made the same kind of mistake in Round 1, spots it: he learns, so he may explain it.
9. **More strokes is a fair test with three nails** (the same nails, the same magnet, the same pole, the same way; only the number of strokes changes), never a number of clips said aloud. The reveal (the big gold fish lifted on Rod 3) makes "stronger" mean something a 9-year-old wants.
10. **The spine is a build with a reason to make a magnet when you already have one:** three hooks, one magnet. It also gives the nails a job, so "the test with steel clips" is the game itself.
11. **Hidden animals with a link:** a woodpecker at the neighbourhood centre (it pecks again and again, very fast, like strokes; its tongue wraps round its head) and an ant in the maker space (its smell path gets stronger as more ants follow it again and again, as more strokes make a stronger magnet). Neither appears in any earlier episode.
12. **No model of the inside of the nail** (no tiny magnets lining up): it is beyond P3 and the book shows none; the journeys stay at the scale a child can see.
13. **The EP23 dare uses a NEW nail** and asks what happens when the battery is taken away (its brief's hook), so it does not repeat EP22's question and stays true.
14. **Settings:** a hardware shop at an HDB neighbourhood centre and a school maker space, new to the series, after EP21's school field and Pulau Ubin.
