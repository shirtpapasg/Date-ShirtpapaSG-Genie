# Success examples the teacher shared (19 Nov 2026): "success examples for us to not reinvent the wheels"
Learn principles only; never copy characters, names, music, footage or look.

1. Sesame Street, "Elmo Visits the Farm on Sesame Street! | THREE Sesame Street Full Episodes"
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GOSF1qlBO54
   - characters look at the viewer and ask for help; a field trip to a real place full of things to discover; many short segments of
     different kinds; rhythm/song as a memory hook; warmth; long multi-episode compilations next to single episodes and Shorts.
2. Dr. Plants, "World's Biggest vs Smallest Egg - Hatching Experiment"  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BthfXVCRWEQ
3. Dr. Plants, "Simulating Africa for 100 Days"  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M3oT3-4sSOY
   - an extreme, simple challenge premise in the title ("X vs Y", "for 100 days"); a mini-world in a box that changes over time;
     a big on-screen day counter and time-lapse; living things as the characters; stakes and suspense ("will it hatch? will it
     survive?"); twists along the way; the payoff held to the end; cinematic close-ups.
   - For us: a "Day 1 ... Day 30" experiment box fits our lock-in game (predict what will happen by day 30, then watch), and suits
     topics like living vs non-living, life cycles, plant needs, germination, decomposition, food chains.
4. Aquarium Info, "I Turned my Bed into a Fish Tank!"  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HH2gKrl22Fw
   - an absurd, irresistible "I turned X into Y" premise; a build told step by step, with problems and fixes along the way (the
     process is the story); a big satisfying reveal; the maker speaks as "I/we", so the viewer feels they are doing it too.
   - For us: the buddies BUILD something to answer the question ("We turned Momo's bedroom into a living-things zoo", "We built a
     sorting machine"), with steps, setbacks and a reveal.
5. Journey to the Microcosmos, "The Incredible World of Bacterial Communities"  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GvQrH8qas0Q
   - real microscope footage of tiny life, treated as a beautiful, alien world; calm, warm, wondering narration ("we") that asks big
     questions; rich ambient music; shots allowed to breathe; a sense of awe more than a list of facts.
   - For us: our first-person "shrink" journeys should feel like this: a living micro-world with soft light, depth, organic textures and
     motion, and moments of quiet wonder between the game beats (the contrast makes the game beats land harder). Perfect for P3 EP7
     (fungi and bacteria), P5 cells, P6 decomposers.
6. Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell, "AI Just Crossed the Terrifying Line - Now What?"  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ujkD4SxPKOI
   - (the topic isn't ours; the craft is) rich flat-vector art with a strong, consistent palette, glowing elements on dark
     backgrounds, layered depth and parallax, smooth constant motion, simple recurring characters as the audience's stand-ins,
     icon-clear diagrams, a big question in the title, a script that builds with "but here's the thing" turns, and a calm, confident
     narrator; very high production polish, every frame worth pausing on.
   - For us: the level of polish to aim for in the new style guide; clean vector shapes, a signature palette, glow and depth, and
     diagrams so clear a 9-year-old reads them in one second.
7. Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell, "Black Holes Explained – From Birth to Death"  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e-P5IFTqB98
   - a hard idea told as a life story ("from birth to death"); built up step by step from something everyone knows; scale made
     tangible with comparisons ("if the Sun were a grain of sand..."); a few strong visual metaphors reused through the video; each
     step answers the question the last one raised, so curiosity pulls the viewer forward.
   - For us: tell a concept as a journey or life story where it fits, use Singapore-sized comparisons (an HDB block, an MRT train,
     a void deck) for scale, and chain the segments so each answer opens the next question.
8. Cocomelon and Pinkfong (Hogi), e.g. "Learn Colors with Yes papa and Carrot | Hogi Pinkfong"  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LDF2puNJggQ
   - the teacher (20 Nov): "for animation models, I still feel cocomelon is better at their 3D characters. Pinkfong too."
   - soft, rounded 3D characters with big glossy eyes, gentle subsurface-looking skin/fur, pastel saturated colours, soft global
     light and contact shadows, simple bouncy squash-and-stretch motion, very readable silhouettes, sing-along rhythm, bright clean sets.
   - For us: explore real 3D (or a convincing 3D look) for Pip, Basil and Momo, keeping their identity; principles only, never their
     characters, names, music or look.
   - also CoComelon, "Wheels on the Animals Bus | CoComelon Animal Time Nursery Rhymes"  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhZgBFSKqKc
     (cute 3D animal characters, chunky toy-like vehicles and sets, warm soft lighting, constant gentle motion, a repeating song structure).
9. TheOneApp, Explored EP9, "Mosquitoes VS Mosquitoes! How Singapore Fights Dengue" (our own)  https://youtu.be/F_oNWbfqtC0
   - the teacher (20 Nov): "I like this video's way of taking perspective of the particle / energy / material, moving into the
     microworld. This concept is new and I did not see it anywhere." -> rule in SERIES_FORMAT.md section 1 ("BE THE THING").
