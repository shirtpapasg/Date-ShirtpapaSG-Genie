# SCRIPT: TheOneApp, Explained (revamp) · P3 Episode 9 · Snap it or bend it: strong vs flexible · Part 1 and Part 2

**Episode title (P3 plan row 9):** "Snap it or bend it: strong vs flexible" · Ch 3 Diversity of Materials, pp. 53-55 (Physical properties and Strength p. 53, Materials have different strengths p. 54, Flexibility p. 55; Let's Apply Q2 p. 68) · NGSS 2-PS1-1 · School Lab 3.2 "The snap test", Part (a) Hold the load

## 2. The spine and the split

One test stand on a stone table at a void deck, after school. EP8 asked: a rubber band and a wooden ice-cream stick, which one can bend without breaking? Basil wants to test them at once. Heart sets out three tests on a board: **PULL, DROP, BEND**. **Part 1** first shows what a test finds out: a paper strip is pushed and we observe it bend; it holds one weight and we count. Observe or measure: a physical property. Test one is the pull: a paper strip and a fabric strip of the same length and thickness, weights added one by one. The paper tears at three; the fabric holds eight. The child then becomes one thread in a fabric bag as five heavy books drop in and the handles lift, and the book's definition of strength names what was felt. Test two is the drop: Basil wants to throw a glass cup on the floor, the child stops him, and an adult drops a metal cup and a glass cup safely into a box: strong, and fragile. Two boxes are ticked. The cliffhanger is test three and EP8's question. **Part 2** moves to the playground. The dare is Round 1; the bend test is watched in close-up: the rubber band bends in half and into a twist, the ice-cream stick cracks and snaps. Flexibility, flexible, rigid. The child becomes a rubber band at a chicken rice stall. Three tubes show that flexibility comes in amounts. Then the mix-up most pupils make: Momo's notes say paper bends easily, so paper is strong, and the child, who watched paper tear in Part 1, catches it. The chant says the three definitions, and the dare hands over to water: a metal spoon and a giant metal ship.

**Why the split is here:** it is the book's own order and page break (strength on pp. 53-54, flexibility on p. 55), each part has one property and one test the child can do with the eyes, and the Showdown needs both parts: Part 1's torn paper strip is the evidence against Part 2's mix-up.

- **Colours.** The two fixed answer colours: **STRONG** and **FLEXIBLE** green, **FRAGILE** and **RIGID** blue (tags and trays). No red, no buddy colour and no sun yellow is used for an answer. The strips are their own colours: paper white with blue exercise-book lines, fabric plain cream with a visible weave.
- **The test stand** must read clearly as what it is: a low metal stand with a base and an upright rod, standing on the table with a shadow; a clamp near the top holds a strip, which hangs straight down; a small clip on the strip's lower end carries a hanger, and flat round weights slide onto the hanger one by one; a soft mat lies under it. A counter pill stands beside each strip: **WEIGHTS: 0**. The label on the base: **OUR TEST STAND**.
- **The board OUR 3 TESTS:** a cream board on a low stand at the end of the table, three rows with a tick box each: **1 PULL · 2 DROP · 3 BEND**. A green tick is stamped when a test is done.
- **Things do not float.** The stand, the board, trays, the easel and the drop box stand on the table or the ground, or slide low with dust puffs. Weights are slid on by a gloved hand. The cups fall because the test is a drop; they fall straight down into the box.
- **Realism:** the paper strip tears from one edge straight across, a clean tear with a slightly fuzzy edge, no sagging first; the fabric strip shows its weave, and its threads draw tight and straight under load; both strips are the same length and thickness and hang from the same height. The metal cup is the stainless-steel cup of a drinks stall; it bounces once, rings, and may roll, with no dent drawn. The glass cup cracks and breaks into four or five big pieces that stay inside the box (no flying bits, no sharp close-up). The rubber band bends in smooth curves and returns to its loop. The wooden ice-cream stick bows a little, a pale crack opens on the outside of the bend, and it snaps into two with a splintered edge. The three tubes are the same length and thickness, each clamped at one end with the same weight on the free end: the rubber tube droops in a deep curve, the plastic tube in a shallow curve, the metal tube hardly at all. In the first journey the fabric is a plain weave: threads over and under, never knitted loops. Label lines are plain lines with no arrowheads.

## 11. Choices I made

1. **Book order, with EP8's dare held to Part 2.** EP8 asked a flexibility question, but the book teaches strength first (pp. 53-54) and flexibility second (p. 55).
