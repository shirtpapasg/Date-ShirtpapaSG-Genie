## 2. The chain of questions, the spine and the split

**The chain (each answer opens the next question; SERIES_FORMAT 1c):**
1. *EP16's dare:* a caterpillar will become a butterfly. Is it a nymph, like the young grasshopper? → **No: a nymph looks like the adult, and a caterpillar looks nothing like a butterfly.** (Basil thought any young insect that munches leaves is a nymph.)
2. *Then what is it? Is it a worm?* (Momo) → **Count its legs: six pointed legs at the front, so it is an insect. The stumps at the back are prolegs, false legs with tiny hooks.**
3. *So what does the caterpillar do, and what does it become?* → (be a butterfly egg) **It eats a lot and moults many times as it grows; then it hangs still, and its skin splits: a hard case.** The caterpillar is the butterfly's **larva**.
4. *It does not move or eat. Is it just resting?* (Pip) → **No: the pupa does not eat, but inside the pupal case it is changing into the adult.** Out comes a butterfly.
5. *So how many stages?* → **Four: the egg, the larva, the pupa and the adult, round a loop. The young does not look like the adult.**
6. *Tiny things wriggling in a pail of rainwater: what are they, and what comes next?* → (Part 2) **Young mosquitoes, wrigglers: the mosquito's larva. Next comes the pupa.** (Momo: "straight to the adult".)
7. *Does every mosquito spread dengue?* → **No: most do not, but some do (Aedes).** (Basil's homework.)
8. *How does knowing the life cycle keep us safe?* → **The eggs are laid in stagnant water, even a tiny plate of it. Tip it out: no eggs, no wrigglers, no pupae, no new mosquitoes.** (Pip: "too little water".)
9. *And the frog from last week: is its life cycle the same as the mosquito's? Both lay eggs in water, and both young look nothing like the adult...* → EP18's dare (EP18: how to compare: count the stages).

**The spine:** one place, our block's community garden (EP15 and EP16's), from dawn to late morning, and one corridor of the same block. The lime plant carries the whole butterfly cycle (EP16's caterpillar becomes the pupa and then the butterfly: the egg on a leaf in the cold open closes the loop); the old pail by the tap and a plate under a corridor pot carry the mosquito; the void deck holds the buddies' boards and the dare.

**Why the split is here:** the book's own order: the four-stage lead-in, the beetle and the butterfly (pp. 86-87), then the mosquito and dengue (pp. 88-89). Part 1 ends on what a child would find next in the same garden after rain (wrigglers in a pail); Part 2 explains it and turns it into a safety habit.
