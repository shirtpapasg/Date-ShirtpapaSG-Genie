## 2. The chain of questions, the spine and the split

**The chain (each answer opens the next question; SERIES_FORMAT 1c):**
1. *EP18's dare:* a magnet, a steel pin and a copper wire: both are metal. Which will the magnet pull? → (Momo: the pin, she has seen it in Grandma's sewing box; Pip: the wire, it is lighter; Basil: both, "my magnet pulls metal".)
2. *Before we test: what IS a magnet? It looks like a plastic toy.* (Momo) → **The first magnets were rocks from the ground (lodestones); a magnet is usually made of iron or steel, and comes in many shapes.**
3. *So what will it pull?* → (be a steel pin) **The magnet pulls the steel pin across the gap; the copper wire never moves, light as it is.** Answer: 1.
4. *Momo's magnet could not lift Mum's iron wok: so the wok is not attracted?* → **It holds on to the wok: attracted, just too heavy to lift (like a fridge magnet on the fridge door).**
5. *It holds on, so is the magnet sticky?* (Basil) → **No: the pin moves before it touches. The magnet attracts (pulls) the pin. The pin (steel) and the nail (iron) are magnetic materials.**
6. *But copper is a metal, just like iron and steel. Why does the magnet ignore it?* (Basil; Part 1's cliffhanger) → Part 2: **first, two cans that look alike: the steel food tin is pulled, the aluminium drink can rolls on (be an aluminium can, at a recycling plant).**
7. *The drink can and the copper wire are both metal: why are they ignored?* (Momo) → **Not all metals are magnetic: copper and aluminium are metals, but non-magnetic, like wood, glass and plastic.**
8. *Basil says the foil is magnetic because it is shiny and grey, like steel. Can we tell by looking?* → **No: test it. Watch how each object interacts with a magnet, and sort them into two groups: magnetic and non-magnetic (classify).**
9. *Pip's two ring magnets on a pencil: the top one floats. What holds it up, a pull or a push?* → EP20's dare (poles; attract and repel).

**The spine:** one place, our block's void deck, the Saturday after LIFE CYCLE DAY. The MAGNET TEST TABLE carries every test and fills up across both parts (the build); its two halves get the book's names at the end (the reveal). The void deck's recycling corner (the blue bin) carries Part 2's cans and its journey; the playground's swing carries "interact". Two ring magnets on the same table carry the story into EP20.

**Why the split is here:** the book's own order and the plan's three goals: what a magnet is and magnetic materials (pp. 101-102) in Part 1; non-magnetic materials, "metals can be magnetic or non-magnetic" and classifying by interaction (pp. 103-104) in Part 2. Part 1 ends on the exact question the next page answers (copper is a metal: why is it ignored?), which is also the episode's title.

It helps them find their way home, even from far away!" **How sure: high (about 90%)**: homing pigeons' use of the Earth's magnetism is a classic, well-supported finding (small magnets fixed to pigeons upset their homing on cloudy days); exactly how they sense it is still studied, hence "scientists think". It is about homing pigeons; the wild pigeons at a void deck are the same species (the rock pigeon).
  2. **The snail fun fact:** "Deep in the sea lives a snail with iron in its shell. A magnet can attract this snail!" (the scaly-foot snail of the Indian Ocean's deep-sea hot springs: its shell's outer layer and the scales on its foot contain iron minerals, iron sulfides, and they are magnetic). **How sure: fairly high (about 80%)** that "a magnet can attract it" is fair as worded (the iron sulfide greigite is strongly magnetic and the snail is often described as "magnetic"; I have not seen how strong the pull is). **If the teacher wants a safer fact:** "A sea animal called a chiton has teeth coated with iron. A magnet can attract its teeth!" (chiton teeth are capped with magnetite: very sure, about 95%; chitons live on Singapore's rocky shores; but the hidden animal would then not match a land snail so well).
  3. **The recycling plant (Part 2's journey):** recyclables from Singapore's blue bins are taken to sorting plants (materials recovery facilities), and sorting plants commonly use big magnets over the belt to pick out steel cans; aluminium cans are separated by a different machine. **How sure: high (about 85%)** in general; the script says only "a recycling plant" and "a giant magnet", and never names a Singapore plant or its machines.
  4. **"A magnet is usually made of iron or steel" (6.2) and the cut-away:** the book's sentence is used exactly. Many modern magnets are ferrite (a ceramic that contains iron) or neodymium (an alloy with iron); the book's "usually" covers them, and the episode never says "always". The cut-away shows grey metal, which is true of the classic steel or iron-alloy bar magnet. Pip's "A lodestone is a magnet that comes from the ground. Nobody made it!" is true (a natural magnet; very sure).
  5. **Steel and stainless steel:** some stainless steels (many kitchen sinks, spoons, lift doors and some snack boxes, like EP8's) are hardly attracted to a magnet, although they are "steel". So the episode's steel things are ordinary steel only: a sewing pin, a paper clip, a food tin, the iron wok, the fridge door. **Never film or draw a stainless-steel spoon, sink or lift door as a "steel" example.** (The void deck's lift doors are in the background only and are never tested.)
  6. **Mum's iron wok:** iron (cast-iron or carbon-steel) woks are attracted to magnets (very sure); some woks are aluminium or stainless steel, so the line and picture say "iron wok".
  7. **The fridge door:** a fridge magnet holds on to most fridge doors (painted steel); some glass-fronted or stainless-fronted doors do not hold magnets. Heart's line is general ("it holds on to the fridge door"), and the inset shows an ordinary painted door.
  8. **The pin jumps across a gap** before touching: true with a strong bar magnet and a light pin over a short gap (very sure). **The food tin** (a "tin can") is steel with a thin coat of tin: attracted (very sure). **Drink cans** in Singapore are almost all aluminium; Basil says "drink cans like this" (his Dad's words) and the picture is an aluminium can.
  9. **Coins (not spoken):** Singapore's current coins (the third series, from 2013) are plated steel and are attracted to a magnet, copper-coloured ones included (about 85% sure; the brief says to check with a real coin before filming). So no coin is ever used as a non-magnetic example; the optional coin line in section 6 stays cut unless the teacher checks a coin.

## 11. Choices I made

1. **A build-and-reveal on one table** (the MAGNET TEST TABLE at EP18's void deck): classifying by interaction is the chapter's skill, so the episode needs one tray that fills up object by object and is named in the book's words at the end. The void deck carries straight on from EP18's dare (Basil's magnet on the old board's metal frame).
2. **The dare is answered by being the pin:** the child feels the tug grow and slides across the gap, then looks down at the copper wire that never moved; the "light is easier" idea (Pip) is answered by the same picture.
3. **"What is a magnet?" comes before the test** (Momo thinks it is plastic): the lodestone (a magnet nobody made) and a cut-away window into Basil's own magnet make 6.2 something seen, not just read.
4. **"Attracted" is not "lifted"** (Momo's wok HOLD ON!), and **"attract" is not "sticky"** (Basil's look-closer): the brief's "say attract, not stick" becomes two real children's ideas with evidence (it holds on to a heavy wok; the pin moves before it touches), and the fridge door is the child's own example.
5. **Part 1 ends on the title's own question** (copper is a metal: why is it ignored?), asked by Basil, whose "my magnet pulls metal" was the wrong pick.
6. **Part 2 starts from two cans that look alike** (a steel food tin, an aluminium drink can), then rides the aluminium can to a recycling plant, so "not all metals" has a real use children can picture (and a Singapore habit: rinsing cans for the blue bin).
7. **The METALS family board** is the "why" at P3 level: metals are not all the same; iron and steel are magnetic, copper and aluminium are not. I did not give a deeper reason (it is beyond P3), and I did not say "magnetic because it has iron in it" (stainless steel makes that untrue).
8. **"Test, do not guess"** (Basil's homework guesses by colour) leads into the book's classifying sentence (6.4a) and the theme's word INTERACT, so the theme's word is taught where the book uses it.
9. **Hidden animals with a link:** a pigeon (it lives at every void deck, and scientists think it senses the Earth's magnetism) and a snail (after rain at the recycling bin; its deep-sea cousin has iron in its shell).
10. **The dare into EP20 is the brief's own hook** (ring magnets on a stick, the top one floats), set up on the same table by Pip, as a pull-or-push question, without naming poles.
