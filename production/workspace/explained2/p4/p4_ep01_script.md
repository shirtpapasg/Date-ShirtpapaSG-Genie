## 1. The chain of questions
1. Three minutes before the race, Basil is sitting in an ice box, and his bike has no bell, no chain and no brakes. *Which fix gets him
   racing?* → three fix cards; you pick one.
2. *How did it get like this?* Ten minutes earlier: "LIGHTER IS FASTER!" *What does the bell do?* → the pigeons answer: it warns people
   (and pigeons) that you are coming.
3. *What does the chain do?* → without it, the pedals cannot turn the back wheel, and a snail overtakes him.
4. *What do the brakes do?* → without them, nothing can slow the wheels: a runaway bike, stopped only by the ice box.
5. Fix 1, the race: pedal faster. *Can strong legs do the chain's job?* → no. And the old, heavy bike wins. *But WHY?*
6. Look closer at Uncle Lim's bike: every part has its own special job (FUNCTION). *How does the chain turn the wheel?* → be a chain link:
   each part acts on the next (INTERACT).
7. The reversal, fix 2: chain and brakes, skip the bell. *Without a bell, can the bike still move?* → yes, but not as safely: the whole bike
   is likely to be affected.
8. Fix 3, all three parts back: different parts, each with a function, interacting (SYSTEM). *Is anything else a system?* → your body and
   the rain tree.
9. The snail crosses the line last, and Basil learns who the real King of the Void Deck is. (EP2's Chilli appears in the outro; see 12.)

## 2. Science checks (every picture and every sentence)
- **The textbook's own words (Inspiring Science P4, Systems opener, p. 3):** "A system is made up of different parts. Each part has its own
  special function. The different parts of a system interact with one another to perform specific functions. When one part of the system
  stops functioning, it is likely that the function of the whole system will be affected." The words that earn marks (w1203) say the same
  in the shorter form a P4 pupil writes, unchanged from v1. Pip's l804 uses the book's "its own special job" ("special function").
- **The bike's drive, drawn true (v1, unchanged):** the chain runs on the drive side from the front gear (the chainring, fixed to the pedal
  cranks) to the back gear (the sprocket, fixed to the back wheel's hub). Pedalling turns the front gear. Its teeth sit in the gaps of the
  chain and pull the TOP run of the chain forward; the chain turns the back gear, and the back wheel turns. The kid words are "front
  gear" and "back gear". The journey follows this path exactly. **Never** a chain to the front wheel.
- **The bell (stop 1):** "A bell warns people, and pigeons, that you are coming." Basil can still slow and stop at the pigeons: his brakes
  are on at that point, and he uses them. He cannot warn the pigeons, so they flap up around him at the last moment and some settle on him.
- **The chain (stop 2):** "Without a chain, the pedals cannot turn the back wheel." The pedals and the front gear spin and the back wheel
  stays still (true). A bike rolls freely with no chain, but on the flat, with no push, it does not move.
- **The brakes (stop 3 and the runaway):** brakes slow the wheels (rim brakes squeeze the rims). With the brakes off, a riderless bike on
  a ramp keeps rolling and speeds up. Pip's "Then nothing can slow its wheels down. It can't stop!" is the in-the-moment version; the
  sentence that teaches is Heart's: "Brakes help a bike slow down and stop safely." The bike is stopped by something big pushing back (the
  ice box), which is true and is the trio's idea. **The riderless bike is the point of the scene: nobody rides a bike without brakes.**
- **Fix 1 (the race):** "Basil's pedals turn, but nothing joins them to the back wheel. Pedalling faster cannot do the chain's job." (the
  wrong idea: one part can do another part's job).
- **Lighter is faster?** Busted with evidence: "His bike is heavier, but it won, because all its parts work together." The script never says
  weight does not matter at all (a P4 child need not hear that); it says this heavier bike won because its parts work together.
- **Fix 2 (chain and brakes, skip the bell):** it moves and it stops, but it is not as safe. The think question ("Without a bell, can the
  bike still move?") gives the book's "likely": "When one part stops working, the whole bike is likely to be affected." (Keep "likely";
  never "the whole system will stop".) The words-that-earn-marks card then says "the whole system".
- **The body (e1102), kept to the book's Ch 2 (pp. 23-29):** bones and muscles move the legs; the heart beats faster and breathing gets
  faster when we exercise (the book's opener: Ali running); "The human body is made up of many systems working together." No system names
  (those come in EP5). It is said to Basil, a lizard, and to the viewer ("your"): lizards also have bones, muscles, a heart and lungs, so
  it is true for both. Organs are drawn pink, red or dark maroon, bones off-white, never purple.
- **The rain tree (e1103):** a plant is a system with roots, stem and leaves, each with its own function (Ch 1). They are only named here;
  EP2 explains them. The trunk carries the label "stem (trunk)". The rain tree is the book's own example.
- **The snail:** no fact is stated. It moves slowly (true) and always faces the way it moves.
- **Level gate:** no P5/P6 words (no "gravity", "friction", "force" or "energy" spoken; the ramp is just "the ramp").
- **Safety (rulebook + brief):** every rider wears a helmet, buckled, while riding (Basil all through the flashback and the race; Uncle Lim
  always). Uncle Lim sips his kopi only when stopped. The runaway bike has no rider. Small print: **"Ride safely; check your brakes."**
  (cold open and end), **"Fixing brakes or a chain? Ask an adult to help."** (reversal), **"No brakes? Push it, don't ride it."** (reversal).
  Pip's fourth-wall line "Don't do this at home. Seriously." comes straight after the runaway. Nobody is hurt anywhere. The pigeons
  are unhurt and settle calmly. The ice box is full of ice and cans, and Basil sits up at once, helmet on.
