## 1. The chain of questions
1. The ride is in one hour and Pip's bike will not move. *What did he take off, and how do we fix it?* → three fixes; you pick one.
2. Pedal harder! The pedals spin, the back wheel does not. *Why can't strong legs move the bike?* → nothing joins the front gear to the back wheel; one part cannot do another part's job.
3. Then join them with a skipping rope! It slips off. *Isn't a loop a loop?* → look closer: the gear's teeth fit the chain's gaps, so the gear pulls the chain (FUNCTION: each part has its own special job).
4. *What does the chain actually do, from the inside?* → be a link: pulled forward by the front gear, tugging the back gear round.
5. The chain goes back on and the wheel spins. *Why does it work now?* → each part acts on the next (INTERACT).
6. "Squeeze the brakes." Pip took those off too. *Does every part matter, even ones that don't make it go?* → yes: brakes stop it safely, the bell warns people; if the bell falls off it can still move, but less safely: the whole system is likely to be affected (SYSTEM).
7. On the ride Pip puffs. *Is something inside him broken?* → no: his body is made of systems too, working together. And the ride ends under a rain tree: a tree is a system too.
8. Back at the void deck, a chilli plant hops in: *"I'm watered every day. Why am I wilting?"* → EP2, the Plant Help Desk.

## 2. Science checks (every picture and every sentence)
- **The textbook's own words (Inspiring Science P4, Systems opener, p. 3):** "A system is made up of different parts. Each part has its own
  special function. The different parts of a system interact with one another to perform specific functions. When one part of the system
  stops functioning, it is likely that the function of the whole system will be affected." The words that earn marks (e903b) say the same
  in the shorter form a P4 pupil writes in an exam (see section 7); Basil's line e714b uses the book's "its own special function".
- **The bike's drive, drawn true:** the chain runs on the drive side (the side shown) from the front gear (fixed to the pedal cranks) to the
  back gear (fixed to the back wheel's hub). Pedalling forward turns the front gear; its teeth sit in the gaps of the chain and pull the TOP
  run of the chain forward; the chain pulls the back gear round, and the back wheel turns. The bottom run goes back towards the back gear.
  The journey follows exactly this path (forward along the top, round the front gear, back along the bottom, round the back gear).
  Kid words used: "front gear", "back gear" (no "chainring", "sprocket", "crank").
- **Fix 1:** with no chain, pedalling turns only the pedals and the front gear; the back wheel stays still (true). Heart: "Pushing harder on
  one part cannot do another part's job."
- **Fix 2:** a smooth rope on the teeth slips (no gaps for the teeth to sit in), so it cannot pull the back gear (true; it would also jump
  off). The ✗ chip sits at the START of the wrong idea ("✗ a smooth rope: the teeth slip"); no strike-through.
- **The test** is the real mechanic's check: lift the back wheel off the ground and turn a pedal by hand. No riding until brakes are on.
- **"Likely to be affected":** the book says "likely". The think question shows why: no chain, it cannot move; no bell, it can still move,
  but it is less safe. Heart's line keeps the book's word: "the whole system is likely to be affected."
- **The body:** only what the P4 book says in Ch 2 (p. 23-29): bones and muscles move the body (the muscular system works closely with the
  skeletal system), the heart beats faster and he breathes faster when running (the book's own opener: Ali running). No system names are
  taught here (EP5 does that); "Your body is made up of systems too" (Basil, playfully) is the book's "The human body is made up of many
  systems working together." Organs drawn pink, red or dark maroon, bones off-white, never purple.
- **The tree:** the book's plant system (Ch 1): leaves, stem and roots, each with its own function. Only named, not explained (EP2 does it).
  The rain tree is the book's own example (its roots: "strong and sturdy").
- **Basil's tail:** lizards use their tails for balance when they run and climb (true). Not a taught fact; a joke with a true reason.
- **Safety:** helmet on whenever Pip is on the bike; "Fixing a chain? Ask an adult to help." and "Check your brakes before every ride." as
  small print; the bike only stands or is held while it has no brakes.

## 7. The words that earn marks, the exam wording, the misconceptions busted, the non-textbook sentences
**The words that earn marks (Basil reads, e903b):** "A system is made up of different parts. Each part has its own function. The parts
interact to perform a function. When one part stops working, the whole system is likely to be affected."

**The way accepted in PSLE (the exam wording box):**
- "A system is made up of different parts. Each part has its own function." "The parts work together / interact to perform a function."
- "When one part stops working, the whole system is likely to be affected" (keep "likely"; avoid "the whole system will stop", which the
  bell example shows is not always true).
- In an answer about a broken part, name the part, its function and the effect: "The chain joins the front gear to the back wheel. Without
  the chain, the back wheel cannot turn, so the bicycle cannot move."
