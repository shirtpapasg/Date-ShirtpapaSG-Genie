## 1. The chain of questions
1. A cactus is watered every day, kept in the shade and wearing plastic leaves, and it is leaning and soft. *What does it really need?* → three fixes; you pick one.
2. Keep the plastic leaves? *Where are a cactus's leaves, and how does it make food?* → its spines are its leaves (they reduce water loss and protect it); its green stem makes food; the plastic was blocking the light.
3. *So is a cactus a real plant?* → yes: it makes its own food, like every plant.
4. More water? The soil is soaking and the base is soft and brown. *Isn't a desert plant always thirsty?* → it stores water in its thick stem and its spines lose very little; too much water makes it rot.
5. Sun, a pot with holes, water only when dry: three weeks later, firm and green. *Basil was right, so does he get the point?* → no: right answer, wrong reason.
6. "So I'm the only odd plant here?" *Do other plants have parts that look different too?* → woody and non-woody stems; climbers that coil up to the light; creepers that spread their leaves.
7. *Where does the food a leaf makes go?* → be a bit of food: leaf → stem → a root that stores it (STORAGE ROOTS: sweet potato, carrot).
8. *Can a plant start from just a stem?* → a stem cutting grows roots in water. Parts look different because they help plants live in different places. → EP5: an obstacle race and the body's systems.

## 2. Science checks (every picture and every sentence)
- **The book (Inspiring Science P4):** p. 13: "The cactus lives in dry conditions. Cactus leaves are shaped like spines to reduce water loss.
  The sharp spines also help to defend the cactus from animals. Unlike other plants with green leaves, it is the green stem of the cactus
  that makes food." p. 8-9: trees and shrubs have hard, woody stems; some plants, including some herbs (mint), have non-woody stems; the
  baobab's stem stores water. p. 14: some plants have weak, non-woody stems: they coil around structures to climb higher to get enough
  light to make food; others creep on the ground and spread their leaves to get enough light. p. 10: "Some food made by the leaves are
  sometimes stored in the roots. These are called storage roots" (carrots, turnips, sweet potatoes). p. 11: "A part of a stem separated
  from a plant is called a stem cutting. New roots and leaves will grow from a stem cutting if you put it in water." p. 12: "The plant parts
  look different because they help plants growing in different environments to stay alive." Every taught line in EP4 is one of these.
- **"A cactus stores water in its thick stem"** (e403): true (a cactus stem is a water store); the book's own water-storing stem is the
  baobab's. Flagged (section 12).
- **"A cactus is a real plant: it makes its own food, like every plant"** (e307): busts TheOneApp's misconception M-46 ("A cactus is not
  really a plant because it has no proper leaves").
- **The plastic leaves:** taped over the green stem, they block light from the part that makes food; the paler patch under the tape is the
  on-screen evidence (a stem kept from light loses some green). Flagged as a simplification (section 12).
- **Too much water:** a cactus in wet soil every day, in a pot that holds water, rots at the base (true; the same cause as Chilli's roots in
  EP2). Shade: a cactus needs bright light; in dim light it grows pale and stretched (drawn, not taught by name).
- **Climbers drawn true:** the butterfly pea (Clitoria ternatea, common in Singapore gardens: the blue flowers of blue rice and kueh)
  twines its STEM round the pole: the book's "coil around structures". No tendrils are drawn on it. The callback's cucumber plant is a
  climber too (it climbs with tendrils; only named, not drawn).
- **Creeper:** the sweet potato plant's stems creep along the soil with heart-shaped leaves spread to the light (true).
- **Storage roots:** the sweet potato and the carrot are roots that store food (true: a sweet potato is a root, not a stem). The journey: a
  bit of food made in a leaf travels along the stem and down into the root, where it is stored. No "starch", "phloem" or "transport" term.
- **Stem cutting:** the money plant (common in HDB homes in a bottle of water) grows roots from the cut stem in water, then is planted in
  soil (the book's order). Small print: "Cutting a plant? Ask an adult."
- **Woody / non-woody:** the rain tree's trunk (woody: thick, hard); mint (non-woody: soft, green, bends). Both are the book's kinds of
  example (trees; herbs such as mint).
- **Pictures never plant a misconception:** spines are drawn growing from the cactus in clusters (they are its leaves); no "flat leaf"
  hidden anywhere on Spike; the cut-away is of a healthy cactus stem (juicy flesh), labelled, beside Spike's brown base; water loss shown
  only as "loses more water" / "loses very little" (no P5/P6 "water vapour through stomata").

## 7. The words that earn marks, the exam wording, the misconceptions busted, the non-textbook sentences
**The words that earn marks (Basil reads, e903b):** "The cactus has spine-like leaves to reduce water loss. Its green stem makes food.

**The way accepted in PSLE (the exam wording box):**
- "The leaves of the cactus are spine-like to reduce water loss." (Not "the cactus has no leaves"; not "spines are for protection" alone,
  when the question is about water.)
- "The green stem of the cactus makes food." (Not "the cactus does not make food".)
- "The plant has a weak stem. It coils around the pole to climb higher to get more light to make food." Name the structure, its
  feature and how it helps the plant.
- "Food made by the leaves is stored in the storage roots."
