## 1. The chain of questions
1. A leaf lands on the desk. *Which of the three plants nearby dropped it?* → three ways to crack the case; you pick one.
2. Match the colour. All three are green. *Can colour tell plants apart?* → rarely: most leaves are green.
3. Match the size. A young mango leaf is small; the small pandan plant has the longest leaves. *Can size?* → a weak clue: leaves on one plant come in many sizes.
4. *Then what does a scientist look at?* → the parts: the LEAF BLADE (its shape and edge), the LEAF STALK, the main vein and the smaller veins.
5. *How are the veins laid out inside?* → be a drop of water: stalk → main vein → smaller and smaller veins that split and join: NET VEINS.
6. Suspect one, the pandan: its veins run side by side (PARALLEL VEINS). Cleared. *Is the mango tree, with net veins too, the culprit?* → not yet: two suspects have net veins.
7. *What else is different?* → the edge: the mango's is straight, the mystery leaf's is jagged. Cleared.
8. The hibiscus matches every clue, side by side with a leaf still on the hedge. Case closed. *And next?* → a cactus with plastic leaves taped on hops up to the desk: EP4.

## 2. Science checks (every picture and every sentence)
- **The book (Inspiring Science P4, pp. 6-7):** the main parts of a leaf are the leaf blade, the leaf stalk, the main vein and many smaller
  veins. The leaf blade is usually the largest part of a leaf; its edges determine its shape and differ between plants. The leaf stalk
  attaches the leaf to the stem; some are long, some short. The main vein is attached to the leaf stalk and branches into smaller veins.
  The veins are arranged in a parallel pattern or a net pattern. (p. 13:) leaves differ in edge (straight or jagged), shape (oval, round),
  colour (most are green; some purple or red) and texture (waxy, hairy). Every spoken fact in EP3 is one of these.
- **The book's words are used:** "net veins" / "net pattern" (the brief said "network veins"; the book and TheOneApp's spelling list say
  "net"), "parallel veins", "straight edge", "jagged edge".
- **The three suspects, drawn true:**
  - **Pandan** (Pandanus amaryllifolius): long, narrow, strap-like leaves in a spiral from a short stem; veins run side by side along the
    leaf (parallel). It has no long leaf stalk (the leaf clasps the stem): the episode never points to a pandan leaf stalk, and the
    pandan is cleared by its veins.
  - **Mango tree** (Mangifera indica): long, narrow-oval, pointed leaves with a straight (smooth, slightly wavy) edge, a clear main vein,
    net veins, a short leaf stalk; young leaves are smaller and reddish-bronze (true: and a quiet nod to "some leaves are red").
  - **Hibiscus** (Hibiscus rosa-sinensis, the common hedge hibiscus): oval leaves with a pointed tip, a jagged (toothed) edge, net veins
    from a main vein, a short leaf stalk. The mystery leaf is one of these.
- **"Most leaves are green, so colour alone cannot tell these three apart"** (e303): true; the inset shows that some leaves are red or purple
  (the book's own point), so the line does not suggest all leaves are green.
- **"Leaves on one plant come in different sizes, and a small plant can have long leaves"** (e405): true (young and old leaves; pandan).
- **The journey:** a drop of water from the stem into the leaf stalk, along the main vein, out into smaller and smaller veins that split and
  join "like a net". That veins carry water through the leaf is true; the word "transport" in plants and the tubes' names belong to P5, so
  the journey names only the parts the book names (leaf stalk, main vein, smaller veins, leaf blade).
- **The comparison is fair:** the mystery leaf is always compared side by side with a leaf of the suspect, never overlapped (no ghost
  overlaps), and each match or difference is joined by a plain line to the part it describes.
- **The snail's fun fact:** a snail's tongue (the radula) is a ribbon covered in rows of thousands of tiny teeth, used to scrape food such as
  leaves. True; the word "radula" is not used.

## 7. The words that earn marks, the exam wording, the misconceptions busted, the non-textbook sentences
**The words that earn marks (Momo reads, e903m):** "The main parts of a leaf are the leaf blade, the leaf stalk, the main vein and the

**The way accepted in PSLE (the exam wording box):**
- Label a leaf with the book's names: leaf blade, leaf stalk, main vein, smaller veins.

  that describes both), as the book's comparing activity asks.
- Describe differences with the book's words: edge (straight / jagged), shape (oval / round), colour, texture (waxy / hairy).
