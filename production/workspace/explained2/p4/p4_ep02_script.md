## 0c. The villain: why GLITCH, and how the peace is made (teacher's rule, 27 Nov 2026: "for episode 2 onwards, include a villain")

**P4 rule (recorded here as the coordinator asked):** from EP2 on, every episode has a villain, one of the approved recurring troublemakers
(Kopi the Macaque, GLITCH the Robot, Duchess). The villain causes or worsens the problem in a way that IS the science, is funny and in
character (never truly mean or scary), is won over through that episode's scientific method rather than beaten by force, and leaves a
small hook to come back.

**Why GLITCH, not Kopi or Duchess**
- **His mistake IS the science.** GLITCH believes two wrong ideas a child might hold: "more water, on a schedule, is always better"
  (watering on a timer, not by need), and "holes are leaks" (a pot without holes "wastes no water"). Together they make exactly the
  waterlogged, airless soil that rots Chilli's roots. Kopi would raid the plant for food, which repeats fix 3's pigeons and does not cause
  waterlogging. A macaque tipping water into a pot is not real behaviour either, and approaching or feeding macaques is a real safety rule
  in Singapore that we should not blur. Duchess is a contest rival who suits a Challenge (C) episode.
- **His defeat IS the method.** He demands "Show me the data." The look-closer's root inspection is the data. The water test is the fair
  check of the fix. He updates his rule ("water only when the top of the soil is dry") and proves it himself at the end ("Is the top of my
  soil dry?" "...No." "Then, no.").
- **He is funny, never mean or scary:** small, pompous, kind underneath. He crashes when he sees the evidence (BZZT!, swirly screen
  eyes), droops ("Error. My plan was... not efficient."), and Momo's kindness wins him over.
- **The hook:** "Rule saved. Next project: the whole corridor!" (Pip: "Um..."). He can come back in any episode where a rule is applied
  without checking the evidence. His link to Heart's arc (a talking machine) is left untouched in EP2: he is never in the shot for hint #3.

**The peace, beat by beat:** the cause is revealed by his own words (4:11) → Pip's careful objection → GLITCH's "Show me the data." → the
evidence, from Momo's look-closer, on his chest screen → he crashes and droops → Momo's kindness → "Updating!" → he helps (he turns round
politely for the screen gag, and saves the new rule after the water test) → he proves the rule himself at the end → he rolls off with the
hook. The villain's screen time is about 55 s across three scenes: a glimpse at 0:03, the scene at 4:11, the repair, and the end at 6:57.

## 1. The chain of questions
1. A chilli plant is watered every day, yet it is wilting. *Why?* → three fixes; you pick one.
2. Water it more! The water sits on top and spills over the rim. *Isn't a droopy plant always thirsty?* → no: this soil is already full of
   water. *But WHY did no water come out of the bottom?* (Momo's question waits.)
3. Feed it a balanced lunch! Only the pigeons eat. *Don't plants take in food from the soil?* → no: a plant makes its own food, in its
   leaves, using light (MAKE FOOD).
4. Check every part. The leaves are green but drooping; the stem is bending; the soil is soggy. *And the roots, which we can't see?* →
   brown and mushy, and the pot has no hole.
5. *Chilli has loads of water, so why is it droopy?* → roots need air; in waterlogged soil they rot, and rotting roots cannot take in water
   (ABSORB). One part stopped working, and the whole plant was affected: a plant is a system.
6. *Who waters Chilli every day, in a pot with no holes?* → GLITCH, "for maximum efficiency". *Will he believe the evidence?* → he does,
   and he updates his rule.
7. *What is the real fix, and does it work?* → holes in the pot, the rotting roots cut off, fresh soil; the water test drains; water only when
   the top is dry; two weeks later Chilli stands tall.
8. *Where does the water go once a healthy root takes it in?* → be a drop: root → stem → leaf, where food is made (TRANSPORT).
9. A leaf that is not Chilli's lands on the desk. *Whose leaf is it?* → EP3, the Case File.

## 2. Science checks (every picture and every sentence)
- **The book's functions (Inspiring Science P4 Ch 1, pp. 6-11, concept map p. 21; v1's check, unchanged):** leaves make food (they "contain
  a green substance to absorb light so that the plant can make food") and take in and give out gases. The stem holds the plant upright to
  get light, and transports food, water and mineral salts to different parts. The roots hold the plant firmly to the ground and absorb
  water and mineral salts. The words that earn marks say exactly these, plus the book's Systems sentence ("When one part of the system
  stops functioning, it is likely that the function of the whole system will be affected"; EP1's check, p. 3).
- **"A plant does not take in food from the soil. It makes its own food, in its leaves, using light."** (u406; TheOneApp misconception
  M-20). No "photosynthesis", "chlorophyll" or "nutrients" (P5/P6 words). Never "plant food" for fertiliser.
- **Roots absorb water and mineral salts** (never "food" or "nutrients": TheOneApp mix-up card plants:38). **The stem transports** (never
  "stores": card plants:39). j805 says the stem carries "food from the leaves to other parts" (the book: "to different parts").
- **Why wet soil can make a plant wilt (the twist, x602-x603):** roots need air. In waterlogged soil the spaces between the soil
  particles fill with water, so there is little air; roots kept like this rot (soft and brown) and cannot take in water, so the leaves
  droop. This is true and said at P4 level: no "oxygen", "respiration" or "root hairs". The picture also shows a wobble at the base of
  the stem, labelled "cannot hold the plant firmly" (shown, not spoken, for length).
- **The cause is one, not three:** the pot has no drainage hole AND it is watered with a big jug every day. Light is fine at the start (the
  leaves are green); Chilli is still moved to the sunny corridor afterwards, because leaves need light to make food.
- **GLITCH's two wrong ideas are answered on screen within seconds:** "One jug, every day, for maximum efficiency" and "No holes, so no
  water is wasted" (4:11-4:25) are answered by Pip at once (4:28: "the water had nowhere to go, so the roots got no air") and by the
  evidence (4:36-4:45). Heart never says them. GLITCH's new rule says the right thing ("water only when the top of the soil is dry").
- **Basil's s653, "One part stopped working, and the whole plant was affected!"**, describes THIS plant (it was affected). The general
  rule on the card keeps the book's "likely": "When one part stops working, the whole plant is likely to be affected."
- **The fix is real gardening:** drainage holes drilled in the base of a ceramic pot (a masonry or tile bit, done by an adult) with a saucer
  under it; the soft, brown roots cut off with scissors by an adult; fresh soil; water when the top of the soil feels dry. The water test
  (water drains out of the holes) is the evidence now; two weeks of recovery is the evidence later (new white roots, new leaves). Some
  healthy roots are shown left after the trim: plants rarely recover from advanced root rot (section 14, item 3).
- **Healthy roots are firm and white or light-coloured; rotting roots are brown, soft and smell bad** (drawn so).
- **The pigeons:** rock pigeons drawn true. They land, eat the crumbs and fly off unhurt; nobody touches them. Small print: "Please don't
  feed the pigeons." (NParks: leftover food attracts pigeons; feeding them is an offence.)
- **Level gate:** no P5/P6 words (no photosynthesis, chlorophyll, stomata, xylem, phloem, root hair, oxygen, respiration).
- **Safety:** "Drills and scissors: an adult's job." (small print, repair scene) and Pip's spoken "an adult must cut off the rotting
  roots". Nobody is hurt; the water on Basil's feet is just water. Uncle Lim holds the drill and the scissors; no child or buddy uses them.

**Facts verified for v2 (27 Nov 2026), with sources** (this session could not open the pages themselves, because the network blocks those
hosts, so each was checked through the search engine's extracts of the page):
- Chilli padi is *Capsicum frutescens* (bird's eye chilli), easy to grow in pots in Singapore; chilli is "especially vulnerable to root rot
  caused by waterlogging" and needs full sun. NParks Flora & Fauna Web (https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/5/2/5206) and
  Gardening Singapore, Chilli (https://gardeningsg.nparks.gov.sg/page-index/edible-plants/chilli).
- Wet soils leave little space for air to reach the roots, which favours root rot. Rotting roots can no longer absorb water, so the plant
  wilts in wet soil. A pot with no hole stays wet at the bottom. The fix is to drill a drainage hole or use a liner pot. Plants rarely
  recover from advanced root rot. Sources: University of Illinois Extension, Container Drainage Options
  (https://extension.illinois.edu/container-gardens/container-drainage-options); Mississippi State Extension, "So why do plant containers
  have drain holes" (https://extension.msstate.edu/blog/so-why-do-plant-containers-have-drain-holes); Missouri Botanical Garden, How to
  water indoor plants (https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/gardens-gardening/your-garden/help-for-the-home-gardener/advice-tips-resources/visual-guides/how-to-water-indoor-plants.aspx);
  Virginia Tech SPES-804 (https://www.pubs.ext.vt.edu/content/dam/pubs_ext_vt_edu/spes/spes-804/SPES-804.pdf).
- Leftover food attracts pigeons, and feeding pigeons is an offence in Singapore (a fine of up to $10,000). NParks pigeon advisory
  (https://www.nparks.gov.sg/-/media/05---pigeon_a4_03.pdf) and NParks AVS, Pigeons in Singapore
  (https://avs.nparks.gov.sg/wildlife/encountering-wildlife/house-crows-pigeons-javan-mynas/pigeons/).
- Singapore school P4 handbooks under the 2023 syllabus list Plant System (parts and functions): leaves make food; the stem holds the plant
  up and carries water from the roots and food from the leaves; the roots take in water and hold the plant. The water and food tubes come
  in P5. Sources: Shuqun Primary P4 Science Handbook (https://www.shuqunpri.moe.edu.sg/files/P4_Science_Handbook.pdf); Admiralty Primary
  P4 Science (https://www.admiraltypri.moe.edu.sg/files/Level%20Matters/P4_Science.pdf). The marks-earning wording itself follows the
  textbook, as quoted above.

## 8. The words that earn marks, the exam wording, the misconceptions busted, the non-textbook sentences
**The words that earn marks (Momo reads, m903):** "The roots hold the plant firmly in the soil and absorb water and mineral salts. The stem
holds the plant upright and transports water, mineral salts and food to all parts of the plant. The leaves make food for the plant. A plant
is a system. When one part stops working, the whole plant is likely to be affected."

**The way accepted in PSLE (the exam wording box):**
- Roots "absorb water and mineral salts" (not "absorb food", "absorb nutrients" or "get food from the soil"); roots "hold the plant firmly
  in the soil".
- The stem "transports water, mineral salts and food" (not "stores food" for a typical stem); it "holds the plant upright so that the leaves
  can get (more) light".
- Leaves "make food" (P4).
- Explaining a wilting plant in waterlogged soil (an exam-style "why"): "The soil was waterlogged, so the roots rotted. The roots could not
  absorb enough water, so the plant wilted." Name the part, its function and the effect.
- "When one part stops working, the whole plant is likely to be affected" (keep "likely").
