## 1. The chain of questions
1. A leaf has landed on the Help Desk, and GLITCH says it is the mango tree's. *Which of the three plants really dropped it?* → three ways to
   crack the case; you pick one.
2. Match the colour. All three plants are green. *Can colour tell these plants apart?* → no: most leaves are green (and even the mango tree's
   young leaves are red).
3. Measure the size. One mango leaf is bigger, one smaller; the little pandan has the longest leaves. *Can size?* → a weak clue: the leaves on
   one plant come in many sizes.
4. *Then what does a scientist look at?* → the parts: the LEAF BLADE (its shape and its edge), the LEAF STALK, the main vein and the smaller
   veins. *Where do all the little veins go?*
5. Be a drop of water: stalk → main vein → smaller and smaller veins that split and join: NET VEINS.
6. Suspect one, the pandan: its veins run side by side, like the lanes on a running track (PARALLEL VEINS). Cleared. *The mango tree has net
   veins too: case closed?* → not yet: two suspects have net veins.
7. *What else is different?* → the edge: the mango's is straight, the mystery leaf's is jagged. Cleared.
8. The hibiscus matches every clue, side by side with a leaf still on the hedge. Case closed. *Will GLITCH believe the data?* → he does,
   and he files the lesson.
9. *Can a fallen leaf go back on?* → no; the plant grows new leaves. *And who taped plastic leaves on that cactus?* → EP4.

## 2. Science checks (every picture and every sentence)
- **The book (Inspiring Science P4, pp. 6-7; v1's check, unchanged):** the main parts of a leaf are the leaf blade, the leaf stalk, the main
  vein and many smaller veins. The leaf blade is usually the largest part of a leaf; its edges determine its shape and differ between plants.
  The leaf stalk attaches the leaf to the stem; some are long, some short. The main vein is attached to the leaf stalk and branches into
  smaller veins. The veins are arranged in a parallel pattern or a net pattern. (p. 13:) leaves differ in edge (straight or jagged), shape
  (oval, round), colour (most are green; some purple or red) and texture (waxy, hairy). Every spoken fact in EP3 is one of these, or one of
  the true lines below.
- **The book's words are used:** "net veins" / "net pattern" (never "network veins"), "parallel veins", "straight edge", "jagged edge".
- **The three suspects, drawn true** (checked against NParks Flora & Fauna Web, 28 Nov 2026):
  - **Pandan** (*Pandanus amaryllifolius*): long, narrow, linear leaves, about 25-75 cm long and 2-5 cm wide, slightly pleated, in a spiral
    from a short stem; **parallel** venation; entire edges and a pointed tip; no long leaf stalk (the base clasps the stem). A pandan leaf can
    easily be longer than Pip is tall. The episode never points at a pandan leaf stalk; the pandan is cleared by its veins.
  - **Mango tree** (*Mangifera indica*): simple, deep-green, oblong to lance-shaped leaves up to about 30 cm long, with an **entire, slightly
    wavy edge** (the book's "straight edge"; question 2), a clear main vein and net veins; **young flushes coppery red to bronze** (true: the
    o405 inset). Its leaf stalk is a few centimetres long.
  - **Hibiscus** (*Hibiscus rosa-sinensis*, the common hedge hibiscus): oval (ovate) leaves with a pointed tip and a **toothed (jagged)
    edge**, net veins from a main vein, joined to the stem by a leaf stalk. The mystery leaf is one of these, the same leaf in every shot.
  - **Why the match leaves out the stalk:** hibiscus and mango stalks overlap in length, so "the same short leaf stalk" (v1) would not tell
    them apart fairly. The match uses veins, edge and shape.
- **"Most leaves are green, so colour alone cannot tell these three apart"** (o405): true for these three. The inset (the mango's young red
  leaves, a purple-leaved plant) keeps the book's point that some leaves are red or purple, so the line never suggests all leaves are green.
- **"Leaves on one plant come in different sizes, and a small plant can have long leaves"** (s507): true (young and old mango leaves; the
  pandan). No invented measurements: the size test shows "bigger" and "smaller" only.
- **The journey** (j701-j705): a drop of water from the leaf stalk along the main vein and out into smaller veins that split and join "like a
  net". That veins carry water through the leaf is true; it continues EP2's "the stem carries water and mineral salts up" (approved).
  The tubes' names and plant transport in detail are P5, so the journey names only the book's parts (question 3).
- **Uncle Lim's line** (g908): a fallen leaf cannot be stuck back on, and a plant grows new leaves (the inset shows new leaves unfolding at a
  hibiscus twig tip). True; no wrong idea is left standing from GLITCH's taping (it is called out in the cold open, stopped by Basil at
  0:28 and undone on screen at 6:02).
- **GLITCH's wrong ideas are answered within seconds:** "Owner: the mango tree" (0:13) → Basil stops him (0:28), the case answers it.

## 8. The words that earn marks, the exam wording, the misconceptions busted, the non-textbook sentences
**The words that earn marks (Pip reads, m1003):** "The main parts of a leaf are the leaf blade, the leaf stalk, the main vein and the smaller
veins. The leaf stalk attaches the leaf to the stem. The veins can be arranged in a parallel pattern or a net pattern."

**The way accepted in PSLE (the exam wording box):**
- Label a leaf with the book's names: leaf blade, leaf stalk, main vein, smaller veins.
- "Leaf A has parallel veins, while leaf B has net veins." When comparing, name the feature for BOTH leaves (a difference that describes both),
  as the book's comparing activity asks.
- Describe differences with the book's words: edge (straight / jagged), shape (oval / round), colour, texture (waxy / hairy).
- To tell two leaves apart, compare more than one part (two leaves with net veins can still come from different plants).
