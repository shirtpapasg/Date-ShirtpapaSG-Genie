## 1. The chain of questions
1. A chilli plant is watered every day, yet it is wilting. *Why?* → three fixes; you pick one.
2. Water it more! The water sits on top and spills. *Isn't a droopy plant always thirsty?* → no: this soil is already full of water.
3. Feed it curry puff! Only ants come. *Don't plants eat food from the soil?* → no: a plant makes its own food in its leaves, using light (MAKE FOOD).
4. Check every part. Leaves green but drooping; stem soft and bending; *and the roots, which we can't see?* → brown, mushy, in a pot with no hole.
5. *The soil is soaking wet, so how can the plant be thirsty?* → roots need air; in waterlogged soil they rot, and rotting roots cannot take in water (ABSORB).
6. *So what is the real fix, and does it work?* → a pot with holes, trimmed roots, water only when the top is dry, light; the water drains; two weeks later Chilli stands tall.
7. *Where does the water go once a healthy root takes it in?* → be a drop: root → stem → leaf (TRANSPORT).
8. A leaf drifts onto the desk, and it is not Chilli's. *Whose leaf is it?* → EP3, the Case File.

## 2. Science checks (every picture and every sentence)
- **The book's functions (Inspiring Science P4 Ch 1, pp. 6-11, and its concept map p. 21):** leaves: make food; take in and give out
  gases (tiny openings). Stem: holds the plant upright to get light; transports food, water and mineral salts to different parts. Roots:
  hold the plant firmly to the ground; absorb water and mineral salts. The words that earn marks say exactly these.
- **"A plant does not take in food from the soil. It makes its own food, in its leaves, using light."** (e404; misconception M-20 in
  TheOneApp). The book: leaves "contain a green substance to absorb light so that the plant can make food". No "photosynthesis",
  "chlorophyll" or "nutrients" (P5/P6 words). Never "plant food" for fertiliser.
- **Roots absorb water and mineral salts** (never "food" or "nutrients": TheOneApp mix-up card plants:38). **The stem transports** (never
  "stores": card plants:39).
- **Why wet soil can make a plant wilt (the twist):** roots need air (the P4 activity book: a plant needs air, water and light). In
  waterlogged soil the spaces between soil particles fill with water, so there is little air; roots kept like this rot (go soft and brown)
  and cannot take in water, so the leaves droop. True and stated at P4 level (no "oxygen", "respiration" or "root hairs").
- **The cause is one, not three:** the pot has no drainage hole, and Chilli is watered every day. Light is fine at the start (the leaves are
  green); Chilli is still moved to the sunny corridor afterwards, because leaves need light to make food.
- **The fix is real gardening:** a pot with drainage holes and a saucer; trim the soft, brown roots (an adult, with scissors); fresh soil;
  water when the top of the soil feels dry. The water test (water drains out of the hole) is the immediate evidence; two weeks of
  recovery is the long one (new white roots, new leaves). (Recovery depends on how many roots rotted: see section 12.)
- **Healthy roots are firm and white or light-coloured; rotting roots are brown, soft and smell bad** (drawn so).
- **Gases:** only "tiny openings let gases in and out" (the book's words); no "stomata", "oxygen", "carbon dioxide" in this episode.
- **The journey:** soil → a fine white root takes the drop in → up the stem → into a leaf "where food is made". No P5 labels (no xylem,
  root hair). The book: "Water and mineral salts are then transported to different parts of the plants by the stem."

## 7. The words that earn marks, the exam wording, the misconceptions busted, the non-textbook sentences
**The words that earn marks (Pip reads, e903p):** "The roots hold the plant firmly in the soil and absorb water and mineral salts. The stem
holds the plant upright and transports water, mineral salts and food to the different parts of the plant. The leaves make food for the
plant, and take in and give out gases."

**The way accepted in PSLE (the exam wording box):**
- Roots "absorb water and mineral salts" (not "absorb food", "absorb nutrients" or "get food from the soil").
- The stem "transports water, mineral salts and food" (not "stores food" for a typical stem); it "holds the plant upright so that the
  leaves can get (more) light".
- Leaves "make food" (P4); "take in and give out gases".
- Explaining a wilting plant in waterlogged soil (an exam-style "why"): "The roots rotted / were damaged, so they could not absorb enough
  water. The plant wilted." Name the part, its function and the effect.

**Misconceptions busted (each answered within seconds):**
| The wrong idea (only ever a buddy's) | Answered at | By what |

not take in food from the soil; it makes its own food in its leaves using light), e502p-e503p (leaves green, can make food, tiny openings
