## 2. The spine and the split

**One spine: a field trip, the "What can living things DO?" trail.** EP1 was a 30-day box. EP2 was a build-and-reveal (the Buddy
Hotel). EP3 is the third model in `success_examples.md`: the field trip to a real place full of things to discover (Sesame Street
at the farm), with a rhythm as the memory hook.

Why a field trip fits THIS topic better than the other ideas weighed:
- **The evidence cannot come to us.** A mimosa grows on a grass patch. A hen hatches her eggs in a hen house. Kittens stay with
  their mother. A fern keeps its spores under its leaves. None of these can honestly go into a box or a kit on a window ledge, and
  animals must be shown cared for, so the buddies go to them.
- **A "Living Things Detective kit" would be a second build-and-reveal in a row**, and its best use is testing a mystery object,
  which EP1 already did with the six things in the box.
- **A trail has a built-in progress bar.** The trail card has three empty circles: GROW, RESPOND, REPRODUCE. One stamp is earned
  each time the class has seen the thing happen and said the exam sentence. The full card at the end IS the big idea in one picture:
  "Living things grow, respond to changes and reproduce."
- **It continues the story.** EP2 ended in the community garden under the block in Toa Payoh, with Basil touching the folding
  plant. EP3 starts on that shot. EP1's chant asked "What can it DO?"; this trail answers it. EP1's plant on the window ledge
  comes back as the plant that grows towards sunlight.
- One beat is borrowed from the Dr. Plants hatching video (a day counter and "will it hatch?"), but as a round inside the trip, not
  as a second spine.

| Stop | Place | What we find | Stamp | Which part |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The community garden and grass patch under the block, Toa Payoh | The mimosa folds when touched and re-opens. Eyelids in bright sunlight. The plant on the window ledge. A snail. | RESPOND | Part 1 |
| 2 | A hen house on a small farm in Kranji, Day 21 | Five eggs: four hatch. The farm cat's kittens. A tomato's seeds. A fern's spores. | REPRODUCE | Part 2 |

**Why the split is here.** Part 1 is the mimosa mystery from EP2's dare, asked and fully answered: "respond", with the shrink and
the HOLD ON! round on what is NOT responding. Part 2 is the farm: "reproduce" and "grow", the two ideas that are about young ones,
and the takeaway. Part 1 ends at the hen house on Day 21, on a question a child can already argue about: will every egg hatch?
Each part has its own goals, its own key words, its own score and its own lizard.

**Order.** The book's order is grow, respond, reproduce. The videos teach respond (it is the dare), then reproduce, then grow (the
new chicks will grow), and always SAY the three in the book's order: the trail card's circles, the chant and the last Mark Sentence.
EP2 did the same with water, food and air. See open question 2.

**The shrink (Part 1, about 29 s).** While we wait the "few minutes" for the leaf to open, you shrink down and stand ON a mimosa
leaf. A fingertip touches it once; the little leaves fold inwards, two by two, towards you; the whole leaf droops; we wait; the
leaves open and the light comes back. It is true at P3 level and uses no new term: every action in it is from the book's own
sentences (fold inwards, droop, re-open after a few minutes). Nothing is said about HOW the plant does it.

**The trail card, stamp by stamp:**

| When | Stamp | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1, 2:39 | RESPOND (the mimosa) | 1 |
| Part 2, 2:07 | REPRODUCE (the hen and her chicks) | 2 |

**Four things the teacher should know before reading:**
- **The times are a drawn story, not data.** The mimosa folds "in seconds" and re-opens "after a few minutes" (the book's words);
  the hen's eggs hatch on Day 21. Nobody has timed this exact plant or nest. Time-lapse shots carry a small "speeded up" tag, and
  the take-home dare asks the child to time a real mimosa. Please correct any time you know to be off.
- **Four of five eggs hatch because the story needs one honest "no".** The round asks about Momo's rule ("EVERY egg hatches"),
  not about this one nest, so one egg that does not hatch settles it fairly. Heart's general sentence is "Not every egg hatches."
- **Episode 4 is not taught.** Seeds and spores appear only in the book's two sentences for this chapter (1.24). There is no
  "flowering", "non-flowering", "spore bags", "fruit" or "four groups" here; the four groups are only the dare, and none is named.
- **Wrong ideas are only ever a buddy's guess or question,** each answered within seconds: "it's shy" (Basil), "anything that changes
  is responding" (Pip), "every egg hatches" (Momo, Pip), "growing is only getting bigger" (Momo, as a question), "a fern makes
  seeds" (Basil's notes).

**The wrong idea rotates:** Basil (Part 1, Round 1), Pip (Part 1, HOLD ON!), Momo and Pip (Part 2, Round 1), Basil (Part 2, his
notes). In EP2 it went Basil, Pip, Basil, Momo. Nobody is "the one who is always wrong", and bold Basil gets to be the only buddy who
is right about the eggs.

**Score.** Each part keeps its own score.
- Part 1: Round Zero = 1, Round 1 = 1, HOLD ON! = 1. A perfect game is 3 fingers. The Buddies score 2 (Round Zero: all three; Round 1:
  Pip and Momo).
- Part 2: Round 1 = 1, Showdown = 2. A perfect game is 3 fingers. The Buddies score 2 (the Showdown; in Round 1 only Basil is right).
- The Buddies score as a team when at least two of them are right with a good reason. "No lock-in, no point."
- So in both parts Heart can say EP1's line: "More than two? You beat the Buddies. Three fingers? High five!"

**The lock-in (the same routine every time):** Heart asks a closed question with two or three choices; the viewer answers with
fingers; the ring timer drains for 5 s (6 s in the Showdown) while the music drops to a woodblock tick that speeds up; a bell;
"LOCKED IN!"; only then do the buddies answer, one line and one reason each. The answer stays hidden during the ring.

**Colours.** The answer cards are plain cream with big dark-violet numbers 1, 2, 3, because the choices are different in every
round. The three stamps keep three fixed colours all episode: GROW green, RESPOND orange, REPRODUCE blue. No buddy colour and no
sun-yellow is used for a choice or a stamp. See open question 9.

**Marks used in the tables:**
- **[LOCK-IN n]** the viewer answers with fingers. **[HOLD ON!]** cross your arms. **[SCORE]** score hand.
- **[SAY IT]** a buddy (or all three) SAYS "Say it with me!" or "Say it with us!" aloud, with lip-sync and an inviting gesture; then a
  0.8 s pause; then Heart reads or the chant starts.
- **[YOUR TURN!]** the tag by the YOU hands in the chant; one bar (about 1.6 s) for the class, with the beat going on softly.
- **[MARK SENTENCE n of N]** exact textbook words on the cream card, read by Heart with no change, then 2 s of stillness.
- **[BOOK WORDS]** other exact book sentences that Heart says, shown as a plain caption.
- **[KEY WORD]** said for the first time: pops LARGE with a chime, holds 1.5 s, then slides to the key-word shelf (top right, under
  the score pill; sun-yellow pills stacking downwards).
- **[BELL]** a teacher pause point: a small bell icon shows for 3 s (see the teacher sheet).
- **✗** a buddy's idea that is not right: a small ✗ at the start of the speech bubble only; the words stay readable; it is answered by
  evidence within seconds. Wrong ideas are never in Heart's voice.

**Picture rules that apply to every row (from `SERIES_FORMAT.md` sections 5 to 7, and the P3 plan's realism notes):**
- The buddies are the teacher's painted cut-out sprites and **always sit in their circular aura discs** when they are next to real
  things (the mimosa, the notebook, the nest box, the cat basket, the pots, the easel). They are presenters, not tiny animals in the set.
- **Nothing floats.** The trail card stands on a small stand; answer cards, photo boards, the stopwatch, the easel and the crates
  stand on the ground; things arrive by being carried or slid by a buddy. An adult's hands cut the tomato and turn the fern leaf.
- **The mimosa:** leaves made of many small leaves in two rows along a stalk; when touched, the small leaves fold inwards pair by
  pair, THEN the stalk droops; re-opening takes "a few minutes" and always has a stopwatch or clock in the shot, never instant.
  Small prickles on the stem; pink pom-pom flowers. No plant has a mouth, eyes or a face.
- **The window-ledge plant** leans towards the window (the light side), not away.
- **The animals:** a brown hen; yellow chicks, wet at first, fluffy later; the cat has four legs and her kittens are smaller copies;
  the snail has a coiled shell and its body pulls in; the ice cream drips; the door swings with wind lines. Fern spores are brown
  dots in rows on the underside of the leaf. The growth line keeps the SAME person: taller, longer limbs, more hair.
- Label lines are plain lines with no arrowheads and never cross other things; every label and small print fits fully inside the frame.
- Pose swaps: squash, hard cut, stretch, overshoot, a small hop, sparkles and dust. Never a cross-fade.
- No text in the bottom fifth of the end scenes. No burned-in captions (an `.srt` is made for each part).
- The shelf slides aside during the cold open, the countdown, the shrink and full-screen cards.
- Something changes on screen every 10 to 15 s (the lock-in rings, the shrink and the still egg are the planned quiet moments).
- Word check: "dead", "die", "kill", "cheat", "trap" and "dumb" are never spoken. The book's definition of extinct ("...they will all
  die out.") is shown on the strip as the P3 plan asks; Heart says "disappear forever".
- Safety and care on screen: "One finger, one leaf." · "The stem has tiny prickles." · "Never pull the plant." · "Wash your hands." ·
  "Look. Do not pick it up." · "We look. We do not touch. The chicks stay with the hen." · "An adult cuts."

**How the times were estimated.** The same way as the EP2 script (spoken words, plus 0.45 s before each of Heart's lines and 0.4 s
before each buddy's line, plus every planned pause: rings, the 0.8 s "get ready" pauses, the 1.5 s key-word holds, the 2 s card
holds, the YOUR TURN bars, the 1.2 s read-along gaps), **but with the speaking rate measured from EP1's finished videos at the new
slower pace**: `ep01_part1.mp4` is 5:05 with 595 spoken words and `ep01_part2.mp4` is 4:50 with 509, which is about **0.355 s for
each spoken word** once the planned pauses are taken out. The EP2 script used 0.28 s (the pace before the teacher's "too fast"
note), so its 4:39 and 4:32 may well come out over 5:00 (about 5:20 by this measure); that is for the EP2 team to check, and nothing in `ep02/` was changed.
Part 1 here has 574 spoken words and Part 2 has 589. The real times come from the voice files; expect each part to land within
about 15 s of the estimate. If a part comes out over 5:00, see section 6.

**Real classroom activities (choose one or two):**
- **The mimosa hunt (10 minutes, school field or a grass patch).** Rules first: one finger, one leaf; the stem has tiny prickles;
  never pull the plant; wash your hands after. In pairs: one touches, one times. Record: seconds to fold, minutes to open again
  (check back after 5, 10 and 15 minutes). Ask: what was the change? What was the response? Why might folding protect the plant?
- **Which way will it grow? (1 week).** Stand a small pot plant by a window. Draw it on Day 1. Do not turn the pot. Draw it again on
  Day 5. Then turn the pot half-way round and watch for another week. (It grows towards the sunlight.)
- **Bigger AND more complex (2 weeks).** Grow green beans on wet cotton wool. Twice a week measure the height (bigger) and count the
  leaves and roots (more complex). Two columns in the table, so "grow" always gets both.
- **Responding or only changing? (card sort).** Snail pulling into its shell · your eyelids in bright sunlight · a plant growing towards
  a window · the mimosa folding · an ice cream melting · a door banging shut in the wind · a puddle drying up. Rule: is it a living
  thing, AND is it doing something because of a change around it?
- **How do they reproduce? (card sort).** Young born alive: cat, humans, rabbit. Eggs that hatch: hen, lizard, mosquito, a robin.
  Seeds: tomato. Spores: fern. Extra numbers from the P3 plan: humans usually have one baby at a time; a rabbit can have up to
  twelve; a female mosquito lays 100 to 200 eggs at once. Ask: why might an animal lay so many eggs? (Not every egg hatches.)
- Looking at small animals: look, do not pick up; a snail is touched once, gently, by the teacher, and left where it lives; wash hands.
  Do not handle chicks or eggs at a farm.

**The mind map (from the P3 plan):** "Living things can" in the middle. Three branches: **Grow** (bigger, more complex) · **Respond to
changes** (eyelids, plant to light, mimosa) · **Reproduce** (young born alive, eggs hatch, seeds, spores).

**The answer frame (for "Is this a living thing responding? Explain."):**
1. Is it a living thing? "The ______ is a living thing."
2. What changed around it? "It was ______ (touched / in bright light)."
3. What did it do? "It ______ (folded its leaves / pulled into its shell). It responded to a change."

**Marking points (exact book words):**
- "Living things grow, respond to changes and reproduce." (all three; in this order)
- "When living things grow, they increase in size. They also become more complex." (bigger alone is not complete)
- "Living things respond to changes in the environment." (say *respond*, not *shy*, *scared* or *sleeping*)
- "The ability to respond to changes in our environment helps to protect us from danger and keeps us safe."
- "When living things reproduce, there will be more of them. The ability to reproduce is important to ensure the survival of all
  living things." (say *reproduce*; the lab's "young ones" is not the exam word)
- "Some animals reproduce by giving birth to their young alive. Other animals lay eggs and their young hatch out of the eggs."
- "Some plants reproduce by producing seeds. Other plants can reproduce through spores."
- Extinct: "Living things that are not able to survive will become extinct. This means that they will all die out."

**Common slips to listen for:** "The plant is shy." · "Plants cannot respond." · "The ice cream responded to the sun." · "Growing means
getting bigger." (only half) · "Every egg hatches." · "All plants have seeds." · "Reproduce means grow."

**Key words to read together:** Part 1: mimosa, respond to changes. Part 2: reproduce, spores, extinct, more complex.

## 11. Open questions for the teacher

1. **Two parts at about 4:48 and 4:55, or three shorter parts?** At the pace of the finished EP1 (5:05 and 4:50), this
   chapter fills two parts right up to the 5-minute line, and that is after taking out the Part 1 chant, the spoken goals and a few
   game lines. Three parts of about 4 minutes (Respond · Reproduce · Grow and the six clues) would give room for the chant in every
   part, a round on "grow", the P3 plan's baby and rabbit counters and the lab line. Which do you prefer?
2. **The order.** The book goes grow, respond, reproduce. The videos teach respond first (it is EP2's dare), then reproduce, then
   grow, and always SAY the three in the book's order. Is that fine? (EP2 did the same with water, food, air.)
3. **No chant in Part 1.** It is only in Part 2, as in EP1. Would you rather have it in both parts (about 12 s more in Part 1, so the
   countdown or the field-trip line would go)?
4. **"Environment".** The exam sentence uses it, but the book does not define it. We left out our own gloss ("The environment is
   everything around a living thing.") for length and for safety, and put it on the teacher sheet. Should Heart say it in the video,
   and should ENVIRONMENT be a third key-word pill in Part 1?
