## 1. How this episode follows the rulebook, and what was left out

- **One spine: a build-and-reveal.** Four crates with a **?** (they slid in at the end of Episode 3). They are opened one by one. Part 2 opens the plants crate again and sorts it into two.
- **Two parts**, each with its own goals, score, key words, lizard and ending. Part 1 ends on a question; Part 2 opens with a silent recap and a true "Last time...".
- **BE THE THING in both parts** (section 1 of the rulebook): Part 1, you shrink in three steps to the size of bacteria; Part 2, you are a spore. Each has scale tags, a short card at each stop, calm music, and the book's words straight after.
- **Real Singapore places:** a farm in Kranji (Episode 3's), then the community garden under a block in Toa Payoh (Episodes 1 and 3's), with a rain tree and a bird's nest fern.
- **Exam-exact:** seven definitions are quoted word for word (section 10 A). The grass misconception (plan, accuracy note for 2.7) is the Showdown.
- **Wrong ideas are only ever a buddy's guess**, each answered within seconds, except Basil's opening guess (big, small, green, scary), which is the question of Part 1 and is answered by the four crates and closed in his own words at the end of segment 8.
- **Every "Say it with me / us!" is spoken**, then 0.8 s. Each Mark Sentence holds 2 s. Each new key word pops large for 1.5 s.
- **Left out on purpose:** Linnaeus and two-part names (definition 2.2: a fourth idea; it goes in the video description); the fifth group, protists (the book: "which you will learn about in future"); the comparing table of plants and animals (the two definitions are shown one after the other instead; the book's "similarities only" wording of comparison, 2.4, is not used); useful and harmful fungi and bacteria, microscopes (Episode 7 on the plan, pp. 34-39); the School Lab 2.1A line (as in Episode 3, held back until that lab is signed off; it is in the description wording below); the atomic-habit and mind-map beats of the old format.
- **Spoken examples:** bird's nest fern, grass, hibiscus (in Pip's notes). **Shown, not spoken:** water hyacinth, water clover (picture chips under the two Mark Sentence cards).

## 2. The spine and the split

- **Part 1 = "what are the four?"** Why sort at all (too many to study one by one); the first two crates are easy (plants, animals); the mushroom needs a crate of its own (fungi); the last crate looks empty until you shrink (bacteria). Then the test that separates plants from animals (who makes their own food), used at once in HOLD ON!
- **Part 2 = "plants, sorted again".** The chilli plant with no flowers today (flowering: flower, fruit, seeds); the bird's nest fern (non-flowering: spores in spore bags); "spores mean fungus?" answered with Part 1's test; grass (it can flower); the four groups as a chant.
- **The split point** is a question a child can argue about at home: no flowers today, so is it a flowering plant?

## 3. Cast, the game and the conventions

- **Heart** (Kokoro af_heart, speed 0.95, about 0.45 s between sentences; slower and softer in the two journeys). Never says a wrong idea.
- **Pip** (purple #8B5CF6, careful), **Basil** (teal #14B8A6, bold), **Momo** (coral #FB7158, curious): the painted sprites, always in their aura discs, in their Character Voice Kit voices about 7% slower. Pose swaps: squash, hard cut, stretch, hop.
- **The wrong idea rotates:** Part 1: Basil (the opening guess), Momo and Basil (Round 1), Pip (the green rule). Part 2: Momo (Round 1), Basil right for a silly reason (no point), Basil's "fern is a fungus?", Pip (the grass line).
- **Scores.** Part 1: YOU up to 2 (Round 1, HOLD ON!), BUDDIES 1 (Pip, Round 1). Part 2: YOU up to 3 (Round 1; Showdown 2), BUDDIES 2 (Pip, Round 1; the Showdown).
- **Colours.** The four crates: PLANTS green, ANIMALS orange, FUNGI brown, BACTERIA blue. Part 2's two choices: FLOWERING green, NON-FLOWERING blue. No buddy colour is used for an answer.
- **Things do not float.** Crates, cards, pots and boards slide low along the ground with dust or stand on little feet; the mushroom card makes one low hop into its crate; the magnifying glass is on a stand.
- **Bacteria** are drawn as rods, spheres and spirals only: no faces, no arms or legs (plan note for 2.31).
- **Care.** The hen and her chicks stay on the nest, the cat in her basket; living things are sorted as picture cards, never put in a crate. "Look. Do not pick." "An adult cuts."
- **The lizard** is "lizard", never "cicak"; one in each part, each revealed before the part ends.

**Mark Sentences (pupils say them with the buddies).**
1. The grouping of living things based on their similarities and differences is called classification.
2. Living things can be classified into four main groups — plants, animals, fungi and bacteria.
3. Flowering plants bear flowers. They reproduce from seeds. The flowers in flowering plants develop into fruits. The seeds are found in the fruits.
4. Non-flowering plants do not bear flowers. They reproduce from spores that may be found in spore bags.

**Also quoted from the book:** Plants are living things that usually have leaves, stems and roots. They are able to make their own food when there is light. · Animals are unable to make their own food. They depend on other living things for food. · Bacteria are tiny living things and they come in different shapes and sizes. · Plants can be classified into two groups — flowering plants and non-flowering plants.

**Key words:** classification · fungi · bacteria · flowering plants · spore bags · non-flowering plants.

**Misconceptions the videos work on.** (1) "Groups are about size or colour" (Basil's guess; Pip's green rule). (2) "A mushroom is a plant." (3) "If we cannot see it, nothing is there." (4) "No flower open today means non-flowering": the group depends on whether the plant CAN flower (the chilli plant, grass). (5) "Spores mean fungus": a fern makes its own food, so it is a plant.

**After watching.** (a) Sort ten picture cards into four hoops; one hoop will stay nearly empty: why? (b) School Lab 2.1A "Bloom or not", Part (b) Sort plants. (c) Home: find one flowering plant and one non-flowering plant near your home. Look, do not pick. (d) The two lizard questions: which group is a lizard in? Does a new lizard come from a seed, a spore or an egg?

**Not in these videos (later lessons):** the six animal groups (Episodes 5 and 6); useful and harmful fungi and bacteria, microscopes (pp. 34-39); protists; Linnaeus and two-part names (p. 20, "Science Around Us").
