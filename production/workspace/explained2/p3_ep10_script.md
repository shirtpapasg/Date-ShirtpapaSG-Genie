## 2. The spine and the split

EP9 asked: a metal spoon sinks, but a giant metal ship floats; what is inside the ship that helps it to float? **Part 1** is at a beach shelter at East Coast Park, where real giant ships sit on the horizon. A clear tub of water stands on the stone table. Test one: drop it in. Two blocks of the same size, wood and metal: the wood floats, the metal sinks. Two spoons: the same. Momo tries a rule ("heavy things sink, and light things float") and the child stops her: the giant ship is very heavy and it floats. The book's rule is about the material. But the ship is metal, and metal sinks, so the dare is played as Round 1. Then the ship is opened in a cut-away: big rooms, full of air, beside the solid metal block on the bottom of the tub. The child becomes that air, trapped inside the ship. The book's two sentences name it. A metal bowl proves it small: it floats, water takes the place of the air, it sinks. Then the rain comes, and the cliffhanger: a plastic raincoat and a fabric school shoe. **Part 2** is at the void deck of an HDB block in the rain. Test two: drip on it. One drop on the raincoat sits on top and rolls off; one drop on the shoe soaks in and passes through to the sock. Absorb; waterproof. The child becomes a raindrop. Four waterproof materials stand in the rain. Test three: wax it. Wax is rubbed on one of two fabric shoes, and a drop on it rolls off: the treatment changed the property. Basil's notes hold the mix-up most pupils make ("it dries fast, so it is waterproof"). The chant says the three ideas, and the dare hands over to light.

**Why the split is here:** it is the book's own page break (p. 56, then p. 57), each part has one question the child can answer with the eyes, and the two parts are two ways water meets a thing: under it (does it float?) and on it (does it get in?).

## 3. Cast, the game and the conventions

- **Heart** narrates (Kokoro af_heart, speed 0.90, about 0.5 s between sentences, 0.6 s after a new term; softer and at about 0.85 in the two journeys and in every [LOOK CLOSER], with about 0.8 to 1.0 s between sentences). Never says a wrong idea. Never reads a Mark Sentence card or a goal card.
- **Pip** (purple #8B5CF6, careful), **Basil** (teal #14B8A6, bold), **Momo** (coral #FB7158, curious): the painted sprites, always in their aura discs, in their Character Voice Kit voices about 12% slower than default. Pose swaps: squash, hard cut, stretch, hop. When a buddy reads a card, the words light up as they are read.
- **Poses that exist** (`characters/sprites/poses/`): Basil: sit (talking), cheer, oops, surprised, arms crossed (no pointing, thinking or waving pose: Basil never points in this script; when he reads the goal cards or leads a card he does an inviting hop). Pip and Momo: sit, cheer, oops, surprised, arms crossed, pointing, thinking, waving. No new pose is needed.
- **The game:** cream answer cards with big numbers; fingers 1, 2, 3; arms crossed for HOLD ON!; one finger per point on the score hand. The buddies score a point on a lock-in round when two of the three are right; nothing in HOLD ON!; nothing in a Showdown built on a buddy's mistake. **Scores:** Part 1: HOLD ON! 1 : 0, Round 1 2 : 1 (You 2, Buddies 1). Part 2: Round 1 1 : 1, Showdown 3 : 1 (You 3, Buddies 1).
- **Colours.** The two fixed answer colours: **FLOATS** and **WATERPROOF** green, **SINKS** and **NOT WATERPROOF** blue (tags and trays). No red, no buddy colour and no sun yellow is used for an answer. Water is a clear light blue everywhere; the tags sit on cream so blue tags never vanish into the water.
- **The tub** must read clearly as what it is: a clear plastic tub about as wide as the table's middle, three-quarters full, standing on the stone table with a shadow; a level water surface with a bright line; the label **OUR TUB** on its front. Things in it are seen through its side.
- **The board OUR 3 WATER TESTS:** a cream board on a low stand at the end of the table, three rows with a tick box each: **1 DROP IT IN · 2 DRIP ON IT · 3 WAX IT**. A green tick is stamped when a test is done.
- **Things do not float in the air.** The tub, the board, trays, the easel and the picture boards stand on the table or the ground, or slide low with dust puffs. An adult's hands lower things into the water and lift them out.
- **Realism:** a floating thing sits partly BELOW the water surface, never on top of it (the wooden block about half under, the wooden spoon low and level, the bowl with its rim above the water, the ship's hull with a clear waterline). A sinking thing goes straight down with a few small bubbles and rests on the bottom. The two blocks are exactly the same size and shape; the two spoons the same size and shape. The ship is a real container ship drawn from photos (a long dark hull, a red band below the waterline, a white bridge tower at the back, stacked containers), simplified and friendly; in the cut-away its inside is big empty rooms and tall spaces with metal walls, an engine room at the back, ladders and doors for scale; the air is shown by a soft pale tint and plain label lines, never as clouds. The bowl is a plain stainless-steel bowl; as water pours in it sits lower and lower, the water runs over the rim, and it goes down level and rests on the bottom. Water drops on plastic are round beads with a highlight that wobble and roll downhill, leaving the plastic dry; on fabric a drop flattens, spreads and darkens the cloth. The school shoe is a white fabric (canvas) school shoe with a rubber sole; in the magnifier its fabric is a plain weave, threads over and under. Wax is a cold, pale block; where it has been rubbed the fabric looks a little smoother and slightly shiny. Rain falls straight, in fine lines, outside the void deck only. Label lines are plain lines with no arrowheads.

## 11. Choices I made

1. **The dare is answered in Part 1,** in the book's order: float or sink is p. 56 and comes first, so EP9's question is restated word for word at 0:06, held through one short test ("Hold on to your answer!"), and played as Round 1 at about 2:00 with the same three cards (there Heart says it short: "What is inside the ship? 2. **Spine: OUR 3 WATER TESTS** (build-and-reveal): drop it in, drip on it, wax it, with a tick for each. Part 1 is water under a thing, Part 2 is water on a thing.
3. **Five [LOOK CLOSER] moments**, one for every key idea: the material matters (two blocks and two spoons in the tub), trapped air (the cut-away of the hull beside the solid metal block), trapped air tested small (the metal bowl), absorb and waterproof (the slow drop test with a magnifier on the threads), treatment (wax on one of two shoes). 4. **The metal bowl, not a foil ball.** A foil boat against a scrunched foil ball was suggested, but a loosely scrunched foil ball often floats (it traps air too), so a child who tries it at home could "disprove" the video. A stainless-steel bowl always floats empty and always sinks full, and it shows the air being replaced. The solid lump of the same metal is the metal block from test one, already lying on the bottom of the tub.
