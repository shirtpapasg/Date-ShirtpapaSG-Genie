**Topic:** the end of Primary 3 and the first taste of Primary 4 Heat (P4 Ch 6-7: heat flows from a hotter place to a colder place; good
and poor conductors of heat; trapped air), using two P3 skills (a fair test, EP24; magnets attract iron and steel but not aluminium, EP19-20).
**Big idea:** an ice-cream box in the sun gains heat from the warmer surroundings; wrapping it in wool, a poor conductor of heat full of
trapped air, slows the heat flowing in, so it stays frozen longer. Nothing cold ever "flows"; wool never "makes" heat.

## 1. The chain of questions
1. The party is in twenty minutes and it is 33 degrees. *How do we get the ice cream there still frozen?* → three fixes, you pick one.
2. Run! Pip runs out of puff and hugs the box. *Is the cold going into his tummy?* → no: heat flows out of Pip into the ice cream.
3. Fan it! It drips faster. *Isn't wind cool?* → a fan only pushes the 33 °C air along; heat flows from the hotter air into the colder ice cream (HOTTER TO COLDER).
4. Wrap it in WOOL? *Won't warm wool melt it?* → inside the wool: trapped air and wool let heat through only slowly (POOR CONDUCTOR OF HEAT); wool makes no heat; the same scarf slows heat both ways.
5. *Can we prove it?* → a fair test (FAIR TEST): change only the wrapper; the wool cup melts least (5 ml).
6. The rescue, and a drain: *will a magnet get the opener back?* → steel yes, aluminium no (P3 magnets).
7. At the party, a fan blows on the ice cream. *What does it deliver?* → heat (the callback). And next: P4 (systems, matter, light, shadows, heat).

## 2. Science checks (every picture and every sentence)
- **Heat flows from a hotter place to a colder place** (P4 Ch 7). Every heat arrow is orange-red and points from the hotter thing to the
  colder one: Pip's tummy → the box; the 33 °C air → the scoop; the hot side → through the wool → the frozen box; a child's neck → out
  through the scarf on a cold day. **Never** a "cold" arrow, never anything blue flowing.
- **"The cold is going into my tummy!"** is Pip's wrong idea only, corrected in the next line by Heart: "Nothing cold is flowing. Your
  tummy is warmer than the ice cream, so heat flows out of your tummy into the ice cream. Your tummy loses heat, and the ice cream gains heat
  and melts!" (P4 Ch 6: heat gain and heat loss).
- **The fan:** "A fan cannot make air cold. It only pushes the air along." Forced air at 33 °C over a −18 °C object brings more warm air
  past it, so heat flows in faster. (Why a fan feels nice on a sweaty face is P5 evaporation: deliberately not explained.)
- **−18 °C:** a typical shop freezer setting ("about minus eighteen degrees Celsius"). **33 °C:** a hot Singapore afternoon in the sun
  (the shop's thermometer). Pip's body: called "warmer", no number.
- **Wool:** "Wool does not make heat." Wool is a **poor conductor of heat**; so is the **air trapped** between its fibres (P4 Ch 7:
  wool, polystyrene, trapped air). An insulator **slows** heat flow; it never stops it (the journey says the heat "creeps in, very, very
  slowly"; the fair-test wool cup still melts 5 ml). The same scarf slows heat flowing OUT of a neck on a cold day and IN to the box today.
- **The fair test (P3 7.9):** only the wrapper is changed; kept the same: the same ice cream cups, the same sun, the same five minutes;
  measured: how much has melted (the melted part poured into measuring cups: 30 ml no wrapper, 20 ml thin plastic bag, 5 ml wool).
  The bag helps a little (a thin layer, little trapped air); the thick wool, full of trapped air, most. The big box waits in the freezer
  during the test (so it is not melting meanwhile).
- **Magnets (P3 6.3, 6.4):** the bottle opener is steel (magnetic); the ring pull is aluminium ("Aluminium is not a magnetic material.").
  The bar magnet is drawn as ONE piece of metal (red/blue are paint, N and S labels).
- **The covered walkway:** the shade keeps the hot sun off the box (no P5 radiation words).
- **Exam wording (the way accepted in PSLE):** "Heat flows from a hotter place to a colder place." · "Wool is a poor conductor of heat. It
  slows down the heat flowing from the warmer surroundings to the ice cream." (equivalently: "the ice cream gains less heat from the
  warmer surroundings"). Never "cold flows in", never "wool keeps the cold in", never "wool gives out heat".

## 3. The music plan (NEW music; the teacher: "New music")
- **"Scoop Parade", the P4 theme:** F major, 112 BPM, 8 bars (F | Dm7 | Bb | C7 | F | Am7 | Bb | C): electric-piano offbeats, a bouncy
  plucked bass with octave walk-ups, kick on 1 and 3, soft claps on 2 and 4, shaker eighths and a glockenspiel hook (8 bars of every 16;
  a marimba answer in the others). Under the problem, the fix cards, the picks, fixes 1 and 2. Not Kampong Walk, not the old game groove.
- **The lite bed** (teaching): the same chords on the electric piano and the bass only, a glockenspiel note every other bar: under the
  look-closer, the wool talk, the fair test and the words that earn marks.
- **The tense deadline bed:** D minor (Dm | Bb | Gm | A), a ticking clock (tick, tock), a pizzicato ostinato in eighths, a low string pad
  rising over four bars, a soft timpani each four bars: "fifteen minutes left", the covered walkway and the drain.
- **The warm party finale:** the theme with brass stabs, a string pad and bigger claps: the party, the P3 year, the P4 teaser, the
  read-along, the blooper. "Now you know!" ends on the series' happy blast (brass, cymbal, confetti), re-voiced in F.
- **The stings:** the "plan goes wrong" sad trombone (four falling brass notes, the last one wobbling) at Pip's flop and Momo's drip; the
  slow-motion replay cue (a rewind, a reversed whoosh into a deep slowed hit, a low pad, slow heartbeats); the title sting; the level-up
  fanfare; a rimshot under three of Basil's one-liners; a record scratch and a clapper for the blooper.
- **Quiet journey:** from "Be a tiny bit of air..." to the end of the pull-out only the series' micro-world pad (no theme), as the 25 Nov rule.
- **Smooth:** ONE bar clock for the whole video (every bed on the same 112 BPM grid from 0:00, so any bed resumes on the beat); every bed
  change is a 0.8 s crossfade; the music ducks under every voice; the countdown has only the ticks and the bell.
- **Every place sounds alive:** cicadas in the afternoon sun, birds, children far off and a car passing at the shop, the path and the
  walkway; the void deck's children and the fan's hum at the party; foley on every action (the dash, the flop and puffing, the fan's flaps,
  drips, the scarf's cloth, the plastic bag, pouring, the clink into the drain, the magnet's click, the standing fan's click and whirr).
