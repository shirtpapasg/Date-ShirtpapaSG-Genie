Pre-approved to go live without the teacher's check (the main session still reviews before any upload).
**Big idea (our own words):** A full-mark open-ended answer has four parts, in this order: **C**hoice (the answer the question asks you to pick, only when it asks you to pick), **E**vidence (what the question shows you), **C**oncept (the science rule you remember), **L**ink (one short sentence joining the rule back to exactly what was asked, often starting "Thus, ..."). "If you help with one thing, help with L."

## 2. The three (four) original questions and their CECL answers (the Genie's cards)
**Q1 (Part 1, magnets, a "Which ...?" question, so it has a Choice).** *Mei Ling hangs two metal bars, X and Y, from strings. She brings the North pole of a magnet near each bar. Bar X moves away from the magnet. Bar Y moves towards the magnet. Which bar is a magnet? Explain your answer.*
- **Choice:** Bar X is a magnet.
- **Evidence:** When the North pole of the magnet was brought near bar X, bar X moved away.
- **Concept:** Like poles repel. Only a magnet can be repelled by another magnet.
- **Link:** Thus, bar X was repelled, so bar X is a magnet.
- Buddies' answers: Basil "X, because it moved." (1 of 3: a Choice, but "it moved" is not the evidence that matters and there is no Concept or Link); Pip "Bar X. Like poles repel." (2 of 3: Choice and Concept, no Evidence, no Link); Momo's, built with the Genie, is the full answer (3 of 3). (Bar Y could be a magnet or just a magnetic material, so we cannot tell from attraction: said by Momo as a bonus, "Bar Y moved towards it, but a magnet attracts magnetic materials too, so that does not show Y is a magnet.")

**Q2 (Part 2, fair test, an "Explain" question: no Choice).** *Ali wants to find out if more batteries make an electromagnet stronger. Set-up A has an iron nail, ten turns of wire and one battery. Set-up B has a steel nail, twenty turns of wire and two batteries. Explain why Ali's experiment is not a fair test.*
- **Choice:** none (the question says "Explain").
- **Evidence:** Ali changed the type of nail, the number of turns of wire and the number of batteries.
- **Concept:** In a fair test, only one variable is changed. The other variables are kept the same.
- **Link:** Thus, Ali cannot be sure that the number of batteries made the electromagnet stronger.

**Q3 (Part 2, life cycles, "Do you agree?": a Choice; the Showdown).** *Siti says that a cockroach has the same life cycle as a butterfly. Do you agree? Explain.*
- **Choice:** No, I do not agree.
- **Evidence:** A cockroach has three stages: egg, nymph and adult. A butterfly has four stages: egg, larva, pupa and adult.
- **Concept:** An animal with a three-stage life cycle has no pupa stage, and its young looks like the adult.
- **Link:** Thus, the cockroach and the butterfly do not have the same life cycle.
- Basil's Showdown answer: lines 1 (Choice), 2 (Evidence), 3 (Concept) and **no Link** (the missing part).

**Q4 (Part 2, magnets, the most common mistake: no Link).** *Explain why the door of a refrigerator stays closed.*
- **Evidence:** There is a strip of magnet inside the edge of the door, and the body of the refrigerator is made of steel.
- **Concept:** Magnets attract objects made of iron and steel.
- **Link:** Thus, the magnetic strip is attracted to the steel body, and this keeps the door closed.
- Pip's answer "Magnets attract steel." (the Concept only: no Evidence, no Link). The Genie: "If you help with one thing, help with L."
