## 2. The chain of questions, the spine and the split

**The chain (each answer opens the next question; SERIES_FORMAT 1c):**
1. *EP17's dare:* a frog and a mosquito both lay eggs in water, and both have young that look nothing like the adult. Are their life cycles the same? → **Compare them properly: look for what is the same AND what is different.** (Momo: two similarities, so the same; Basil: different because of size.)
2. *Momo found two things that are the same: how can they be different?* → **Her two similarities are real. But count the stages.**
3. *How many stages?* → (be a raindrop) **The frog has three; the mosquito has four: it has a pupa, and the frog does not.** Similar because the young do not look like the adults; different because three stages and four (5.14). What we can see and count are **observable characteristics**.
4. *So the frog and the grasshopper both have three stages: are they exactly the same?* (Pip) → **No: look at the young too. A tadpole looks nothing like a frog; a nymph looks like a small grasshopper.** One same, one different (5.12).
5. *The hen is sitting on her eggs: which came first, the chicken or the egg?* → (Part 2; be an egg) **Neither: you go round and come back. The stages are repeated; there is no first or last stage.** (Basil: the egg, because we say it first.)
6. *Two animals with four stages each: can they still be different?* → **Yes: the mosquito's larva lives near the water surface, the beetle's larva on land; compare the physical features.** (Momo's homework: she missed the beetle's still pupa.)
7. *Why do life cycles matter at all?* → **The butterfly lays an egg, and the loop goes round again: life cycles let living things of the same kind continue; too few plants, too few butterflies.** (The Cycles theme closes: what makes up a cycle, how a cycle helps us predict, why cycles are important to life.)
8. *Basil's magnet holds a card on the board. Will it pull a copper wire too? Both the pin and the wire are metal...* → EP19's dare (the Interactions theme: why a magnet ignores a copper wire).

**The spine:** one place, our block's void deck beside the community garden, on LIFE CYCLE DAY. The board carries every comparison (the COMPARE FRAME with its SAME and DIFFERENT strips); the garden gives the living evidence (the pond, a flower-pot plate, the hen on her nest, the lime plant and its butterfly); the board's metal frame and Basil's magnet carry the story into the next theme.

**Why the split is here:** the book's own order: comparing by the stages and by the young (pp. 90-92), then the day-and-night story and "no first or last stage" (p. 93), Let's Apply (the mosquito and the beetle) and What I Have Learnt with the butterflies (p. 97).
