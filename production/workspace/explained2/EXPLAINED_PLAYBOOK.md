## 0. Who it is for, and the one thing to remember

- **The teacher:** a Singapore primary science teacher who owns the YouTube channel "TheOneApp" and the TheOneApp learning app.
  The series is for **children aged 9–12 (P3–P6)**, Made for Kids.
- **The one thing to remember:** EP1 is good because the teacher **watched it scene by scene and gave about 45 notes in 4 rounds**.
  Later episodes, built on autopilot with no review, kept the rules but lost the care: buddies less cute, scenes less alive,
  explanations reduced to facts. The teacher's verdict (22 Nov): *"the visuals has lesser emphasis on the cuteness of the pet
  buddies, the explanations are less in depth, and the visuals less engaging"*, and on EP11 (light): *"leaning back to the previous
  version of pushing out facts, and not focusing the perspective of young learners, requiring a combination of visual and more
  in-depth explanations"*.
- **So:** every rule below is a floor, not the goal. The goal is a child who **understands why**, laughs at a buddy, and wants the
  next episode. When a rule and that goal clash, ask the teacher.

## 1. What made Episode 1 work (copy these, not just the rules)

1. **A chain of questions as the spine.** Each answer opens the next question, so the child is pulled forward, not lectured:
   - Teddy bear and Momo look alike, yet one is living. *So looks are not the clue. Then is moving the clue?*
   - The wind-up robot moves but is non-living. *So moving is not the clue. Then is something still always non-living?*
   - The green bean sits still for two days, then grows. *So what ARE the clues?*
   - The clue card: what a thing needs and what it can do. *Can we now sort all six?*
   - Momo sorts by soft and hard (a real way to classify, but a different question). *So who sorted them properly?*
   - Pip's homework has one mistake. *Was everyone right? Open the box on Day 30.*
   - Day 30: three things changed, three did not. *Day 31: the plant is drooping. What does it need?* (leads into EP2 as a story)
2. **One real object carries the whole story**: the clear "OUR BOX" with six things and a DAY 1 → DAY 30 counter. The evidence is
   *watched happening*, never just announced.
3. **The why is shown, not stated.** The robot slows and a see-through window opens on its back to show the spring that was
   pushing it. The bean splits in a time-lapse. The plant turns to the window. Heart says what we SEE, then what it means.
4. **The buddies are characters, not decorations**: wrong for a reason a child would have, right sometimes, funny, reacting to
   everything, cute in every frame.
5. **The teacher reviewed it.** Every floating object, wrong proportion, unreadable label, silent bubble and robotic line was caught
   by watching, and fixed in one re-render.
6. **Episodes connect by story**, not by quiz: Day 31's drooping plant opens EP2.

## 2. Every note the teacher gave (the source of the rules)

### Direction (19 Nov)
- The old videos were "animated facts chunking machines throwing facts at viewers… no interaction… nor any entertainment value."
  Make it engaging for 9–12 year olds; aim for a channel that teachers worldwide praise. Doctor Binocs' two-character format is one
  formula; go better. Use **the teacher's own pet buddies** from TheOneApp. Learn from the success examples (`success_examples.md`).
- Quality of animation must be "greatly improved". Cocomelon / Pinkfong characters are admired, **but no 3D**. The approved motion
  reference is `characters/sprites/pose_test_robot_round.mp4` ("exactly what I want").

### Buddy art (19–20 Nov)
- Basil's head must not look dented; a lizard's eye sits at the far corner in a side / three-quarter view.
- Use the teacher's **own uploaded** buddy paintings, never redrawn faces.
- Signature colours: Pip purple `#8B5CF6`, Basil teal `#14B8A6`, Momo coral `#FB7158`.

### Length, pacing, layout (20 Nov)
- Slow the video 10%, then a further 5% (20 Nov): new terms like "classification" need time to digest.
- Split into Part 1 and Part 2 (about 3–5 min each). Key words top right.

### The 16 EP1 fixes (20 Nov, `ep01/NOTES_ROUND3.md`), now rules
- The experiment box must read as THE box (lid, air holes, "OUR BOX", on the ground), never a floating pane of glass.
- "Flying robot?": things never float above heads; they hop low along the ground or are carried.
- "Say it with me!" is **spoken** by a buddy, then a 0.8 s pause, then that buddy reads the card.
- "Bean sprouts", not "taugeh". "Lizard", not "cicak".
- Read today's 3 key words together before each part ends, with pauses for the class.
- Buddies always stand in their circular aura disc next to real props, or the size looks wrong.
- "Say it with us!" + 0.8 s before the chant; the chant is call-and-response with "YOUR TURN!" and 1.5 s pauses.
- Say the care for animals and plants (food, water in the juicy carrot, sunlight, moist soil).
- Every hidden animal is revealed at the end; make it cute and findable.
- Readable word strips (dark text on cream), held about 3.5 s and read aloud.
- Extend the ending by 4 s with animation running (YouTube's end screen).
- The class says "Now you know!": "One", "two", "Now you know!" exactly 1.0 s apart, ending in a happy blast, never robotic.
- "Keep all these new rules in the md file… apply them to future videos."

### After EP1 (20–21 Nov)
- Basil speaks more (at least as much as Pip and Momo).
- Add the "be the thing" journey into the micro-world (from the Explored series; reference https://youtu.be/F_oNWbfqtC0).
- Part 2's opening recap must be calm: 2–3 pictures, each held ≥ 2.5 s, labelled, no blinking montage ("no meaning to the kids").
- A **buddy** reads the goal cards and every Mark Sentence card, not Heart.
- Lock-in answer options and clue labels stay on screen through the countdown; clue labels sit on top of the ring.
- Animals are **cute AND alive** (the penguin was "not cute and not animated properly"); true shape from real photos (e.g. the
  Malayan water monitor), then simplified.
- The hidden animal changes per episode to fit the topic, and **Heart names it at the start** ("A little [animal] is hiding in this
  video. Can you spot it?") with its picture; the reveal says where it was.
- No orphan labels (a stray "YOU" tag). Props never hide behind buddy discs.
- **LOOK-CLOSER** (21 Nov): every key idea gets EP1's zoom-in-and-describe treatment (section 3.2).
- Say names right (section 7). Never delete old videos (section 6).
- (Paused 22 Nov) The pipeline ran ahead: when episode N is live, build N+1 and write the script for N+2. Now: quality before
  speed, one episode at a time, a teacher's-eye review before render, fully automatic.

### 22 Nov: the quality problem (the most recent and most important)
- Recent episodes: less cute buddies, less in-depth explanations, less engaging visuals; EP11 pushed facts again instead of the
  young learner's view. The scripts themselves were judged fine; the **visual telling and depth** fell.
- Thumbnail click-through was 1.3%; a new thumbnail style was piloted (section 5).

## 3. How to make an episode (the rules, in the order you need them)

### 3.1 Plan the spine and the chain FIRST
- Take the next row of the P3 series plan (`Plans/videos/P3-series-plan.md` in the teacher's 2nd brain repo
  `shirtpapasg/SecondBrain`; exact P3 wording in `Plans/videos/P3-definitions.md`).
- Pick ONE story spine (a 30-day box, a field trip, or a build-and-reveal) with ONE real object or place that carries the evidence.
- **Write the chain of questions** (7–9 lines, as in section 1.1): every answer opens the next question, from the hook to the
  link into the next episode. Where possible the link is the story continuing (Day 31's drooping plant), not just a quiz dare.
- **(Proposed 22 Nov, awaiting the teacher's yes)** Send the teacher the chain and the look-closer list (one screen) before writing
  the full script.

### 3.2 Explain from the learner's seat: the LOOK-CLOSER moment
Every key idea gets a look-closer, built like EP1's robot and bean. **From EP14 on (teacher, 22 Nov): fewer ideas, deeper:
two look-closers per part, each walking the child's path:**
1. **A buddy asks the child's question in a child's words** ("Why can I see the robot, but it's all blurry?").
2. **A buddy guesses wrong** the way children really think ("The glass is dirty!"). Heart never says a wrong idea.
3. **Zoom in on the real thing and show it happening**: slow push-in until it fills the frame, the rest dims, the buddies, score and
   shelf step back; a cut-away window, a magnifier, a time-lapse with a day counter and "speeded up" tag, or a before/after.
4. **The why, with a simple picture model**, in short concrete sentences ("Watch. Tiny bumps on the glass send the light every which
   way, so the picture gets mixed up."). Arrows, dots, a cut-away. True at P3 level, no P5/P6 labels (no "cells" in P3).
   Heart may explain the why in simple words and pictures beyond the book's own verbs; the book's exact wording stays for the
   Mark Sentences, definitions and key words. (EP11 showed that "book verbs only" turns into fact-pushing.)
5. **Back to the child's life** ("That's why the bathroom window is frosted: light comes in, but nobody can see in.").
6. **Hold it** 6–10 s or more with the thing's own sound, music low; then **one verdict tag** held ≥ 2.5 s; then the buddy reads the
   book's sentence (now a name for something the child already understands).
Mark these as **[LOOK CLOSER]** in the script. Never cut them for length.
**The approved look (teacher, 22 Nov: "this 'Look Closer' segment is good!"):** EP13's strap look-closer,
`reference/lookcloser_good_ep13_straps.webp`: LOOK CLOSER banner, one cream panel over the blurred set, the real object big and
doing the action, a magnifier circle linked to the exact spot showing the evidence, short labels, one green verdict pill, safety
small print. Build every look-closer to this look.

### 3.3 The "be the thing" journey (the series signature)
At least one per episode (20–40 s): the camera IS the particle / drop / light / seed; second person, present tense ("You are a drop
of water…"); a real path through the science with a short card at each stop; calm music, Heart soft; then pull back out and the
book's words name what the child just lived. True at P3 level.

### 3.4 The game (the child plays)
- Lock-ins: ONE set of choice cards — each a big picture card with its number badge beside it, the difference between the
  choices drawn big and obvious; never a picture card plus a separate number card (teacher, 22 Nov). A closed question with 2–3 choices, answered with the body (point, fingers 1/2/3); a 5–6 s ring timer that speeds up,
  a bell, "LOCKED IN!"; options, ring picture and clue labels stay on screen throughout; the buddies reveal after the viewer, each
  with a one-line reason; the wrong idea rotates; a buddy can be right for the wrong reason (no point).
- "HOLD ON!": a buddy uses a bad rule or an unfair test; the viewer crosses their arms.
- Showdown: a buddy's homework with one mistake, double points. Score hand: one finger per point; a perfect game ends in a high five.
- Hidden animal: topic-related, cute and alive, named by Heart at the start with its picture, revealed and circled at the end.
- **Hidden-animal moments are one smooth shot (teacher, 22 Nov):** from the real place, the camera pushes straight in onto the
  animal, then the badge, the YOU hand and ring, the score pill and the buddy discs pop out over it. No hard cut to a separate game
  screen; the reveal works the same way. Avoid unnecessary cuts anywhere: in the same place, move the camera instead of cutting.
- Key-word read-along before each part ends; "Say it with us!", then the class shouts "Now you know!" (1.0 s count-in, happy blast);
  about 4 s of animated outro; nothing in the bottom fifth of the frame at the end.

### 3.5 The cast and how they must look
- **Heart**: host, voice only, warm, never wrong; never reads a goal card or a Mark Sentence card.
- **Pip** (chinchilla, careful), **Basil** (lizard, bold, the children's favourite: the most lines), **Momo** (cat, curious).
- Art: the teacher's own paintings as cut-out sprites (`characters/sprites/`, `characters/sprites/poses/`). No 3D, no code-drawn
  redraws, fur never recoloured. New poses are made from the buddy's own picture, one consistent model.
- **Cuteness is the job, not a garnish.** Every buddy line gets a fresh pose or reaction (squash, hard cut, overshoot, hop, sparkle,
  never a cross-fade); idle life always (breathe, sway, lean in, blink); lip-sync on every spoken line. **(Proposed 22 Nov, awaiting
  the teacher):** buddies stay present and react in a corner during look-closers instead of disappearing; more poses (thinking,
  laughing, pointing, proud, scared).
- Always in their aura disc next to real props; never green, blue or sun-yellow.
- **The camera goes to what is being talked about (teacher, 22 Nov):** when a line names a board, chart or object, push in so it
  fills most of the frame, the speaking buddy big beside it; wide shots only briefly to set the place.
- **Approved scene (teacher, 22 Nov):** `reference/scene_good_ep13_marksentence.webp` — the reading buddy big on the left,
  the card large on the right, a small evidence recap along the bottom; the size and balance target.
- **From EP14 (trial, teacher 22 Nov):** no discs; buddies stand in the scene, at least 1.5× their EP12–EP13 size, the speaker
  about half the frame height; every scene balanced, buddies spread across the frame, never bunched in one corner.

### 3.6 Scenes that feel made with care (the 22 Nov concern)
The polish bar from `STYLE_GUIDE.md`: pause on any frame and it should work as a poster. One clear subject, three depth planes, one
key light, nothing touching the frame edge by accident, no text over a face or prop, palette colours only. Also:
- Real Singapore places (HDB flat, void deck, canteen, MRT, hawker centre, park), not a plain dimmed stage.
- Life in every scene: something small moving in the background (a fan, leaves, a bird, steam), camera never fully still (slow
  push or drift), warm light (sun through windows, evening glow, rain).
- Objects never float; things travel low or are carried; props read clearly as what they are; labels fit inside the frame.
- Animals: true shape from photos, then cute and alive (idle loop + their own action).

### 3.7 Pace and length
- Heart: Kokoro `af_heart`, speed 0.90, 0.5 s between sentences, 0.6 s after a new term (said a little slower). Buddies: Character
  Voice Kit, about 12% slower than default.
- New key word: pops large with a chime, holds 2.0 s, flies to the shelf (top right, max 3 per part).
- Mark Sentence card: "Say it with me!" (spoken) + 0.8 s, the buddy reads it, 2.5 s still after.
- Definitions on a cream strip, read aloud, 3.5 s.
- Something changes on screen every 10–15 s. Each part ≤ about 5:15; cut game chatter first, never pauses around new terms; split
  into parts rather than trim teaching.

### 3.8 Words
- **Full, human sentences (teacher, 22 Nov).** Heart and the buddies never speak in 2–3 word fragments ("No water.", "Not
  living."). Each fact, clue or verdict is a natural sentence with its reason: "The robot does not drink any water, so it is a
  non-living thing." Short reactions ("Wow!") are fine; on-screen tags may stay short.
- Exam-exact: every definition and key word is the textbook's wording (from `P3-definitions.md`). Every non-textbook sentence goes on
  a list for the teacher.
- Nothing may teach a wrong idea; wrong ideas are only a buddy's guess, corrected within seconds by evidence.
- Plain English food names; "lizard"; animals always shown cared for; safety small print where a child might copy something.

## 4. The production pipeline (what to run)

### 4.1 Tools (one-time setup on a new machine)
- Node + Playwright/Chromium (frames are HTML/SVG rendered by `render.js`), ffmpeg, Python 3 with numpy, soundfile, parselmouth.
- **Heart's voice:** Kokoro ONNX at `/home/user/heart-tts/` (`kokoro.onnx`, `voices.bin`, a `venv`). Set-up kit:
  `tools/kits/heart-voice-kit` in the SecondBrain repo. **Buddy voices:** `tools/kits/character-voice-kit` in the same repo.
- `sg_pron.py` (approved pronunciations), `progress.py` (progress bars: `python3 progress.py`).

### 4.2 Stages and who does them

| Stage 1 setup | Opus | `epNN/`: engine copied from the newest episode, `script.py`, voices (`make_voice.py`), timelines (`build_tl.py`), sound cues, placeholder scenes, `SCENES_TODO.md`, `STATE.md`, `upload.json` |

- One agent per job; never two renders at once. Usage limits reset every 5 hours: resume the same agent after a reset.

- Commit and push work often; never commit mp4/wav or files over 50 MB.

### 4.3 YouTube
- `production/youtube/explained_upload.py epNN/upload.json` (run `--check` first): uploads public + Made for Kids, sets thumbnails,
  inserts parts into the playlists right after the given video, and with `replaces` + `retire_old` takes the old video out of the
  playlists and adds a "▶ NEW VERSION" first line to its description. Video IDs are stored in `production/youtube/explained_uploaded.json`.
- The sign-in token lives only on the working machine (`.yt_token.json`, never in git); `yt.py` does the device-flow sign-in.
  Never put client secrets or tokens in a file or a chat.
- Playlists: P3 `PLb8LpPqxQI4E`, main Explained `PLPfEuT7l4KjI`, Shorts `PLO1GzAf41cwM` (Shorts go only there).
- Daily quota about 10,000 units (one upload ≈ 1,600); resets 08:00 UTC.
- **Never delete an old video** (its views count towards monetising). Take it out of the playlists and point it to the new one.
  Ask the teacher before making anything unlisted or private.
- Captions: YouTube's API scope does not allow caption upload; send the teacher the `.srt` files to add in Studio.
- After each episode is live: update the teacher's episode guide (`production/episode-guide/`, published at
  https://claude.ai/artifact/WwLKY24ztfQRKpCwJatJd4), send the links and `.srt` files, update the SecondBrain handoff
  (`Plans/videos/P3-new/HANDOFF.md`).

## 5. Thumbnails
- **New style (pilot, 22 Nov; `THUMBNAIL_V2.md`, `thumb_v2.js`)**: 0–3 big words that ASK and never answer (the teacher's own example:
  "SHIP FLOATS, HOW?"); one buddy big (about a third of the frame) with a strong expression; one close-up subject from the episode's
  art; a bold, high-contrast background; a tiny part chip, no big PART badge; readable at 320 px. EP12 onwards use it. Old-style
  rules (section 8 of `SERIES_FORMAT.md`) apply to EP1–EP11 until the teacher decides on the pilot swap.

## 6. Sound
Heart on top; a light bouncy bed ducked under speech; a calm pad for journeys and look-closers; woodblock tick that speeds up for
lock-ins, bell, "LOCKED IN!" stamp, pops on pose swaps, clap-clap-stomp for the chant, a soft chime on new key words; every action's
sound on its own timeline mark; about -16 LUFS. **A human must listen before upload**: the agents can only measure sound.

## 9. Checklist before any episode goes live
- [ ] The chain of questions holds from hook to the next episode; each answer opens the next question.
- [ ] Each key idea has a look-closer that shows the WHY from the child's seat, ends on a verdict, and links to the child's life.
- [ ] The buddies are cute in every frame, react to everything, and Basil has the most lines.
- [ ] Every scene is a real place with life in it; nothing floats; no prop hides behind a disc; no orphan labels; nothing cut off.
- [ ] Every spoken fact, clue and verdict is a full, natural sentence (no 2–3 word fragments).
- [ ] Book wording exact on cards and key words; no wrong idea said by Heart; the non-textbook list is ready.
- [ ] Hidden animal named at the start and revealed, each as one smooth push-in from the real place (no jump cut); goal cards and Mark Sentences read by buddies with "Say it with me!" + 0.8 s.
- [ ] Each part ≤ about 5:15; -16 LUFS; `.srt` with no overlaps; thumbnails readable at 320 px.
- [ ] The teacher's-eye review passed (`NOTES_REVIEW.md` all fixed); key stills sent to the teacher for information.
- [ ] Upload with `--check` first; never delete an old video; update the guide, send links and `.srt`, update the handoff.
