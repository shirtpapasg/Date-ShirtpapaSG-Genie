## 2. The chain of questions, the spine and the split

**The chain (each answer opens the next question; SERIES_FORMAT 1c):**
1. *EP20's dare:* Basil hangs his bar magnet on a string, so it can turn freely, and spins it again and again. Where does it stop: a different way every time, or the same way every time? → (Momo: a different way, like her spinning top; Pip: the same way, magnets follow rules; Basil: the same way, he tried it at home.)
2. *Be the hanging magnet:* → **you are spun, you slow, something you cannot see turns you gently back, and you stop facing the rain tree; spun again, the rain tree again.** Answer: 2, the same way every time. Basil marks the line with two cones.
3. *Why that way? Is it the steel goalpost pulling it round?* (Pip) → **Hang it far from the goalpost, hang it in the classroom, float it on a lid in a basin of water: it comes to rest along the same line every time. The line runs North and South. A freely suspended magnet comes to rest in the North-South direction.**
4. *My string got tangled, so I laid it on the bench. Nobody is holding it, so it is free?* (Basil, HOLD ON!) → **The bench rubs on it and stops it turning. Hang it up and it turns freely again: free to turn, not just "nobody holding it".**
5. *But how does the magnet know which way is North? Nothing is touching it!* (Momo) → *A giant magnet hiding somewhere?* (Basil, half right) → **Fly up until you see the whole Earth: magnets all over the world come to rest North–South. Earth behaves like a magnet, with its own North and South poles; it interacts with a freely suspended magnet.**
6. *Momo's hiking compass: its needle lines up with Basil's cones too. Is the needle a magnet?* (Part 1's cliffhanger) → Part 2, first as a game: **bring a bar magnet close, and the needle turns towards or away from it** (be the compass needle). **The needle is a small magnet that can turn freely.**
7. *If I turn the compass round, does the needle turn round with it?* (Basil) → **No: the letters turn, the needle stays North–South. Turn the compass until the N sits under the needle's red end. A compass can help us to find the direction.**
8. *Momo's checklist: keep Basil's magnet next to the compass to help it?* (Showdown) → **No: a magnet near a compass attracts and repels the needle. Keep magnets and phones away.** *But will a compass work at night, when we cannot see the Sun?* (Pip) → **Yes: on a ship in the Singapore Strait at night, and in fog far away in the United States. A freely suspended magnet can be used to tell the direction.**
9. *Pip strokes an iron nail with Basil's magnet, again and again, always the same way. Then he holds it near steel paper clips. What happens?* → EP22's dare (the stroke method).

## 11. Choices I made

1. **The dare is answered by being the magnet:** the child is spun twice and stops facing the same rain tree both times, and feels "something you cannot see" turn them back, which is the question look-closer 2 answers.
2. **The North-South line comes from a fair test children can do** (the field far from the goalpost, the classroom, a basin of water; lab 6.4 Parts (a) and (b)) against Pip's real misconception (magnets attract steel, so the goalpost turns it). The Earth gets place words only (*North*, *South*), no colours, no magnet inside, and no line says which of its poles pulls which end (the teacher's caution; "Facts to check" 1).

6. **The brief's quiz becomes Round 1 of Part 2,** and the journey answers it from inside the compass: the needle's red end is pushed away, then pulled in (EP20's push and pull, so the needle is shown to be a magnet). The giant hand is an adult's, because all three buddies ride along.

8. **The Showdown is a safety idea:** "keep a magnet next to the compass to help it" sounds sensible to a child who has just learnt the needle is a magnet; the fix says "keep magnets and phones away", as the brief and the task ask.
9. **Night and fog, Singapore and the United States** in one look-closer: the brief's Singapore anchor (ships in the Singapore Strait) and US bridge (a hiker in a national park), both spoken, and the book's "Hence... tell the direction" as the Mark Sentence.

12. **The floating toy globe (the brief's p. 112 opener) is left out:** real floating globes use an electromagnet and a sensor, so any picture of it risks a wrong idea; EP20 already showed magnets floating with a push.
