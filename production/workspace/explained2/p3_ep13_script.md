## 2. The spine and the split

EP12 asked: a school bag; its strap must bend, and it must not tear; which material would you choose: fabric, metal or wood? **Part 1** is in a Primary 3 classroom after school. A school bag lies on the teacher's table, one strap torn off. The dare is played as Round 1, and then three straps are looked at closely over the back of a chair: the fabric strap bends round it and lifts a bag full of books without tearing; the metal bar and the wooden stick stay straight. We did not guess: we checked the properties, and that is what engineers do. The book's words: knowing the physical properties of a material is important when designing and making new products. Engineers follow a plan, and the board OUR DESIGN PLAN is filled for the whole bag: what it is for (to carry books to school), its parts (straps, body, zip, base), what each part must do (its intended use). Pip jumps to a rule ("every part of fabric!") and the child stops him; Basil knows why: his bag sits on a wet canteen floor. So the design is drawn, and a material is chosen for each part, each for a property we watch: a plastic base (a drop of water stays on top: waterproof), a metal zip (open, close, no break: strong), fabric straps and a fabric body (flexible and strong). The book's words: the materials are selected for the different functions of the parts based on their physical properties. The child becomes a book inside the bag and lives all three. Then Basil wants to make a thousand bags, and Heart stops him: will the new strap really hold? **Part 2** is in the school's eco-garden. The old strap held five weights, then it tore; the new strap, the same length and width, holds nine: it is stronger, and now we know. After designing, engineers test their product. The child becomes the new strap. Then the board is wiped and used again, for the book's own task: a home for the goats at the zoo. It must keep the goats safe and let visitors see them: a wooden fence is strong, and we see the goats between its rails. The book's summary sentence: choose a material whose physical property is suitable for the intended use of the object. Basil's zoo map holds the theme's exam trap: a dragonfly among the birds, because it has wings. Six groups, twelve animals: a great variety. Why is it important to maintain diversity? The book's own sentence from Chapter 1 answers it: living things that are not able to survive will become extinct. The chant says the three verbs, and the dare hands over to the Sun.

**Why the split is here:** Part 1 is the whole of p. 66, the plan row's three goals, with the dare answered and one object designed from the first box to the last. Part 2 is the two things the plan row's big idea adds after the semicolons: the test ("after designing, engineers test their product") and the Theme Closure (the same plan used for a zoo home; why the great variety matters). Each part has its own question and its own evidence.
