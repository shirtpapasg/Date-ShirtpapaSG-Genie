## 2. The spine and the split

EP11 asked: a supermarket trolley; which material would you choose to make it: glass, metal or fabric? **Part 1** is on the covered walkway in front of a neighbourhood supermarket, at the trolley bay. Before anyone answers, Heart asks what a trolley is for, and we watch one do its job: rice, bottles and tins go in, and it rolls. That is its purpose, and the book's words say that objects are things you use for different purposes. Then the dare is played as Round 1. The loaded metal trolley is looked at closely: the bars at the bottom stay straight, and a hard knock in the trolley bay leaves no crack. Metal is strong. Glass is fragile (the book's own sentence, with the cup from EP9). Fabric is flexible (Pip's own shopping bag holds the rice, but folds flat). The book's words: the physical properties of a material allow it to be used in certain ways. Momo jumps to a rule ("glass is a bad material!") and the child stops her; Basil knows why: the fish tank in the aquarium shop next door is glass. We can see every fish clearly, and the shelf under it is dry: transparent and waterproof, the right material for that use. The child becomes a metal bar of the trolley. Then three water bottles turn up in the trolley: plastic, glass, metal. **Part 2** is in the car park beside the block. A bottle for school must not break when dropped, and you want to see the water inside: all three are waterproof, two let you see the water, and only one of those does not break easily when dropped. The choice depends on the use. Then the shopping goes into a parked car, and the car is looked at part by part: rubber tyres, fabric seats, a glass windscreen, a metal body. Four parts, four materials, each chosen for the job of its part. The child becomes a drop of water and meets all four. Momo's notes hold the exam trap: "because glass is made from sand" is true, but it is not a property. The chant says the three steps, and the dare hands over to the school bag.

**Why the split is here:** Part 1 is "one object, one material, and why" (the plan row's first two goals), with the trolley answered and the same glass shown to be wrong for one use and right for another. Part 2 is the two extensions the book adds: one object can be made of different materials (the bottle), and one object can use many materials (the car, the plan row's third goal). Each part has its own question and its own evidence.

**The two-step answer for "Why is X made of Y?":** 1. Name the property ("Plastic is waterproof,"). 2. Link it to the job ("so rain cannot pass through and you stay dry.").

**Also in the book, not spoken in the video:** the ceramic floor tiles (the book calls them strong: they do not break unless hit by a heavy object; elsewhere it calls ceramic fragile) and the wooden furniture and metal trolley, which p. 60 calls "rigid" in the sense of "does not bend under a load", while p. 55 defines rigid materials as those that "break easily when bent" (EP9). **Careful:** the answer to "why this material?" is always a property linked to the job, never where the material comes from and never "because it is nice". A material is not good or bad: it is suitable or not suitable for a use (glass: not for a trolley, right for a fish tank). An object's job can need more than one property (the fish tank: transparent AND waterproof; the school bottle: see the water AND not break). The book's reason for the glass windscreen is that it keeps out wind and rain (waterproof); the video's chips add transparent, and Pip's spoken answer is "Glass is waterproof!".
