# TheOneApp, Explained (revamped): the series format and rules

The teacher's rulebook for every new "TheOneApp, Explained" episode, P3 to P6, from 20 Nov 2026.
P3 EP1 ("We Put 6 Things in a Box for 30 Days. Which Ones Are ALIVE?") is the reference episode.
Its files are in `ep01/`; its notes rounds are in `ep01/NOTES_ROUND3.md`.

Also read:
- `STYLE_GUIDE.md`: the approved look.
- `BRIEF.md`: background and decisions.
- `success_examples.md`: the teacher's 8 model videos.
- `production/THEONEAPP_EXPLORED.md`: the sister series' rules 1 to 10.

---

## 1. What the series is

- One textbook topic per episode, told as a game the child plays at home or in class.
- The child is a player:
  - locks in an answer with their hands,
  - keeps score on their other hand,
  - plays "You vs the Buddies".
- Each episode has one story spine taken from the success examples. Pick ONE per episode, never three:
  - a "30-day box" (Dr. Plants),
  - a field trip (Sesame Street),
  - a build-and-reveal (Aquarium Info).
- **BE THE THING: the first-person journey into the micro-world (the teacher, 20 Nov: "I like this video's way of taking
  perspective of the particle / energy / material, moving into the microworld. This concept is new and I did not see it anywhere.")**
  Reference: TheOneApp, Explored EP9, "Mosquitoes VS Mosquitoes!" (https://youtu.be/F_oNWbfqtC0), plus Explored EP1 (a smoke
  particle), EP4 (a water molecule), EP5 (light energy), EP7 (oxygen) and EP10 (a nutrient).
  - The camera IS the particle, the energy or the material: the viewer becomes the water molecule, the bit of food, the oxygen,
    the light, the seed, the current. "You are a drop of water. You are in the wet soil…" Second person, present tense.
  - The world rushes past at that tiny scale: near things big and fast, far things small and slow; the familiar place (the window
    ledge, the canteen) seen from inside or below; a small "speeded up" or scale tag when time or size is changed.
  - It follows a real path through the science, step by step (soil → root → stem → leaf), with simple true words and a short card
    at each stop; then the camera pulls back out to normal size and the textbook words name what the viewer just lived through.
  - Every episode has at least one such journey (it is the series' signature, not an extra). Typically 20 to 40 s; it may be the
    spine of a part. Calm music, Heart's voice soft, room to breathe (the quiet-wonder look of Journey to the Microcosmos), so the
    loud game beats around it land harder.
  - True at the level taught: no P5/P6 labels in P3 (no "cells", "chloroplast" in P3). Nothing may teach a wrong idea.
  - **The viewer and the buddies come along (teacher, 2026-11-22: "I love these shrink-down scenes! Can we bring the 3 pet buddies
    along? Or even include the audience, like what you did for 'A frog, a bird and a fish'").** Each journey shows a "You: a …" tag
    and the viewer's own presence in first person (as EP5's "You: a frog" with the viewer's frog hands,
    `reference/journey_good_ep05_you_a_frog.webp`), and the three buddies shrunk down riding along, cute and alive, reacting at each
    stop, never covering what is shown. Approved journey look: `reference/journey_good_ep13_insidebag.webp`. (This replaces "the
    buddies are in neither journey".)
- Real Singapore places always: HDB flat, void deck, community garden, canteen, MRT, Kranji farm.
- Exam-exact: every definition and key word is quoted word for word from the textbook (the P3 to P6 plan's `definitions` files).
  - Every non-textbook sentence goes on an approval list for the teacher before rendering.
- Nothing may teach a wrong idea:
  - Wrong ideas are only ever a buddy's guess.
  - They are corrected within seconds.
  - Heart never says them.
- Animals are always shown cared for: food, water, air holes, returned home.

## 1b. The theme gets a "look closer" moment (teacher, 2026-11-21)

The teacher: "future episodes [must] give a stronger emphasis to the theme shown in the video. Like how episode 1 shows the zoom-in
shot and description of the wind-up robot, the zoom-in seed growth, the plant responding to changes. This zoomed-in and extra
emphasis… seems diluted in the other episodes."

Every key idea of an episode gets at least one **LOOK-CLOSER** moment, built like EP1's (see `pilot_p3_ep01_script.md`, Round 1:
the robot slows, a see-through window opens on its back to show the loose spring; the time-lapse of the bean splitting and growing;
the plant turning to the window):
1. **Zoom in on the real thing.** The camera pushes in slowly until the object or living thing fills most of the frame; the rest
   dims or blurs. The buddies, the score and the shelf step back or go quiet.
2. **Show the evidence happening,** not just its name: a cut-away window into it (the spring in the robot), a magnifier, a slow
   time-lapse with a day counter and a "speeded up" tag, a before/after side by side. Small arrows or plain label lines point at the
   exact part ("6 legs", "dry scales", "spores here").
3. **Heart describes what we SEE, slowly,** in short concrete sentences ("Watch. The robot stops. A spring inside was pushing it."),
   then says what it means in the book's words.
4. **Hold it.** At least 6–10 s on the close-up, with the soft sound of the thing itself (ticks, crunch, drip) and the music low.
5. **One clear verdict** at the end of the moment (a tag lands: NON-LIVING, FUNGI, GROWS…), then pull back out.
Scripts mark these moments as **[LOOK CLOSER]** in the "On screen" column; each key word / goal of the episode has one. They are
separate from (and in addition to) the "BE THE THING" journey, and they are never cut for length.

**Approved example (teacher, 2026-11-22: "this 'Look Closer' segment is good!"):** EP13 Part 1's strap look-closer
(`reference/lookcloser_good_ep13_straps.webp`, code in `ep13/`). Copy what works: the LOOK CLOSER banner on top; one clean cream
panel over the softly blurred set; the real object big and doing the action (an adult's hand lifts the bag full of books by the
fabric strap); a magnifier circle linked by a plain line to the exact spot, showing the evidence up close (the stitched seam);
short plain labels ("a bag full of books"); one green verdict pill ("no tear"); the safety small print in a corner
("We do not force it.").

**Use the whole panel, and let a buddy react (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP13's bag-parts look-closer: "a lot of wasted space, when the
main focus is 'Our Design Plan' which now is so small… the 'base', 'zip' and 'fabric' segment can be bigger, higher emphasis. Also,
why don't you insert one of the pet buddies in, focusing on its face, looking shocked / agreeing").** This updates step 1 above
(the buddies no longer leave completely):
- The thing explained fills the panel; no big empty cream areas. A board being filled is LARGE beside the object (about half the
  panel), never a thumbnail in a corner. Each part or label being named gets its own big moment: push in on that part, big label.
- A buddy's FACE peeks in from a lower corner of the panel (a close-up, about a quarter to a third of the panel's height), reacting
  live on the beats: eyes wide / gasp at the reveal, nod and smile when agreeing, a thinking face while it happens.
- Never semi-transparent "ghost" overlaps of two objects. Bad examples: `reference/lookcloser_bad_ep13_bag_*`.

## 1c. Explain from the learner's seat (teacher, 2026-11-22; from EP14 on)

The teacher, on EP11: "leaning back to the previous version of pushing out facts, and not focusing the perspective of young learners,
requiring a combination of visual and more in-depth explanations". And on recent episodes: "the visuals has lesser emphasis on the
cuteness of the pet buddies, the explanations are less in depth, and the visuals less engaging." EP13 was released as it was; from
EP14 on every script is written this way, by Opus:
- **The chain of questions comes first** (as `pilot_p3_ep01_script.md` "How the segments chain"): 7–9 lines, each answer opening the
  next question, from the hook to the link into the next episode (a story carrying on where possible, not only a quiz dare).
- **Fewer ideas, deeper: two LOOK-CLOSERs per part**, each walking the child's path: a buddy asks the child's question in a child's
  words → a buddy guesses wrong as children really do → we see it happen up close → the WHY with a simple picture model (arrows,
  dots, cut-aways), true at the level taught → back to the child's life → verdict → the buddy reads the book's sentence.
- **Heart may explain the why in simple words beyond the book's own verbs**; exact book wording stays for Mark Sentences,
  definitions and key words.
- The buddies stay cute and alive in every frame and react to what we see; scenes are real places with life in them.
- Full guide for any agent or account: `EXPLAINED_PLAYBOOK.md`.

## 1d. Trial from EP14: big buddies in the world, and buddies who teach (teacher, 2026-11-22)

The teacher: "I want to try a new episode with the pet buddies not having the colour circular background. Let them blend in. Also,
make them bigger so the cute pet buddies can create a more relatable connection with the viewer. Focus more on their dialogue and how
they explain / teach scientific terms." From EP14 (a trial; if the teacher likes it, it becomes the rule):
- **No aura discs.** The buddies stand IN the scene: on the floor, the table, the window ledge, with a soft contact shadow and the
  scene's light on them (a warm rim light at dawn, cooler at night). This replaces "buddies always sit in their circular aura disc"
  (section 5) for the trial. Signature colours stay on their name tags, speech-bubble borders and pick cards.
- **Bigger.** A speaking buddy is about 40–55% of the frame height (two-shot / close-up framing like a children's show), not small
  in a corner. Props are scaled to sit naturally beside them; the camera frames the buddy who talks, and moves (never jump-cuts)
  between them.
- **50% more space, balanced layout (teacher, 2026-11-22: "I want the audience to connect more to the pet buddy characters. Make
  the characters bigger and occupy 50% more space. Ensure you consider the layout of this scene, not too clustered at one side").**
  Every buddy is at least 1.5× the size used in EP12–EP13. Each scene is composed so the buddies are spread across the frame (one
  left and one right, or a large speaker in the foreground with the others on the other side), never bunched in one corner; cards,
  props and look-closer panels sit in the space between them. Scene plans give each buddy's position and size.
  **Approved scene (teacher, 2026-11-22: "this is a good example of a good scene"):** EP13's Mark Sentence 1
  (`reference/scene_good_ep13_marksentence.webp`): the reading buddy BIG on the left (about half the frame height), the card large on
  the right, a small visual recap of the evidence along the bottom (the three straps with SUITABLE / NOT FOR THIS JOB and the
  property chips), the key-word pill under the score. Balanced left/right, nothing bunched, nothing cut off. Use this as the size
  and balance target for buddies.
- **The camera goes to what is being talked about (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP13's "OUR DESIGN PLAN" scene: "when talking about
  'Our design plan', you need to zoom into the plan, showing a bigger image of it. The 3 pet buddies are too small to make any
  impact in this scene").** Whenever a line names or explains a board, chart, card or object, the camera smoothly pushes in so that
  thing fills most of the frame while it is talked about, then eases back; the speaking buddy stays big beside it. A wide shot of a
  place is only a brief establishing moment. Bad example: `reference/scene_bad_ep13_designplan_toosmall.webp`.
- **Things stay where they were (teacher, 2026-11-22: EP13's OUR DESIGN PLAN sat top-left in the look-closer, then jumped to the
  bottom in the next scene, "shifted its position unnecessarily when it can just remain at the top left as before").** A recurring
  board, chart or prop keeps one home position and size across consecutive scenes; if it must move, it glides there in view, never
  jumps across a cut. Bad example: `reference/continuity_bad_ep13_designplan_jumps.webp`.
- **Buddies teach.** Each scientific term is explained by a buddy in their own words, in a short back-and-forth with another buddy or
  the viewer ("Rotation means the Earth spins round and round, like my globe!"), with a picture beside them; Heart hosts, sets up
  the question and confirms, but the buddies carry the explaining. Full, natural sentences (section 5 "Talk like people").

## 2. Length and parts

- **Each video is about 3 to 5 minutes.** A topic that needs more is split into **Part 1 and Part 2**.
- Choose the split point so each part ends on a question or cliffhanger and each part has its own goals.
- **Part 1** ends with:
  - a tease of Part 2 ("Find out in Part 2!"),
  - its own score,
  - its own key-word recap.
- **Part 2** opens with a calm, focused recap (teacher, 2026-11-21, on EP5 Part 2 0:00–0:04: "the blinking around is too quick and
  i see no meaning to the kids… too fast with no focus"):
  - NO fast flashing montage. At most 2 or 3 pictures from Part 1, each held at least 2.5 s, one at a time, centred and large,
    with a gentle push-in (no blinking, flicker or fast cuts);
  - each picture has one short label that says what it means (e.g. "The frog: moist skin");
  - Heart speaks over them: the "Last time…" line, TRUE to what Part 1 showed, so every picture has a meaning;
  - about 8–10 s in all, then the new question of Part 2.
- Each part keeps its own score ("You vs the Buddies").

## 3. Pacing (the teacher: "too fast… no time for students to digest")

**Slowed a further 5% (teacher, 2026-11-20, from EP6 on): "consider slowing down the pacing of the video by another 5%, especially areas where new terms are introduced."**
- **Heart:**
  - Kokoro af_heart at about speed 0.90 (was 0.95).
  - About 0.5 s between sentences (was 0.45 s).
- **Buddies:**
  - Each speaks in their Character Voice Kit voice.
  - About 12% slower than default (was 7%).
- **New key word:** when a key word is said for the first time, it pops LARGE with a chime and holds about 2.0 s (was 1.5 s), then
  flies to the key-word shelf. Heart pauses about 0.6 s after saying a new term before the next sentence, and the new term is said
  slowly and clearly (a little slower than the rest of the line).
- **Mark Sentence cards:** hold about 2.5 s of stillness after the buddy finishes reading (was 2 s).
- **Definitions** (e.g. "similarities: characteristics that things have in common"):
  - on a light, readable strip (dark text on cream),
  - read aloud,
  - on screen about 3.5 s.
- **Length:** the slower pace makes parts longer. Keep each part at most about 5:15; if it runs over, cut non-teaching lines (game
  chatter, extra examples) first, never the pauses around new terms.
- Something changes on screen every 10 to 15 s.
- Do not trim teaching to hit a length. Split into parts instead.

## 4. Interaction rules (the viewer must have time to react)

- **"Say it with me / us!" is always SPOKEN, never just a bubble.**
  - The buddy next to the card (or all three) says it aloud with lip-sync and an inviting gesture.
  - Then a **0.8 s pause**.
  - Then THAT BUDDY reads the card (not Heart), so children say it together with the buddy.
  - This applies to every Mark Sentence card and every chant.
- **The chant is call-and-response:**
  1. A buddy or all three say "Say it with us!", then pause 0.8 s.
  2. The buddies chant one line on the clap-clap-stomp beat.
  3. A **"YOUR TURN!"** tag pops by the YOU hands.
  4. A pause of about **1.5 s** lets the class repeat the line, with the beat continuing softly.
  5. Repeat for every line, then optionally once all together.
- **Lock-ins:**
  - **Everything the child needs to choose stays on screen through the whole countdown (teacher, 2026-11-21):** the answer cards,
    the picture in the ring AND its clue labels (e.g. "fins", "scales", "gills"). Nothing that was shown with the question is
    taken away while the ring drains; it helps the child decide.
  - **Clue labels sit ON TOP of the ring's picture frame**, fully visible and readable, never tucked behind or clipped by the
    yellow ring (draw them above the frame).
  - A closed question with 2 or 3 choices.
  - **The countdown takes the stage (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP13's HOLD ON!: "this bag with the countdown is so small, I can barely
    see the 3 descriptive words. How do you want the young viewers to know what to focus on?").** During every lock-in, HOLD ON! and
    Showdown countdown the ring and its picture are big and central (about half the frame height); clue labels are large and
    readable on a phone (cap height at least ~40 px at 1080p, high contrast); the background clears to the blue game stage or dims
    strongly; only the question's elements stay on screen (boards and other props leave; at most one buddy, big, reacting at the
    side). Bad example: `reference/countdown_bad_ep13_holdon_toosmall.webp`.
  - **One set of choice cards, big and numbered (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP13's dare round: "the option 1 2 and 3 is a waste of
    space, repeating the textbox above… Put the number 1, 2 and 3 right beside the options above, make the textbox bigger showing a
    noticeable difference among the 3 options").** Never show a picture card AND a separate number card for the same choice. Each
    choice is ONE large picture card with its number badge (1, 2, 3) right beside or on it; the picture makes the difference between
    the choices obvious at a glance (draw the thing that differs big: the fabric strap, the metal bar, the wooden stick), with a short
    label. Verdict tags (SUITABLE / NOT FOR THIS JOB) sit on those same cards. Bad example: `reference/lockin_cards_duplicated_ep13_BAD.webp`.
  - Answered with the body: point left or right, or fingers 1, 2, 3.
  - A ring timer of 5 to 6 s, ticking that speeds up, a bell, then "LOCKED IN!".
  - The buddies reveal AFTER the viewer, each with a one-line reason.
  - The wrong idea rotates between buddies.
  - A buddy can be right for the wrong reason and gets no point.
  - LIVING = green, NON-LIVING = blue (or the topic's two choices in two fixed colours).
- **"HOLD ON!"** (not "FOUL!"): a buddy uses a bad rule or an unfair test, and the viewer crosses their arms.
- **Showdown:** a buddy's homework with one mistake to spot, for double points.
- **Score hand:** one finger per point. A perfect game ends in a high five.
- **The hidden animal (teacher, 2026-11-21: "swap the hidden animal and not always the lizard. Use the related animal appearing
  in the video. The ending question shouldn't matter when the hidden animal changes"):**
  - Each part hides a small, cute animal that BELONGS to that video's topic or setting (e.g. a frog among the lily pads in an
    amphibian episode, an ant on a stall in a canteen, a snail on the damp kitchen wall), not always the lizard.
  - Drawn to the "cute AND alive" rule, small but findable, in the same place for a few scenes.
  - **Heart NAMES the animal at the start** (teacher, 2026-11-21: "Heart no longer shares what animal to find in the video… How will
    the viewer know what to look out for"): "A little [grasshopper] is hiding in this video. Can you spot it?", with a small picture
    of it on screen for a moment, so children know what to look for. The reveal at the end: "Did you spot the [grasshopper]? It was
    [where]! … Tell someone at home." The reveal circles it, as before. Nothing else in the episode depends on which animal it is.
  - Say "lizard", never "cicak", when it is a lizard.
  - **The hidden-animal moments are one smooth shot (teacher, 2026-11-22): "the transition to be from [the real scene], directly
    zoom into the [animal], then the other objects to pop out… more smooth and not disjoint, having no unnecessary cutting of
    scene."** Never hard-cut from the real place to a separate game screen. Stay in the real place, push the camera in smoothly
    (about 1–1.5 s, ease in-out) straight onto the animal while the rest dims or blurs behind; then the game objects pop out over
    it one after another (the name badge by the real animal, the YOU hand and ring, "You lock in first", the score pill, the buddy
    discs). The reveal at the end works the same way: back in (or smoothly into) the real place, push in on the animal, ring it.
    In general, avoid unnecessary cuts: when the next beat is in the same place, move the camera instead of cutting.
    **Update (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP12 v2):** after the smooth push-in lands, the animal sits in a CIRCLE with a thick BLACK
    RING, and everything outside the circle turns into the blue-violet game layout (the starry stage with the soft spotlight beam)
    instead of a dimmed grey scene; then the game objects pop over it. The reveal uses the same circle and ring.
  - **The hidden animal moves (teacher, 2026-11-22: in EP12 and EP13 it "stays at the same spot… makes things easy to guess and
    lost the fun, giving a sloppy vibe").** It hides in a different, fair place in each scene where it could really be (another
    branch, a ledge, a leaf, half behind a curtain), small but findable; scripts and SCENES_TODO list its spot per scene.
  - **The reveal ends with a surprising fun fact (teacher, 2026-11-22: "instead of finding this animal and tell someone at home
    (about what?)… It must be a fun fact which most children are shocked to find out! Then it makes sense to share. Just finding an
    animal that is not related to the episode is sloppy script writing").** The hidden animal is chosen for a link to the episode's
    science, and the reveal goes: "Did you spot the [animal]? It was [where]!" → a buddy, excited, gives one short TRUE surprising
    fact about that animal that links to the episode's idea (with a small picture) → "Tell someone at home!". Fact-check it.
    `reference/reveal_bad_ep12_no_funfact.webp`.
  - **Order in the game segment (teacher, 2026-11-22):** talk about finding the hidden animal FIRST (Heart names it, a line or two
    that makes the search fun, "keep your eyes open"), and only then explain the fingers / lock-in and the score.
- **Key-word read-along before the end of every part:**
  1. The shelf moves to the centre and grows.
  2. Heart: "Let's read today's key words together! Ready?"
  3. Each word lights up as Heart says it, with about 1.2 s for the class to say it. The buddies mouth along.
  4. Heart: "Well done!"
- **The class says "Now you know!" at the very end of every part:**
  - The count-in "One!", "two!", "Now you know!" is evenly spaced: each starts exactly 1.0 s after the last.
  - "Now you know!" ends with a happy BLAST, never robotic: Heart's brightest take, pitched up and rising on "KNOW!", the buddies shouting in unison as a happy crowd, and a big sting on "know!" (brass hit, cymbal, confetti pop, "yay!"), with the title slam and a confetti burst on the same frame.
  1. "Say it with us!", then a 0.8 s pause.
  2. "Ready? One, two…"
  3. Heart and all three buddies shout "Now you know!" together, excited, with the title slamming in and the YOU hands joining.
- **The outro runs about 4 more seconds with the animation still going:**
  - The sunburst turns, the buddies bounce, the music fades.
  - Nothing new is spoken.
  - No text in the bottom fifth of the frame, where YouTube's overlays sit.

## 5. The cast

- **Heart:** host and narrator, voice only. Warm and fair, never wrong.
- **Pip (chinchilla), Basil (lizard), Momo (cat):** the teacher's own painted buddies, the same pets as in TheOneApp.
  - Pip is careful, Basil is bold, Momo is curious.
  - **Basil speaks more (teacher, 2026-11-20: "do give basil more speaking time").** He is the children's favourite voice: give him
    at least as many lines as Pip and Momo in every part, not only the wrong ideas. He leads some Mark Sentence cards and
    "Say it with me!" moments, explains things he gets right, cracks the jokes, and reacts on screen (his bold guesses are right
    about half the time).
  - They are good sports, and nobody is ever made to feel silly.
- **Talk like people, in full sentences (teacher, 2026-11-22).** Heart and the buddies must sound more human-like. Avoid 2–3 word
  fragments such as "No water." or "Not living." Say the whole thought as a natural sentence with its reason, e.g. "The robot does
  not drink any water, so it is a non-living thing." Short exclamations are fine as reactions ("Wow!", "Oh no!"), but every fact,
  clue or verdict is spoken as a full sentence that says what we saw and why it matters. On-screen tags and verdict labels may stay
  short; the spoken line beside them is a sentence.
- **Art:**
  - The buddies are the teacher's own paintings, animated as cut-out sprites: `characters/sprites/` and `characters/sprites/poses/`.
  - No 3D. No code-drawn redraws of the buddies.
  - Fur stays natural, never recoloured.
- **Signature colours** (the same in TheOneApp, free and credit buddies):
  - Pip: purple #8B5CF6.
  - Basil: teal #14B8A6.
  - Momo: coral #FB7158.
  - No buddy may be green or blue (those are the answer colours) or sun-yellow #FFC83A (that is "YOU").
  - The colours are used on each buddy's aura disc, speech-bubble border and tail, name tag and pick card.
  - Bubble fill stays cream.
- **Buddies always sit in their circular aura disc** when shown next to real props (pots, boxes, plants, benches). They are presenters, not tiny animals in the set, so scale never looks wrong.
- **New poses:**
  - Make each from the buddy's own picture, one buddy per image, 1024 px, white background, no shadow.
  - Make talking/blink states by editing only the face.
  - Keep each buddy one consistent model.

## 6. Motion (reference: `characters/sprites/pose_test_robot_round.mp4`, approved "exactly what I want")

- **Pose swaps:**
  - 3 frames of squash,
  - a hard cut to the new pose stretched tall,
  - overshoot, a small hop, sparkles and dust.
  - NEVER cross-fade.
- **Idle life:** breathing, a slight sway, leaning towards the action, blinks.
- **Talking:**
  - lip-sync on the talking sprites;
  - a small drawn mouth plus a syllable bounce on one-face poses.
- **Human hands have four fingers and a thumb (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP13's drawing look-closer: "this is good. But please, human
  has 4 fingers and a thumb").** Every human hand (drawing hand, adult hands, gloved hands, the YOU hands) is drawn with four fingers
  and a thumb in natural proportions. `reference/lookcloser_good_ep13_drawing_but_3fingers.webp` (good scene, wrong hand).
- **Every sample, tile or picture is named on screen (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP13's material samples: "missing information
  'plastic' on the darker material and 'fabric' for the orange material").** When a material sample, tile or picture is shown, its
  name appears with it in a readable label. `reference/labels_missing_ep13_samples.png`.
- **Every tag points to what it describes (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP13's design verdict: "poor display of visual information…
  Which is flexible, strong and waterproof? Where's the arrows, or you can even consider colour coding").** Property chips and tags
  never float free: each sits beside the part it belongs to, joined by an arrow or line, and parts are colour-coded (the part, its
  label and its chips share one colour). `reference/chips_bad_ep13_unlinked_properties.webp`.
- **Feel the big moments (teacher, 2026-11-22, on EP12's "a hard knock": "this does not give me the 'hard knock' vibe. You can
  actually make use of Pip, Momo or Basil, showing an angry / shocked face!").** Every impact or surprise beat is felt: a quick camera
  jolt, an impact flash and burst timed to the sound, and a close-up of a buddy's face reacting big (shocked, wincing, grumpy), then
  the follow-up feeling (relief, a cheer). A label alone is never enough. `reference/impact_bad_ep12_hardknock_flat.webp`.
- **Objects never float or fly.**
  - Things travel low along the ground in small hops with dust puffs, or are carried or slid by the buddies.
  - Nothing hangs above the buddies' heads.
  - Objects keep their normal size.
- **Props must read clearly as what they are.** E.g. the experiment box is a clear glass box with a lid, air holes and an "OUR BOX" label, on the ground.
- **Every label and small print fits fully inside the frame.** Label lines never cross other objects.
- Frames are a pure function of t. Render in 10 s chunks so restarts lose little.

- **Animals are cute AND alive (teacher, 2026-11-21, on EP5's penguin: "not cute and not animated properly").** Every animal is
  drawn in the buddies' warm, rounded, big-eyed style (soft shapes, a face with friendly eyes and a highlight, a little smile) while
  keeping its true shape from real photos (a penguin: upright, black back and flippers, white belly, orange feet and beak; an
  ostrich: long neck, big round body, long legs). And every animal moves: an idle loop (blink, breathe, small bob) plus its own
  action where it appears (a penguin waddles and flaps its flippers, a bird hops or flutters, a fish swims, a frog hops). Never a
  stiff still sticker.
- **No orphan labels.** A tag (like "YOU") appears only with the thing it labels, in the same shot; if the thing is not in a
  scene, its tag is not there either (teacher, 2026-11-21: a stray "YOU" tag in EP6 Part 1's outro).
- **Props never hide behind the buddies (teacher, 2026-11-21).** A board or prop that shares the frame with the buddy discs is
  centred in the free space between them and big enough to read (the EP6 outro's SIX ANIMAL GROUPS board: centred between Pip
  and Momo, 30% taller); it never sits behind or under a disc.
- **Say names right.** Singapore names Kokoro gets wrong are spoken from approved sounds: see `PRONUNCIATION.md` and `sg_pron.py`
  (e.g. Sungei Buloh = "Soon-ai, bur-low"). A new name gets a test clip for the teacher before it is used.
- **Real animals must look real (teacher, 2026-11-20).** Draw an animal's true shape from real photos before drawing it, then simplify
  into the flat cartoon style; never invent anatomy. Example: the monitor lizard from behind has a long narrow head on a long thick
  neck, a heavy body, short legs bent out at elbow and knee with claws down (never straight out like arms), a very long tapering
  tail, and very fine bead-like scales (never big hexagon tiles). Use the Singapore animal children really see: the Malayan water
  monitor (dark grey-brown, rows of pale yellow spots, paddle-shaped tail). Reference photos: `refs/` (gitignored, not for reuse).

## 10. Process

1. **Script first**, written as segment tables (time, on screen, exact words, sound, why), plus:
   - a list of the non-textbook sentences;
   - 3 Shorts;
   - a teacher sheet.
   The teacher approves before anything is rendered.
2. **Then build and render.** Check stills around every swap, card and mouth change, and send contact sheets.
3. **The teacher watches.** Their notes go in a NOTES file and are fixed in one re-render.
4. **Upload** to TheOneApp's YouTube:
   - Made for Kids, public, in the level playlist;
   - only when the teacher says so.
**Autopilot (teacher, 2026-11-20: "Upload into YouTube and make it live automatically. I trust the system.")** From P3 EP4 on, and
for finishing EP2: steps 1 and 3 no longer wait for the teacher. Write the script to this rulebook, build it, run the checks, then
upload it live with `production/youtube/explained_upload.py <epNN/upload.json>` (Made for Kids, public, thumbnails, playlists in
order). Send the teacher the finished parts and the list of things to check; their notes are fixed in a re-render and re-upload.
5. **Never delete an old video** that a new episode replaces (teacher, 2026-11-20). Its views count towards the channel and
   towards monetising it. Keep the old one up, take it out of the main level playlist, and point it to the new episode
   (a card, the end screen or a line in the description). Ask the teacher before making it unlisted or private:
   watch time on unlisted or private videos does not count towards YouTube's public watch hours.
6. **Quality before speed (teacher, 2026-11-22: "focus on improving the quality so audience can notice the differences rather than
   having us waste token and resources").** This replaces the run-ahead rule below until the teacher says otherwise: ONE episode at
   a time, no scripts written ahead, no extras. Each episode: Opus script → Fable drawing → **a "teacher's eye" review** → fixes →
   Opus render, checks, upload — automatically, without waiting for the teacher (teacher, 22 Nov: "Ultimately I want a workflow
   that automates, not waiting for me. Save the rules, follow my directions, put in more effort"; asked to choose between full
   automation (A) and approval before render (B), the teacher chose A). The teacher's-eye review is a separate Opus agent that has
   not drawn the episode: it looks at 6–8 key stills per part plus frame strips of every transition, and judges them the way the
   teacher did EP1 (`EP01_vs_EP04_NOTES.md`, `ep01/NOTES_ROUND3.md`, this rulebook): is every buddy cute and reacting? anything
   floating, cut off, hidden behind a disc, unreadable on a phone? any needless cut? does each look-closer show the why? any 2–3
   word spoken fragment? It writes its notes to `epNN/NOTES_REVIEW.md`; the drawing agent fixes every note; the reviewer checks
   again; only then render. **EP1 is the benchmark (teacher, 22 Nov: "ALL these are important and you did not make these mistakes
   when creating episode 1"):** the reviewer puts each key still next to EP1's matching kind of scene (a lock-in, a look-closer, a
   Mark Sentence, a hidden-animal moment, a board scene) and asks "would a 9-year-old on a phone see what matters as clearly as in
   EP1?" It also checks every 22 Nov rule one by one: buddies 1.5× and balanced; camera on what is talked about; look-closers use the
   whole panel with a reacting buddy face; boards keep their home position; countdowns take the stage; one set of numbered choice
   cards; hidden animal in its black-ringed circle on blue and moving between scenes; no ghost overlaps; full sentences; **no buddy
   ever frozen — every buddy on screen reacts to what happens, every line; no pill, "+1" or label overlapping the key-word shelf or
   anything else; every countdown's numbers step once per second exactly with the ticks, the ring drains in step and the bell lands
   on 0 (measured in the rendered video)** (teacher, 22 Nov, on EP13's HOLD ON!: "Pip is basically not doing anything here… the
   '+1' arms crossed is covered by the keyword… The countdown timer rhythm is off! Basic"; `reference/holdon_bad_ep13_frozen_pip_overlap.webp`).
   **The countdown is animated and the buddies react, as in EP1** (teacher, 22 Nov: "I no longer see the animation on the
   countdown, nor the interactive pet buddies' reactions"): the ring pulses on each tick, the number pops as it changes, the YOU

   pose swap (cheer, groan, good-sport shrug). Reuse EP1's lock-in and reveal routines (ep01/); never ship a still countdown.
   Any "no" is a note to fix before render. The key stills are also sent to the teacher for information (not a gate); the teacher's notes,
   whenever they come, are fixed in a re-render. Spend the effort on what viewers see: cute, reacting buddies; smooth camera with no needless
   cuts; real places with life; deep, visual explanations; full human sentences.
7. *(Paused 22 Nov)* **The pipeline runs ahead on its own (teacher, 2026-11-21: "always write script automatically once 2 episode before is live,
   one episode before start rendering").** When episode N goes live, without waiting to be asked:

## 11. Episode template (one part, about 4 to 5 min)

1. Silent cold open (a real Singapore place).
2. Hook and practice lock-in.
3. Rules and goals (the "Today you'll learn" chips). **A buddy READS the three goal cards aloud** (teacher, 2026-11-21: "make one
   of the pet buddies read these 3"): the buddy on screen says "Today you'll learn…" and then each goal, word for word as printed,
   and each card lifts and glows while it is read (about 0.4 s pause between cards). Rotate the reader between episodes and parts so
   each buddy gets a turn, and give Basil his share.
4. "Are you ready? Three! Two! One! Go!"
5. Two rounds of lock-ins, each with its evidence and its Mark Sentence. A "Say it with me!" before each card.
6. The first-person "be the thing" journey into the micro-world (the series signature; see section 1).
7. "HOLD ON!" and/or the Showdown.
8. The chant as call-and-response.
9. The reveal or payoff.
10. The score hand.
11. The hidden-animal reveal.
12. The key-word read-along.
13. Next episode's dare, or the Part 2 tease.
14. "Say it with us!", then the class shouts "Now you know!".
15. About 4 s of animated outro.
