## 2. The spine and the split

One recess in a school canteen. A board with seven empty tiles slides in: THE 7 MATERIALS. **Part 1** first teaches the word pair the whole chapter stands on, with a spoon: the spoon is the object, metal is its material. Basil's rule mixes the two up and the child stops him. Then the hook is played as Round 1: a plastic box, a metal box and a glass jar, and one of them was made from sand. The child becomes a grain of sand, is melted in a furnace and cools as clear glass. Metal and plastic follow with the book's sentences, then the noodle stall's ceramic bowl: clay, shaped and baked in a kiln. Four tiles are full, and all four started in the ground. The cliffhanger turns to the child's own school shoe: some rubber starts as white drops. **Part 2** fills the last three tiles with the materials that can come from living things: wood from trees (the bench, the pencil, paper), rubber (the child becomes a drop of sap in a rubber tree and ends as the sole of a shoe; and the book's second source, oil, is read on the card), and fabric (cotton from a plant, wool from sheep, silk from a kind of caterpillar). The Showdown is "count the parts": Pip's notes say a school shoe is one material; the child counts four. The full board is read as a Mark Sentence and chanted, and the dare hands over to Episode 9.

**Why the split is here:** each part has one idea about sources that a child can hold (from the ground; from living things), which is also the brief's mind map. The first chapter-change link is free: the series has just spent seven episodes on living things, and Part 2 shows that some materials come from them.

- **Heart** narrates (Kokoro af_heart, speed 0.90, about 0.5 s between sentences; about 0.85 and softer in the two journeys, with about 1.0 s between sentences). After a new term she waits 0.6 s. She never says a wrong idea.

Pip and Momo: sit, cheer, oops, surprised, arms crossed, pointing, thinking, waving. No new pose is needed.
- **The game:** cream answer cards with big numbers; fingers 1, 2, 3; arms crossed for HOLD ON!; one finger per point on the score hand. The buddies score a point on a lock-in round when two of the three are right; they score nothing in HOLD ON! and nothing in a Showdown built on a buddy's mistake. **Scores:** Part 1: HOLD ON! 1 : 0, Round 1 2 : 1, Round 2 3 : 2 (You 3, Buddies 2). Part 2: Round 1 1 : 1, Round 2 2 : 2, Showdown 4 : 2 (You 4, Buddies 2).
- **The board, THE 7 MATERIALS:** a wide cream board on a low stand, standing on the canteen floor at the end of the table. Seven tiles in the book's order: **WOOD · METAL · CERAMIC · RUBBER · GLASS · PLASTIC · FABRIC**. At the start every tile is a dashed outline with a question mark. When a material is taught, its name pops large in the middle of the screen (2.0 s), then hops low to its tile; the tile fills with its stamp colour, a small drawing of the material, and under it a **source slot** with a small drawing and words. The source slots: WOOD *trees* · METAL *dug up from the ground* · CERAMIC *clay* · RUBBER *sap of a rubber tree, or oil* · GLASS *sand* · PLASTIC *oil* · FABRIC *animals or plants*. Stamp colours, stamp colours only, never answer colours and none a buddy colour, green, blue or sun yellow: wood chestnut #8B4A2B, metal slate #5E6B73, ceramic terracotta #C8612E, rubber charcoal #2F2F38, glass ice grey #8FA6B8, plastic hot pink #E0457B, fabric dark mustard #A07A1A.
- **Things do not float.** The board, easels and picture boards stand on the ground or slide low with dust puffs. The magnifying glass stands on its little feet.
- **Realism (brief):** wood has grain lines, and planks are cut lengthwise from the trunk; rubber tapping is a thin diagonal cut on the trunk with a small cup under its lower end, and the sap is white; the kiln glows orange inside and the finished bowl is glazed; melted glass is orange-hot and becomes clear as it cools, and the finished jar shows a highlight; fabric is drawn with a weave; the sheep's wool is fluffy, the silk cocoon oval and pale, the cotton plant has white bolls; the aluminium can is silver, the copper wire orange-brown, the iron gate dark grey, the gold ring yellow.
