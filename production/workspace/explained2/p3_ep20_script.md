## 2. The chain of questions, the spine and the split

**The chain (each answer opens the next question; SERIES_FORMAT 1c):**
1. *EP19's dare:* two ring magnets on a pencil; the top one floats with no glue and no string. What holds it up, a pull or a push? → (Momo: a pull, "attract means pull"; Pip: a push, he feels it push back on his finger; Basil: a push, a pull would bring it down.)
2. *Be the top ring:* → **a push you cannot see holds you up; the closer you are pressed, the harder it pushes; the gap stays empty.** Answer: 2, a push.
3. *Which part of a magnet pulls and pushes the most? The middle, the biggest part?* (Basil) → **The steel paper clips bunch at the two ends. A magnet has two poles, a North pole and a South pole, and its attraction is strongest there. Every shape has two poles; a ring's are on its flat sides.**
4. *The top ring will not come down. So it is broken?* (Pip, HOLD ON!) → **Turn it over and it snaps down: it pulls. It was pushed, not broken.**
5. *So the same two magnets can pull AND push? How?* (Momo) → **Magnets can attract (pull) or repel (push) one another. It depends on which poles are facing each other.**
6. *A red end and a blue end pull; two red ends push. What about two blue ends? Is there a rule?* (Basil; Part 1's cliffhanger) → Part 2, first as a game: **two magnets push apart; turn one round, and they pull together** (be a toy train carriage).
7. *So when two magnets push apart, they must both be North poles?* (Momo) → **No: North and North push, and South and South push too. Unlike poles attract and like poles repel. That is why the ring floats: two North poles face each other.**
8. *Pip's homework: the iron nail was attracted, so it is a magnet. Right?* (Showdown) → **No: a magnet attracts lots of things that are not magnets.** *Then how can I prove something is a magnet?* (Pip) → **Test it: if it repels a magnet, it is a magnet (the repel test).**
9. *Basil hangs his bar magnet on a string and spins it, again and again. Where does it stop?* → EP21's dare (a freely suspended magnet).

**The spine:** Monday after school in Pip's school science room (Part 1), and Pip's HDB flat that evening (Part 2). Pip's pencil with its two ring magnets is on the bench in Part 1 and on the kitchen table in Part 2. Two boards fill up across the episode (THE POLES BOARD on the whiteboard; WHICH POLES FACE? on the fridge door); the fridge card's last line, the rule 6.14, is the reveal.

**Why the split is here:** the book's order and the plan's three goals: two poles and the strongest pull (pp. 105-106) and "magnets can attract or repel one another" (6.9, p. 107) in Part 1, which is also enough to answer EP19's dare (a push); which poles attract and which repel (6.10, 6.11, 6.14, pp. 107-109) and the test for a magnet (6.13, p. 109) in Part 2. Part 1 ends on the exact question the next pages answer (is there a rule?), and the dare's full "why" (two North poles face each other) is Part 2's payoff.

## 11. Choices I made

1. **The dare is answered by being the ring:** the child feels the push from below, gets pressed down and feels it grow, springs back, and looks into an empty gap; Momo's "attract means pull" is answered by the same picture (a pull would bring it down).
2. **Poles come from the class's own paper clips** (the brief's Singapore anchor): one dip shows both the two poles and "strongest at the poles" (6.6, 6.7, 6.8) against Basil's "the middle is biggest". The ring's poles on its flat sides are shown in the same look-closer, so Part 2's payoff needs no new idea.
3. **The HOLD ON! turns the push into a pull:** Pip thinks the floating ring is broken (the brief's misconception); flipping it makes it snap down, which opens the question "so magnets can pull AND push? how?" (6.9).
4. **Part 1 names the colours, Part 2 names the rule:** "a red end and a blue end pull; two red ends push" is what children see first; "unlike poles attract and like poles repel" is the book's name for it, with South and South shown too (the brief's misconception "pushing apart means North and North").
5. **The toy train** is the Part 2 journey: a toy many children own whose carriages refuse to join when turned round, so "it depends on which poles face" is something they have felt.
6. **The rings' full why** (two North poles face each other) is held back to Part 2 and labelled "in our picture" (South and South would float the same way).
7. **The repel test with two look-alike bars** (one a magnet, one iron): both are attracted, so attraction cannot tell them apart; only one repels. It follows Pip's Showdown mistake (attracted, so a magnet), the brief's key misconception, and says the test the book's way, without "only a magnet can be repelled".
8. **Two boards that fill up** (THE POLES BOARD in the science room; WHICH POLES FACE? on the fridge door) give each part a build and Part 2 a reveal (the rule written across the card).
9. **Hidden animals with a link:** a spider (many baby spiders float up into the sky with no wings, like the ring floating in mid-air) and a lizard (it holds on upside down with no glue and no magnet, by the fridge door held with magnets). Neither appears in EP16 to EP19; the spider was last in EP9 and the lizard in the first episodes.
10. **The dare into EP21 asks only "a different way, or the same way?"** so it does not give away "North-South"; the brief's hook (a hanging bar magnet, spun) is kept.
11. **Pip is wrong twice** (the HOLD ON! and the Showdown), because in EP19 he was wrong least; each of his mistakes is a careful-sounding one children really make.
