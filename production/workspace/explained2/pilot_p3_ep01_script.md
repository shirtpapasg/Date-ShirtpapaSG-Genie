## 1. The spine: why the 30-day box

**Chosen: the "30-day box" with a day counter (success examples 2 and 3, Dr. Plants).** Six things go into one clear box on an HDB
window ledge: a teddy bear keychain, a wind-up robot, a pebble, a green bean, a small potted plant and a mealworm. A big counter runs
from DAY 1 to DAY 30. This spine is the strongest for this topic because the science itself needs time. You cannot tell living from
non-living in one look: the robot moves on Day 1 and the green bean does nothing. Only when the days pass do the real clues show: the
robot stops and never needs anything, the bean splits and grows, the plant grows towards the window, the mealworm eats and gets bigger,
and the teddy bear, robot and pebble stay exactly the same. So the day counter is the evidence, and every wrong idea a buddy holds
("it moves", "it is still", "it never left its pot") is settled by watching, never by Heart just saying so. It also gives the lock-in
game a natural question each round ("what will this thing do by Day 30?"), one suspense line held to the end (what is under the cloth
on Day 30?), a quiet first-person moment inside the box on the night the bean splits, and a real class activity (a class can run the
same box). The title is a simple, extreme premise, as in the success examples. The box has air holes and the living things are cared
for with food, water, air and light, which is itself a preview of Episode 2.

**Runners-up, kept for later episodes:**
- **The field trip (Sesame Street):** best where the topic is a place full of things to discover. Suggested for P3 EP4-6 (a trip to a
  real Singapore place such as the Zoo or Sungei Buloh to meet the animal groups) and EP8 (the seven materials in a hawker centre).
  The brief's zoo scene for diversity is not lost: it becomes the opening of EP4.
- **The buddies' build-and-reveal machine (Aquarium Info):** best where the rule is a chain of yes/no checks. Suggested for P3 EP4
  ("We built a sorting machine for every living thing": plants, animals, fungi, bacteria), EP12-13 (build a school bag) and EP22-25
  (build an electromagnet). A sorting machine in EP1 would hide the evidence inside the machine; the box shows it.
- **The micro-world of wonder (Journey to the Microcosmos):** P3 EP7 (fungi and bacteria). Used here only for 26 seconds.
- **The life story (Kurzgesagt, "from birth to death"):** P3 EP14-18 (life cycles).

**How the segments chain (each answer opens the next question):**
1. A teddy bear and Momo look alike, yet one is living. So looks are not the clue. *Then is moving the clue?*
2. Round 1: the wind-up robot moves but is non-living. So moving is not the clue. *Then is a thing that stays still always non-living?*
3. Round 2: the green bean stays still for two days, then grows. It was living all along. *So what ARE the clues?*
4. The clue card: what a thing needs and what it can do. *Can we now sort all six?*
5. FOUL: Momo sorts by soft and hard. A real way to classify, but it answers a different question. *So who has sorted them properly?*
6. Showdown: Pip's homework table has one thing in the wrong column (the plant). *Was everyone right? Open the box on Day 30.*
7. Day 30: three things changed and three did not. *Day 31: the plant is drooping. What does it need?* (Episode 2)

**Two things the teacher should know before reading:**
- **The day counter is a drawn story, not data.** The timings (root on Day 3, leaves by Day 5, a tall plant by Day 30) follow how
  green beans normally grow in Singapore, but nobody has run this box yet. Rule 9 (drawn but true): please have a class or a teacher
  run the real box once and correct the day numbers before the render. Time-lapse shots carry a small "speeded up" tag.
- **Episodes 2 and 3 are previewed, not taught.** The two takeaway sentences ("Living things need water, food and air." and "Living
  things grow, respond to changes and reproduce.") are each said once, on a card tagged "More in Episodes 2 and 3", exactly as the EP1
  brief allows. The words respond and reproduce are named and not explained. Wilting, oxygen, "plants make their own food" and the
  mimosa are not mentioned.

## 2. Cast, game rules and conventions

| Who | Role in this episode |
|---|---|
| **Heart** (host, Kokoro af_heart voice 3) | Runs the game, reads every Mark Sentence. Never says anything wrong. |
| **Pip** (chinchilla, careful) | Right in Round 1. Not right in Round 2 ("it did nothing for a whole day") and it is his Day 1 homework in the Showdown. His lesson: being careful is not enough, you must watch long enough. |
| **Basil** (lizard, bold) | Not right in Round 1 ("it moves, so it's living"). Right, with a good reason, in Round 2 and the Showdown. |
| **Momo** (cat, curious) | Asks the question children are thinking. Right for a lucky reason in Round 2 (no point). Runs the FOUL (sorts by soft and hard). Right in the Showdown. |
| **You** | A pair of hands and a YOU tag. Lock in with one hand, keep score on the other. |
| **Cicak** | Hidden on the window frame in the Showdown replay (about 5:10, in the DAY 14 half of the split screen, upper left, 2 seconds). |

**The lock-in (same routine every time):** Heart asks a closed question; "Point left for living. Point right for non-living." (or
fingers 1-2-3 in the Showdown); a ring drains for 5 seconds (6 in the Showdown) while the music drops to a speeding-up woodblock tick;
a bell; Heart: "Locked in!"; only then do the buddies answer, one line each. The answer stays hidden during the ring. Screen side
labels during every lock-in: a green LIVING arrow on the left edge, a blue NON-LIVING arrow on the right edge.

**Score:** Round 1 = 1, Round 2 = 1, FOUL = 1, Showdown = 2. Perfect game = 5 fingers = a high five. "No lock-in, no point."
The Buddies score as a team when at least two of them are right with a good reason: Round 1 = 1, Round 2 = 0, FOUL = 0, Showdown = 2.
Final: You (up to 5) vs the Buddies (3).

**Marks used in the tables:**
- **[LOCK-IN n]** the viewer answers with the body. **[FOUL]** cross your arms. **[SHOUT]** say it aloud. **[SCORE]** score hand.
- **[BELL]** teacher pause point: a small bell icon shows top right for 3 seconds (see the teacher sheet).
- **[MARK SENTENCE]** exact textbook words from `definitions.md`, shown on a gold strip and read by Heart without any change.
- **✗** a buddy's idea that is not right: a ✗ at the start of the speech bubble only, no strike-through, words still readable; it is
  answered by evidence within 20 seconds. Wrong ideas are never in Heart's voice.
- Voices: Pip af_sky, Basil am_liam, Momo af_heart (each lifted to a child's voice, per the proposal). Heart excited only on "Now you know!".
- Kit notes: spoken numbers are written as words; `pron` entries needed for *taugeh* (TOW-gay), *cicak* (CHEE-chak), *mealworm*.
  Captions word by word. Every frame depends only on t.
- Style-guide word check: no "dead", "cheat", "trap", "dumb", "kill" anywhere. "FOUL" is the proposal's round name; if the teacher
  prefers a softer word, "HOLD ON!" works with the same crossed arms.

**A real sorting activity (one lesson, plus two minutes a day):**
1. **The class box.** Put six things in a clear box with air holes near a window: a soft toy, a wind-up toy, a pebble, three green
   beans on wet cotton wool, a small potted plant, and (if your school keeps them) two mealworms in a dish of oats with a carrot slice.
   An adult sets it up; pupils look without picking the animals up and wash their hands. No animals available? Leave them out: the
   green beans and the plant are enough.
2. **Day 1 prediction.** Each pupil folds a sheet into two columns, LIVING THINGS and NON-LIVING THINGS, and writes each thing in a
   column in pencil (a table). Collect the sheets.
3. **Watch.** A different pair draws the box each day for 7 school days (or 30 days for the full challenge) on a class day counter.
4. **Last day.** Hand back the sheets. Pupils correct their own table and say what they SAW that changed their mind.
5. **Show it three ways.** Groups present the final sort as pictures, as a table and as a graphic organiser.
6. **Sort it another way (the book's Let's Explore, p. 7).** Classify objects from the classroom by a characteristic the group
   chooses (for example, uses electricity or does not). Another group works out the rule.
Please send us your class's real day-by-day results: we will correct the day numbers in the video to match real data.

**Answer frame (for speaking and writing):**
- "The ______ is a living thing. It needs ______. It can ______."
- "The ______ is a non-living thing. It does not need water, food or air. It does not grow."
- "I classified the things into ______ and ______. I grouped them by ______."

**Marking points (exact textbook wording):**
1. "There is a great variety of living and non-living things around us. This is called diversity."
2. "Classification is the grouping of things based on their similarities and differences."
3. "We can present our classification using pictures, a table, and a graphic organiser."
4. Preview only (taught in Episodes 2 and 3): "Living things need water, food and air." · "Living things grow, respond to changes and
   reproduce."

**Common slips to listen for:** "it's alive because it moves" · "the seed is non-living because it is not doing anything" · "plants
are non-living" · giving a look ("it has eyes", "it is green") as the reason · "classification is putting the same things together"
(the full wording says similarities and differences).

**Questions the video leaves out on purpose (your call in class):** a flame, clouds, an egg, a dry leaf that has fallen, a virus.
The P3 book does not settle these (it leaves viruses open on p. 8), so the video stays with six clear cases.
