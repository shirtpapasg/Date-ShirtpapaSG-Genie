## 2. The chain of questions, the spine and the split

**The chain (each answer opens the next question; SERIES_FORMAT 1c):**
1. *EP22's dare:* Momo coils a long wire round a new iron nail and joins its two ends to a battery; the nail attracts steel clips. She takes the battery away. Do the clips stay or drop? → (Basil: they stay, like Pip's stroked nail stayed a magnet; Pip: they drop, the crane across the road drops its scrap all by itself; Momo: they drop, the clips came only when she joined the battery.)
2. *Be the iron nail, in a coil:* → **the wire wraps round you; its ends are joined to the battery; a pull at your tip, the clips jump up; the battery is taken away; the pull is gone and the clips drop.** Answer: 2. Momo: that is the electrical method.
3. *But is Momo's nail still a little magnet, like Pip's stroked nail?* (Basil) → **Battery away, the nail is dipped: the clips stay in the tray. Battery on: they jump. Off: they drop. The nail loses most of its magnetism without electricity. The scrapyard crane across the road works the same way: on, it lifts; off, it lets go. An electromagnet.**
4. *Momo: one battery makes a magnet, so the wall socket will make a super magnet!* (HOLD ON!) → **Never: electricity from the wall socket is far too strong and can hurt you very badly. Batteries only, not for long, and do not touch the exposed wires** (the book's WARNING box).
5. *Pip: the battery must be the magnet, and the wire just joins it to the nail.* → **The battery alone: no clips. The nail alone: no clips. Coiled, one end joined: nothing. Both ends joined: electricity flows from the battery, round the nail and back, and the nail becomes a magnet.** The three steps.
6. *The ten-turn crane cannot lift the steel bolt, and there are no more batteries. Can it ever get stronger?* (Part 1's cliffhanger) → Part 2, first as a game: **ten turns or twenty, one battery each: the twenty-turn hook lifts the bolt** (be a steel bolt).
7. *Pip: but it is the same battery. How can more turns make it stronger?* → **Each turn of wire adds a little more pull. More turns, a stronger electromagnet; the school's old electric bell has many turns of wire inside.**
8. *Momo's poster: "electricity is stronger, so a light wooden chopstick works too"?* (Showdown) → **No: wood cannot be made into a magnet. Objects made of iron and steel can.** *Then the bolt slips off on the swing, and all the wire is used up. How else?* (Basil) → **Twenty turns each: two batteries attract more clips than one. More batteries, a stronger electromagnet. The crane carries the bolt to the lorry and drops it in.**
9. *Basil compares an iron nail and a steel nail: 25 turns on one, 8 turns on the other. Is his test fair?* → EP24's dare (fair tests).

**The spine:** a build-and-reveal. Recycling Week's mini crane must lift only the steel scrap and drop it into the lorry, and Basil's bar magnet will not let go. Saturday morning at a coffee shop facing a scrap yard (Part 1): the electromagnet is made, switched on and off, made safe, and understood. Monday after school in the science room (Part 2): more turns, more batteries; the steel bolt reaches the lorry (the reveal), *READY FOR RECYCLING WEEK!*.

**Why the split is here:** the plan's three goals and the book's order. Part 1 is p. 115 (the electrical method, its steps, the electromagnet, the WARNING box) plus Let's Apply Q2(a) (take the battery away), which answers EP22's dare fully. Part 2 is pp. 116-117 (more turns, more batteries, the takeaway 7.8). Part 1 ends on Pip's "it can never get stronger", the exact question that opens Part 2's Round 1.

  1. **A coil of wire round an iron nail, with both ends joined to a battery, makes an electromagnet that attracts steel clips** (the book's p. 115 activity, School Lab 7.1B): very sure. With about ten turns and one AA battery it attracts a few clips; how many depends on the wire, the nail and the battery, so **no number is ever said or written**.

"Each turn of wire adds a little more pull" is a true simple picture (each turn adds to the magnetic effect for the same electricity). "More batteries make more electricity flow round the coil" is true for batteries in a row with the same coil (more push, more current: the word "current" is P5 and is not said).
  5. **Heat:** a coil joined straight to a battery is close to a short circuit: the wire, the battery and the nail can get hot within a short time, faster with more batteries (very sure; the book's 7.5 "may become hot"). Hence "Join it only for a moment" everywhere and "More batteries get hot faster" in Part 2.
  6. **The wall socket:** mains electricity (230 V in Singapore) can injure or kill (very sure); Basil's "It can hurt you very badly" is true and not frightening in detail. The book's term "main electrical supply" = the electricity from the wall sockets (Heart's line "Wall sockets give us the main electrical supply." is a simple true gloss). **A 1.5-3 V battery does not give a felt shock**, so the script never says the battery shocks; "Do not touch the exposed wires" is the book's rule (the bare ends can get hot), read word for word and not explained further.
  7. **The scrapyard crane:** scrapyards use a large round lifting electromagnet on a crane; switched on, it lifts iron and steel scrap; switched off, the scrap drops (very sure; the book's 7.12a: "The crane has a large iron disc that becomes a powerful electromagnet. Singapore has scrap metal yards in its industrial estates (very sure); the drawn yard has no name or sign brand (*SCRAP METAL* only).
  8. **The electric bell:** a classic electric bell (a gong with a hammer, as in older schools, and many fire alarm bells) works with an electromagnet: switched on, it pulls an iron strip with the hammer, the hammer hits the gong, the movement breaks the connection, the magnet switches off, a spring pulls the strip back, and it starts again, many times a second (very sure). **Many new school bells are electronic speakers** (very sure), so the script says "**Our** school bell has an electromagnet inside" about the old bell drawn on this corridor wall, never "every school bell". "Many turns of wire": a bell's coils have many turns of fine wire (very sure).
  9. **Steel bottle caps are magnetic; aluminium ring-pulls and plastic caps are not** (very sure: crown caps are tin- or chrome-plated steel; ring-pulls are aluminium). Steel paper clips: very sure. A short steel bolt is magnetic (very sure).
  10. **Lift strength:** a nail electromagnet with twenty turns and two AA batteries lifting a small steel bolt (a few grams) touching its tip: about 85% sure for a small bolt (choose a short M5 or M6 bolt at Stage 2, drawn chunky but small); a ten-turn, one-battery nail lifting steel bottle caps (about 2 g each): about 90% sure. Many millipedes, including Singapore's common brown and rusty millipedes, curl into a tight flat spiral when disturbed (very sure). "Millipede" means "a thousand feet", but most species have far fewer than a thousand legs (from a few dozen to a few hundred; only one known species, found in Australia in 2021, has more than a thousand): very sure. **Not said:** a number of legs.   13. **The cricket fact:** "A cricket's ears are on its front legs, just below its knees! So when our school bell rings, the cricket hears it with its legs!" Crickets hear with eardrums (tympanal organs) on the front legs, on the part just below the "knee" joint (very sure). Field crickets are common in Singapore's school gardens and grassy places (very sure).

## 11. Choices I made

1. **The dare is answered by being the nail inside the coil:** the child feels the wire wrap round in neat turns, feels the pull arrive only when the ends are joined, and feels it go when the battery is taken away. "Take the battery away" is lived before it is explained.

4. **"What makes it a magnet?" tests the parts one at a time** (the battery alone, the nail alone, one end, both ends), so the closed loop is shown without the P5 word "circuit", and Pip's real misconception (the battery is the magnet) is answered.
5. **The real uses are both "on and off":** the scrapyard crane across the road (the reason the mini crane needs a switch) and the school's old electric bell (on and off, very fast; many turns of wire). Both are seen in the real place, safely; the book's crane sentence stays for EP25.
