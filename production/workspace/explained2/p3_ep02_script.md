## 1. The spine: why the Buddy Hotel

**Chosen: the buddies' build-and-reveal (success example 4, Aquarium Info), with a sunset deadline: "We turned our window ledge into a
hotel for a plant and a grasshopper."** EP1 ends on Day 31 with the potted plant drooping, so EP2 starts on that same ledge, on that
same day. The buddies build a three-room hotel, and every room has the same check-in board with three boxes: WATER, FOOD, AIR. Nine
boxes must be ticked by sunset. This is the strongest spine for this topic because a need only shows when it is missing, and a build
gives us three honest "something is missing" problems, one per need: Room 1 has a wilting plant (water), Room 2 gets a pale bean plant
out of Pip's dark cupboard (food, which a plant makes only when there is light), and Room 3 is nearly built without air holes (air),
caught before the grasshopper goes in. Each problem is a round of the game, each fix puts a tick on the board, and the full board at
sunset IS the big idea in one picture: three very different guests, the same three needs. It is also a different shape from EP1 (a
box you watch for 30 days), so the series does not repeat itself, yet it keeps EP1's set, counter and plant, so it feels like the next
chapter. The needs test (School Lab 1.2) sits inside it as Pip's two-plant test, the unfair test is Basil's FOUL, and the toy boat
from the lab's Part (b) is Round Zero and the last joke: it sits at the front desk and needs no room.

**Why not the others this time:**
- **A "no water vs no light vs no air" plant race (Dr. Plants):** tempting, but EP1 already used the day counter as its spine, and the
  "no air" lane cannot be shown truthfully or kindly: a plant in a closed jar does not give a clear P3 result, and no animal may be
  kept without air. Here the air need is taught the way the book teaches it (a box needs holes) and the hole is made before the guest
  arrives. The two lanes that do work (water, light) are kept as short fair tests inside the hotel.
- **The Kranji farm trip (Sesame Street):** kept for EP4-6 (animal groups), where a place full of different animals is the point.
  Here one ledge keeps the three needs side by side on one board.
- **The micro-world (Journey to the Microcosmos):** used for 22 seconds only, inside the plant, while we wait for the water to work.

**How the segments chain (each answer opens the next question):**
1. Round Zero: the toy boat floats on water but does not need water. *Then who does need water?*
2. Round 1: the drooping plant is missing water. It is wilting, not sad. *Where does the water go? (shrink) And who feeds a plant?*
3. Round 2: the cupboard plant had water and air but could not make food in the dark. *So a plant needs all three. Does an animal?*
4. Room 3: the grasshopper's room needs holes for air, leaves for food, drops of water. *Can we test a need ourselves?*
5. FOUL: Basil changes two things at once. *Change one thing only. So can you check a written answer?*
6. Showdown: Momo's homework forgets food. *Is the board full by sunset?*
7. Sunset: nine ticks. Next morning the grasshopper goes home, and a plant in the garden folds up when touched. *(Episode 3)*

**Three things the teacher should know before reading:**
- **The times are a drawn story, not data** (rule 9, drawn but true). "A few hours later" for the watered plant, "two weeks" in the
  cupboard and "four days" without water follow how young plants normally behave in Singapore and the P3 brief's own wording, but
  nobody has run these exact pots. Please have a class run the two tests on the teacher sheet once and correct the times before the
  render. Time-lapse shots carry a small "speeded up" tag.
- **Episode 3 is not taught.** Grow, respond and reproduce are never explained. The cupboard plant is described only as "pale and
  weak" (never "it grew towards..."). The mimosa appears only in the last 8 seconds as the dare, and its folding is not named.
- **The lab link:** School Lab 1.2 is still "In review" in the app. The spoken lab line (Segment 12) should go in only after the
  teacher signs the lab off; the episode works without it.

## 2. Cast, game rules and conventions (same as EP1)

| Who | Role in this episode |
|---|---|
| **Heart** (host, Kokoro af_heart voice 3) | Runs the game, reads every Mark Sentence. Never says anything wrong. |
| **Pip** (chinchilla, careful) | Right in Round Zero and Round 1 (he touched the soil). Not right in Round 2 ("the cupboard door was shut, so it is air"). Catches the missing air holes and Basil's FOUL. Right in the Showdown. |
| **Basil** (lizard, bold) | Uses what he learned in EP1 in Round Zero. Not right in Round 1 ("it's hungry!"). Right for a reason that is not right in Round 2 (no point). Runs the FOUL. Right, with a good reason, in the Showdown. |
| **Momo** (cat, curious) | Not right in Round Zero ("it needs water to sail"). Asks the questions children are thinking ("Is it sad?", "Plants MAKE food?"). Right with a good reason in Rounds 1 and 2. It is her homework in the Showdown. |
| **You** | A pair of hands and a YOU tag. Lock in with one hand, keep score on the other. |
| **Cicak** | Hidden on the wall behind the BUDDY HOTEL sign during the fair re-run in the FOUL (about 5:02, upper right, 2 seconds). |

The wrong idea rotates: Momo (Round Zero), Basil (Round 1), Pip (Round 2), Basil's test (FOUL), Momo's homework (Showdown). In EP1 it
was Basil, Pip, Momo, Pip. No buddy is "the one who is always wrong", and nobody can win by copying or opposing one buddy.

**The lock-in (same routine every time):** Heart asks a closed question; the viewer answers with fingers (one, two or three) or by
pointing (Round Zero keeps EP1's "point left for living, point right for non-living"); a ring drains for 5 seconds (6 in the
Showdown) while the music drops to a speeding-up woodblock tick; a bell; Heart: "Locked in!"; only then do the buddies answer, one
line each. The answer stays hidden during the ring.

**Score (EP1's rules):** Round 1 = 1, Round 2 = 1, FOUL = 1, Showdown = 2. Perfect game = 5 fingers = a high five. "No lock-in, no
point." **Round Zero** is new in EP2: it earns a **streak star** (a star on the scoreboard, "1 episode in a row"), not a finger, so
the perfect game stays a high five. The Buddies score as a team when at least two of them are right with a good reason: Round 1 = 1
(Pip, Momo), Round 2 = 0 (only Momo), FOUL = 0, Showdown = 2. Final: You (up to 5) vs the Buddies (3).

**Marks used in the tables:**
- **[LOCK-IN n]** the viewer answers with the body. **[FOUL]** cross your arms. **[SHOUT]** say it aloud. **[SCORE]** score hand.
- **[BELL]** teacher pause point: a small bell icon shows top right for 3 seconds (see the teacher sheet).
- **[MARK SENTENCE]** exact textbook words from `definitions.md`, shown on a gold strip and read by Heart without any change. Four in
  this episode (the cap). Other exact book sentences that Heart says are tagged **[BOOK WORDS]** and shown as a plain caption.
- **✗** a buddy's idea that is not right: a ✗ at the start of the speech bubble only, no strike-through, words still readable; it is
  answered by evidence within 20 seconds. Wrong ideas are never in Heart's voice.
- **The hotel board:** three rows (Room 1, Room 2, Room 3), three columns with one kind of label (WATER, FOOD, AIR). A tick lands only
  when the guest is shown getting that thing. It is the episode's progress bar and is hidden in the shrink.
- Voices: Pip af_sky, Basil am_liam, Momo af_heart (each lifted to a child's voice). Heart is excited only on "Now you know!".
- Kit notes: spoken numbers are written as words; `pron` entries needed for *cicak* (CHEE-chak), *mimosa*, *Toa Payoh*. Captions word
  by word. Every frame depends only on t.
- Word check (P3 style guide): no "dead", "die", "cheat", "trap", "dumb", "kill" anywhere. The book's "Without air, both plants and
  animals will die" is not spoken. No plant has a mouth or a face. "FOUL" is the format's round name, as in EP1 ("HOLD ON!" is the
  softer fallback).
- Animal care shown on screen: the grasshopper is only looked at, never squeezed; an adult's hands carry its box; the box has air
  holes before it goes in, fresh leaves, drops of water and shade; it stays one night and goes back to the same garden bed. Small
  print on its first shot: "An adult helps. One night only. Then it goes home."

**A real classroom needs test (bean plants; two tests, one thing changed in each):**
Grow green beans on wet cotton wool or in small pots of soil until each has two open leaves (about a week). Then:

| | Test 1: Does a bean plant need water? | Test 2: Does a bean plant need light to make food? |
|---|---|---|
| What we change (one thing only) | Water: plant A is watered every school day, plant B is not. | Light: plant A is by the window, plant B is in a dark cupboard (or under a box). |
| What we keep the same | Kind and size of plant, pot, amount of soil, same place by the window (light and air). | Kind and size of plant, pot, amount of soil, same amount of water every school day. |
| What we observe | Is each plant upright or wilting? A drawing or photo each day for up to five days. | Colour of the leaves (green or pale), stem sturdy or weak. A drawing or photo twice a week for two weeks. |
| When to stop | As soon as plant B is clearly wilting, water it and watch it for a few hours. | After two weeks, move plant B to the window and keep watching for another week. |

- Use three plants in each set-up if you can, so one odd plant does not decide the result.
- **Air:** do not test it by keeping any animal or plant without air. Teach it as the book does: you come up for air when you swim; a
  box for a grasshopper needs holes. (A plant shut in a jar does not give a clear result at P3 level.)
- **The toy boat (lab 1.2, Part (b)):** put a toy boat beside the plants. Give it no water, food or light for the same two weeks. It
  does not change. Non-living things do not need water, food or air.
- Looking at small animals: an adult handles them; a box with air holes, fresh leaves, a few drops of water, shade; back to the same
  spot by the next day; wash hands.

**The answer frame (for "What does this living thing need? Explain what you see."):**
1. What I see: "Plant B is ______ (wilting / pale and weak)."
2. What it is missing: "It does not have enough ______ (water)." or "It has no ______ (light), so it cannot make its own ______ (food)."
3. The rule: "Living things need water, food and air."

**Marking points (exact book words):**
- "Living things need water, food and air." (all three; a list with two is incomplete)
- "It is wilting. It does not have enough water." (say *wilting*, not *sad* or *tired*)
- "All living things need food. Food provides living things with energy."
- "Plants do not eat but they can make their own food when there is light." (never "plants eat sunlight" or "plants eat soil")
- "Living things breathe in air that contains oxygen."
- Fair test: only one thing is changed; everything else is kept the same.

**Common slips to listen for:** "The plant died because it was sad." · "The plant has no food because nobody fed it." · "Plants need
water and air only." · "The boat needs water because it floats." · "The cupboard plant had no air."

**After the video:** "Tell someone at home what a cicak needs." Next lesson: "Touch this plant and it folds up! What is it doing?"
