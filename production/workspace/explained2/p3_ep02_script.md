## 2. The spine and the split

**One spine: a build-and-reveal, the Buddy Hotel.** EP1 Part 2 ends on Day 31 with the plant drooping, so EP2 starts on that same
ledge, on that same day. The buddies turn the window ledge into a hotel with three rooms. Every room has the same row on the check-in
board: WATER, FOOD, AIR. Nine boxes must be ticked by sunset. A need only shows when it is missing, and the hotel gives three honest
"something is missing" problems, one per need. The full board at sunset IS the big idea in one picture: three different guests, the
same three needs.

| Room | Guest | What is missing | Which part |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The drooping plant from EP1's box | Water. It is wilting. | Part 1 (and its last box, FOOD, is the twist at sunset in Part 2) |
| 2 | EP1's mealworm | Air: the jar has a tight lid with no holes. Caught before the mealworm goes in. | Part 1 |
| 3 | A bean plant from Pip's dark cupboard | Food: it could not make its own food in the dark. | Part 2 |

**Why the split is here.** Part 1 is "water and air": the two needs a child can see and fix in a minute. Part 2 is "food": the
hardest idea in the section (plants do not eat; they make their own food when there is light), so it gets a whole part and the
Showdown. Part 1 ends at the moment Pip carries in the covered pot: it had water, it had air, and something is still wrong. That is a
true cliffhanger, and the viewer can already start to answer it. Each part has its own goals, three key words, its own score and its
own lizard.

**Order of the needs.** The book's order is water, food, air. The videos teach water, air, food, and always SAY them in the book's
order (the board's columns, the chant, the last Mark Sentence). See open question 1.

**The shrink (Part 1, about 27 s).** While the watered plant takes a few hours to stand up again, you shrink down and follow the
water: into the wet soil, in through a root, up the stem, all the way to a leaf. It is true at P3 level and uses no new term; the only
science sentence in it is the book's caption "Plants need water to make food."

**The board, tick by tick:**

| When | Tick | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1, 2:10 | Room 1 WATER (Pip watered the plant) | 1 |
| Part 1, 3:21 | Room 2 AIR, FOOD, WATER (holes, oats, carrot) | 4 |
| Part 1, 3:28 | Room 1 AIR ("Even plants need air.") | 5 |
| Part 2, 2:11 | Room 3 WATER, AIR, FOOD (it is in the light now) | 8 |
| Part 2, 3:11 | Room 1 FOOD (it stood by the window all day) | 9 |

**Three things the teacher should know before reading:**
- **The times are a drawn story, not data.** "A few hours" for the watered plant and "two weeks" in the cupboard follow the P3 plan's
  own wording and how young plants normally behave, but nobody has run these exact pots. Please have a class run the two tests on the
  teacher sheet once and correct the times before the render. Time-lapse shots carry a small "speeded up" tag.
- **Episode 3 is not taught.** Grow, respond and reproduce are never explained. The cupboard plant is only "pale and weak" (never "it
  grew towards..."). The folding plant appears only in the last seconds as the dare; it is not named and its folding is not explained.
- **The lab line** (School Lab 1.2 is still "In review" in the app) is an optional insert in Part 2. See section 6.

## 3. Cast, the game and the conventions

| Who | In Part 1 | In Part 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Heart** (host, voice only) | Runs the game, reads every Mark Sentence. Never says anything wrong. | The same. |
| **Pip** (chinchilla, careful, purple #8B5CF6) | Right in Round 1 (he touched the soil). His room has no air holes (HOLD ON!). | Runs the needs test. Right in Round 1 and the Showdown. Says "Say it with me!" on card 2. |
| **Basil** (lizard, bold, teal #14B8A6) | Not right in Round 1 ("give it my biscuit"). Spots the holes. Says "Say it with me!" on card 2. | Not right in Round 1 ("the door was shut"). Right in the Showdown. Says "Say it with me!" on card 1. Finds the folding plant. |
| **Momo** (cat, curious, coral #FB7158) | Asks "Is it sad?" and "Who feeds a plant?". Right in Round 1. Says "Say it with me!" on card 1. | Right number, reason not right, in Round 1 (no point). It is her homework in the Showdown. Says "Say it with me!" on card 3. |
| **You** | Sun-yellow hands and a YOU tag. One hand answers, the other keeps score. | The same. |
| **The lizard** | A cute little house lizard on the wall above the hotel sign. | Peeking from behind the wall clock at sunset. |

**The wrong idea rotates:** Basil (Part 1, Round 1), Pip (Part 1, HOLD ON!), Basil (Part 2, Round 1), Momo (Part 2, right for a reason
that is not right; then her homework). In EP1 it went Basil, Pip, Momo, Pip. Nobody is "the one who is always wrong".

**Score.** Each part keeps its own score.
- Part 1: Round 1 = 1, HOLD ON! = 1. A perfect game is 2 fingers. The Buddies score 1 (Pip and Momo, Round 1).
- Part 2: Round 1 = 1, Showdown = 2. A perfect game is 3 fingers. The Buddies score 2 (the Showdown; in Round 1 only Pip has a good reason).
- The Buddies score as a team when at least two of them are right with a good reason. "No lock-in, no point."

**The lock-in (the same routine every time):** Heart asks a closed question with three choices; the viewer answers with 1, 2 or 3
fingers; the ring timer drains for 5 s (6 s in the Showdown) while the music drops to a woodblock tick that speeds up; a bell;
"LOCKED IN!"; only then do the buddies answer, one line and one reason each. The answer stays hidden during the ring.

**The three choices keep three fixed colours all episode:** WATER blue, FOOD orange, AIR green, on the answer cards and on the board's
column labels. No buddy colour and no sun-yellow is used for a choice.

**Marks used in the tables:**
- **[LOCK-IN n]** the viewer answers with fingers. **[HOLD ON!]** cross your arms. **[SCORE]** score hand.
- **[SAY IT]** a buddy (or all three) SAYS "Say it with me!" or "Say it with us!" aloud, with lip-sync and an inviting gesture; then a
  0.8 s pause; then Heart reads or the chant starts.
- **[YOUR TURN!]** the tag by the YOU hands in the chant; one bar (about 1.6 s) for the class, with the beat going on softly.
- **[MARK SENTENCE n of N]** exact textbook words on the cream card, read by Heart with no change, then 2 s of stillness.
- **[BOOK WORDS]** other exact book sentences that Heart says, shown as a plain caption.
- **[KEY WORD]** said for the first time: pops LARGE with a chime, holds 1.5 s, then slides to the key-word shelf (top right, under
  the score pill; sun-yellow pills stacking downwards).
- **[BELL]** a teacher pause point: a small bell icon shows for 3 s (see the teacher sheet).
- **✗** a buddy's idea that is not right: a small ✗ at the start of the speech bubble only; the words stay readable; it is answered by
  evidence within seconds. Wrong ideas are never in Heart's voice.

**Picture rules that apply to every row (from `SERIES_FORMAT.md` sections 5 to 7):**
- The buddies are the teacher's painted cut-out sprites and **always sit in their circular aura discs** when they are next to real
  props (pots, the jar, the glass box, the board, the homework sheet). They are presenters, not tiny animals in the set.
- **Nothing floats.** The hotel sign, the three room fronts, the check-in board on its easel, the pots, the jar, the answer cards and
  the picture cards all stand on the ledge or on the ground. Things arrive by being carried or slid by a buddy, or in low hops with
  dust puffs. An adult's hands move the mealworm.
- **Props read clearly.** The Buddy Hotel is three small cardboard room fronts with big numbers 1, 2, 3 and a hand-painted sign. The
  glass box is EP1's: clear, with a lid, air holes and the label OUR BOX. The jar is a clear glass jar; its lid is clearly either
  solid metal or full of small holes. The check-in board is a 3 by 3 grid with one kind of label per row and per column.
- Pose swaps: squash, hard cut, stretch, overshoot, a small hop, sparkles and dust. Never a cross-fade.
- Every label and small print fits fully inside the frame; label lines never cross other things. No text in the bottom fifth of the
  end scenes. No burned-in captions (an `.srt` is made for each part).
- The shelf slides aside during the cold open, the countdown, the shrink and full-screen cards.
- Something changes on screen every 10 to 15 s (the lock-in rings and the shrink are the planned quiet moments).
- Word check: no "dead", "die", "kill", "cheat", "trap" or "dumb" anywhere. The book's "Without air, both plants and animals will die"
  is not spoken. No plant has a mouth or a face.
- Animal care on screen: the mealworm always has oats, a slice of carrot and a lid with air holes; only an adult's hands move it; it
  goes back to its tub on Day 32. Small print: "An adult helps. Wash your hands."

**How the times were estimated.** From EP1's finished voice tracks (same voices, same speed, same gaps): about 0.28 s for each spoken
word, 0.45 s before each of Heart's lines, plus every planned pause (rings, the 0.8 s "get ready" pauses, the 1.5 s key-word holds,
the 2 s card holds, the YOUR TURN bars, the 1.2 s read-along gaps). EP1 Part 1 has 592 spoken words and runs 4:22; Part 1 here has
about 608 and Part 2 about 613. The real times come from the voice files; expect each part to land within about 15 s of the
estimate. If a part comes out over 5:00, see section 6.

**A real classroom needs test (bean plants; two tests, one thing changed in each):**
Grow green beans on wet cotton wool or in small pots of soil until each has two open leaves (about a week). Then:

| | Test 1: Does a bean plant need water? | Test 2: Does a bean plant need light to make food? |
|---|---|---|
| What we change (one thing only) | Water: plant A is watered every school day, plant B is not. | Light: plant A is by the window, plant B is in a dark cupboard (or under a box). |
| What we keep the same | Kind and size of plant, pot, amount of soil, same place by the window (light and air). | Kind and size of plant, pot, amount of soil, same amount of water every school day. |
| What we observe | Is each plant upright or wilting? A drawing or photo each day for up to five days. | Colour of the leaves (green or pale), stem strong or weak. A drawing or photo twice a week for two weeks. |
| When to stop | As soon as plant B is clearly wilting, water it and watch it for a few hours. | After two weeks, move plant B to the window and keep watching for another week. |

- Use three plants in each set-up if you can, so one odd plant does not decide the result.
- **Is this test fair?** (the round left out of the video) Basil tests "Does a bean plant need water?" with plant A watered by the
  window and plant B not watered in a dark cupboard. Ask: what is not right? (Two things changed: the water AND the light, so we cannot
  tell which one mattered. Change one thing; keep the rest the same.)
- **Air:** do not test it by keeping any animal or plant without air. Teach it as the book does: you come up for air when you swim; a
  box for a small animal needs holes.
- **The toy boat (School Lab 1.2, Part (b)):** put a toy boat beside the plants. Give it no water, food or light for the same two
  weeks. It does not change. Non-living things do not need water, food or air. It floats ON water, but it does not NEED water.
- Looking at small animals: an adult handles them; a container with air holes, food and something juicy for water; shade; back where
  they came from soon; wash hands.

**The answer frame (for "What does this living thing need? Explain what you see."):**
1. What I see: "Plant B is ______ (wilting / pale and weak)."
2. What it is missing: "It does not have enough ______ (water)." or "It has no ______ (light), so it cannot make its own ______ (food)."
3. The rule: "Living things need water, food and air."

only." · "The cupboard plant had no air." · "The boat needs water because it floats."

**Key words to read together:** Part 1: water, wilting, oxygen. Part 2: food, energy, light.

## 11. Open questions for the teacher

1. **The order of the needs.** The book goes water, food, air. To give "plants make their own food" a whole part, the videos teach
   water and air in Part 1 and food in Part 2 (and always SAY the three in the book's order). Is that fine, or should Part 1 be water
   and food, and Part 2 air? (That split makes Part 1 run past 5 minutes unless the shrink or a Mark Sentence goes.)
2. **Key words.** The P3 plan lists six: WATER, FOOD, AIR, WILTING, ENERGY, OXYGEN. To keep three pills per part, the shelves are
   WATER · WILTING · OXYGEN and FOOD · ENERGY · LIGHT. So AIR is not a pill (it is a board label, in two Mark Sentences and in the
   chant), and LIGHT is added. Would you rather have four pills in Part 1 (adding AIR), or AIR in place of LIGHT in Part 2?
3. **Five Mark Sentence cards** (2 + 3). EP1 had three in all. Each costs about 11 s with the spoken cue and the holds. Too many, or
   right for this section (all three exam-box sentences of the P3 plan are in Part 2)?
4. **Basil's unfair test and the toy boat** are out of the videos for length and are on the teacher sheet. Would you like the unfair
   test back as a bonus Short ("HOLD ON! Is this test fair?"), or as a short Part 3?
5. **"A few hours" and "two weeks".** Please confirm both with real pots (or tell us the times you see), before the render.
6. **"All living things need air to live."** The book's sentence starts "This is because all living things need air to live." May
   we start at "All", shown as a caption, not as a Mark Sentence card?
7. **The mealworm.** It moves from the glass box to a jar with a holed lid for one day, then back to its tub on Day 32. Is a jar a
   good home to show, or should Room 2 simply be its own tub with a holed lid (and Pip's mistake be a tub lid with no holes)?
8. **The colours of the three choices** (WATER blue, FOOD orange, AIR green). In EP1 green meant LIVING and blue meant NON-LIVING.
   Is it fine to use blue and green again here with new meanings, or should AIR and WATER take other colours?
9. **The folding plant** in the dare is drawn but not named and not explained (Episode 3 does that). Basil touches one leaf with one
   fingertip. Is that the right amount to show?
10. **Round names.** Each part starts again at "Round one" because each part keeps its own score. Or should Part 2's round be
    "Round two"?
