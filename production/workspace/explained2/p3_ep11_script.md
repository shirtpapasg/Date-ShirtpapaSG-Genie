## 2. The spine and the split

EP10 asked: clear glass, frosted glass, a wooden door; which one lets only some light pass through? **Part 1** is in the living room of an HDB flat at dusk, the lights low, a test table set up: a small torch on a stand, a holder for one sheet, a white card behind it. Basil is too big to hide behind anything, so his toy robot hides for him. Sheet one, clear glass: the torch goes on, a bright spot lands on the card (most of the light passed through), and the robot behind the glass is clear. The book's words name it: transparent. Basil gets bold and pushes the torch closer for the next sheet, and the child stops him: in a good test, only the sheet changes. Then the dare is played as Round 1. Sheet two, frosted glass, beside the clear one: a soft, dim glow, and the robot is a blurry shape. The book's words: translucent. The child becomes the light and flies through both. Then Basil's robot goes behind a wooden door that is painted white, and the part ends on the question. **Part 2** is in the common corridor at night, at the flat's front door. The torch shines on the white door: the front is bright, but the card behind has no bright spot and no glow, and the robot is gone from view. Opaque: white is only its colour. Rubber, wood and metal fill the third row. Then three sheets stand in a row, three cards behind them: most, only some, none; and the whole board gets its name: transparency. The child becomes the light of the lamp inside the flat, and meets the door and the two windows. Pip's notes hold the trap in the book's own sentences: "this box is plastic, so it is transparent". Two plastic boxes, one clear and one cloudy, go under the torch and land on two different rows. The chant says the three words, and the dare hands over to choosing materials.

**Why the split is here:** the two words that sound alike and are mixed up most (transparent, translucent) are taught side by side in one part, on two sheets of the same material (glass), and EP10's dare is answered there. The third word gets a part with room for the two ideas pupils get wrong: opaque is not about colour, and the material's name does not decide (some plastics are transparent, some are translucent). The name of the property comes last, when all three results stand in a row and "how much light" can be seen.

## 3. Cast, the game and the conventions

- **Heart** narrates (Kokoro af_heart, speed 0.90, about 0.5 s between sentences, 0.6 s after a new term; softer and at about 0.85 in the two journeys and in every [LOOK CLOSER], with about 0.8 to 1.0 s between sentences). Never says a wrong idea. Never reads a Mark Sentence card or a goal card.
- **Pip** (purple #8B5CF6, careful), **Basil** (teal #14B8A6, bold), **Momo** (coral #FB7158, curious): the painted sprites, always in their aura discs, in their Character Voice Kit voices about 12% slower than default. Pose swaps: squash, hard cut, stretch, hop. When a buddy reads a card, the words light up as they are read.
- **Poses that exist** (`characters/sprites/poses/`): Basil: sit (talking), cheer, oops, surprised, arms crossed (no pointing, thinking or waving pose: Basil never points in this script; when he reads the goal cards or leads a card he does an inviting hop). Pip and Momo: sit, cheer, oops, surprised, arms crossed, pointing, thinking, waving. No new pose is needed.
- **The game:** cream answer cards with big numbers; fingers 1, 2, 3; arms crossed for HOLD ON!; one finger per point on the score hand. The buddies score a point on a lock-in round when two of the three are right; nothing in HOLD ON!; nothing in a Showdown built on a buddy's mistake. **Scores:** Part 1: HOLD ON! 1 : 0, Round 1 2 : 1 (You 2, Buddies 1). Part 2: Round 1 1 : 1, Showdown 3 : 1 (You 3, Buddies 1).
- **Colours.** This topic has three answers, so three fixed tag styles: **MOST LIGHT / TRANSPARENT** green, **ONLY SOME LIGHT / TRANSLUCENT** blue (the rulebook's two answer colours), and **NO LIGHT / OPAQUE** plain white with a dark-violet border and dark-violet letters. The third style is white on purpose: a dark tag would teach "opaque means dark". No red, no buddy colour and no sun yellow is used for an answer. The torch's light is a soft pale cream, never sun yellow (that is YOU).
- **The test table** must read clearly as what it is. From left to right, in one straight line on a table top: a small hand torch lying on a low wooden stand, its lens facing right, its back end on a painted line with the label **TORCH MARK**; a wooden holder with a slot, holding one sheet upright; a white card standing upright at the right, labelled **OUR CARD** on its foot. The space between the holder and the card is where the robot stands for the seeing test. The label **OUR TORCH TEST** is on the front of the table edge. Seen from the side for the light test (torch, sheet and card in a row) and from the torch's side, straight on, for the seeing test (we look through the sheet).
- **The sheets** are all the same size, each in the same thin wooden frame: a sheet of clear glass, a sheet of frosted glass, a piece of a wooden door painted white (white on its front, bare wood on its edges and back, with a small round door knob so it reads as a door), and for Part 2 two plastic lunch boxes of the same shape and size, one clear and one cloudy. They wait in a low wooden rack on the table.
- **The board OUR TORCH TEST:** a cream board on a low stand at the end of the table, three rows: **MOST LIGHT · ONLY SOME LIGHT · NO LIGHT**, each row with a place for picture tiles and a blank space for its word. When a sheet has been tested its tile hops low onto its row; when the book's word has been read it is stamped on the row in the row's colour.
- **Basil's robot** is EP1's wind-up robot (the same art: a small tin robot with a wind-up key, two round eyes, a red chest button and two yellow buttons). It is the "object placed behind" in every seeing test. It is always set down by an adult's hand or slid low with dust puffs.
- **Things do not float in the air.** The table, the board, the rack, the easel and the picture boards stand on the table or the ground, or slide low with dust puffs. An adult's hands set each sheet in the holder and switch the torch on.
- **Realism.** The torch is a small hand torch with batteries (never a phone, never a laser). Its lens faces along its beam. With the torch on, the beam is a soft pale cone that widens a little from the lens to the sheet; the room is dim enough for the beam and the card to read clearly, but never black: the buddies and the table can always be seen. **Clear glass:** the beam goes on past the sheet, just as bright, and makes a bright round spot with a clean edge on the card; a faint glint on the glass shows that a sheet is there. **Frosted glass:** the beam stops being a clean cone at the sheet; the sheet itself glows softly; behind it a wide, faint, soft-edged glow reaches the card; no clean spot. **The white door:** the beam ends on the door's front, which is brightly lit; behind the door there is no beam; the card has no bright spot and no glow and looks the same as the rest of the dim room. **No shadow is drawn anywhere and the word is never used** (shadows are P4): for this reason the robot is never on the table while the torch is on. For each seeing test the torch is switched off, the room light comes up a little, and an adult's hand sets the robot behind the sheet. Through clear glass the robot is sharp, every button clear. Through frosted glass it is a soft blur of its colours with no edges and no buttons (the kit's `soft` blur). Behind the door nothing of it shows. The clear plastic box behaves like the clear glass and the cloudy plastic box like the frosted glass; both boxes are empty and closed, standing on their sides in the holder so the torch shines through two flat walls. Label lines are plain lines with no arrowheads.

**Careful:** say "most light" for transparent, never "all the light". Say "only some light" for translucent and "cannot clearly see"; say "do not allow any light" for opaque and "cannot see". "Some plastics" are transparent and some are translucent, so do not decide by the material's name: look at the object. Shadows, reflection and "light travels in straight lines" are P4 (Light and Shadows): the video draws no shadow and does not use the word. The video does not say why frosted glass is frosted. The fair-test idea ("we change one thing only: the sheet") is ours, for the torch test; the words "fair test" come later in the book (p. 118) and are not used here. Torches only in class: never a laser pointer, never at eyes, and nobody looks at the Sun. Glass sheets are handled by an adult; for the class activity use plastic, paper, wood and metal things, not glass.

## 11. Choices I made

1. **The dare is answered in Part 1,** as Round 1 at about 2:25, with the same three boards, torches and cards. The dare's third card, the wooden door, becomes Part 1's cliffhanger and Part 2's first round.
2. **Spine: OUR TORCH TEST** (build-and-reveal): a board with three rows, MOST LIGHT · ONLY SOME LIGHT · NO LIGHT. Each row gets its thing, then its word. The reveal is double: the full board gets one name (transparency), and two boxes of one material land on two rows.
3. **The split is by word, not by page** (the book has one page): transparent and translucent together in Part 1, on two sheets of glass, because they are the pair that sounds alike and gets mixed up; opaque, the name of the property and "some plastics" in Part 2. So **"transparency" is not said in Part 1**: Heart says "how much light" there, and the word arrives in Part 2 when all three results stand in a row. 4. **Five [LOOK CLOSER] moments**, one for every key idea: transparent (the torch on clear glass), translucent (the torch on frosted glass, beside the clear glass), opaque (the torch on a white door), transparency (three in a row) and some plastics (two boxes).
