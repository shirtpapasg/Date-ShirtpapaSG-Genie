## 1. The School Lab path a pupil follows

1. Open TheOneApp. It opens on the **Questions** screen.
2. Tap **"School Lab"** in the bottom bar (button `#nav-lab`, index.html:1548; `window.enterLab`, index.html:1804). On a wide screen, the lab building's door in the scenery opens it too.
3. If asked, sign in: the page is members only ("Members sign in here.").
4. The heading is **"Every experiment in the books."**, with one folder per level: "Primary 3", "Primary 4", "Primary 5", "Primary 6". **Primary 3 is open; tap "Primary 6"** (its line reads "19 coming soon").
5. Inside: "Chapter 1 / Photosynthesis", "Chapter 2 / Energy Conversion", "Chapter 3 / Interaction of Forces", "Chapter 4 / Interactions Within the Environment", "Chapter 5 / Surviving in the Environment". `api/lab.js:47–48` numbers the chapters in the order they first appear. A built lab's row reads, for example, "1.3A  Leaf Factory · In review · Experiment · about 30 minutes · runs in 3D". A planned row reads "2.1  Forms of Energy · Coming soon · Experiment".
6. Tap the experiment (Admin or Adult mode only, until it is signed off). The **Briefing** shows "Photosynthesis · 1.3A · about 30 minutes", the title, "💡 The big idea", "🔭 What we are finding out", "⚠️ Stay safe" where there is one (1.2, 1.3B, 1.4), and the line "Predict first. The moment you make a prediction, **Let's test it now!** lights up and the 3D bench opens for that part." (lab.html:862).
7. The part screen: "Part A · predict, then test it on the bench". 1.2 differs: "Part A · predict, then measure on the bench", "Part B · predict, then measure on the bench", "Part C · predict, then trace on the bench". Next comes the step screen: the set-up line, then 🔮 **Predict**. Tap an answer.
8. **"Let's test it now!"** appears under the answer (`sim.label`). Press it.
9. The 3D bench opens full-screen on that part's pill. **"← Back to the steps"** (lab.html:590) closes it. The next screen asks 👀 **"What did you see?"**. P6 asks this as a graph-reading question ("Look at the points. As the food given increases, the distance run…"). Beside it, the panel **"📊 From your bench"** shows the pupil's own readings, graph and a photo of the bench (lab.html:1437; read by `labs/_shared/lab-results.js`, which lab.html:1237 adds to every bench). A "✨ Worth knowing" note closes the part.

**Inside every P6 bench** (one engine for all seven; `STEPS` at labs/p6-1-1a/index.html:868) the pills read, in order: "Intro", "🧠 Before I Start", one pill per part (for example "🧪 Leave One Out"), "🏆 Challenge", "Wrap-up". Here is what P6 adds (the "P6 differences" in `labs/BRIEF-P6.md` §1 and §4, as built):

- **A seven-scene welcome tour**, shown once per code (`sm-tour-<code>`): "Look Around", "Choose your steps!", "Your Prediction", "Select Variable(s)", "Run the Test", "Study the Results", "Average and Next" (`TOUR`, labs/p6-1-1a/index.html:887–896). 1.5 starts with a tap task, so its tour drops "Select Variable(s)" and ends on "Answer and Next".
- **Prediction reasons.** Each of the three options carries its own reason, and the wrong reasons are real mix-ups. The tour says: "Every step starts with a prediction. Tap the one you believe, and its reason." The bench for a part stays closed until a prediction is tapped. Section 5 lists every wrong reason.
- **Variables chips.** The box at the top of the readings has three rows: "CHANGED VARIABLE", "MEASURED VARIABLE" and "CONSTANT VARIABLES (KEPT THE SAME)" (`VTAG`, labs/p6-1-1a/index.html:877, marked "the full terms pupils must use (teacher, 2026-10-10)"). Each row offers three choices. A wrong pick bounces with "Which of these did you change on purpose?", "Which of these did you measure each time?" or "Which of these stayed the same in every run?". The run button reads "Set the variables first" until all three are right, so an unfair test never runs. The BRIEF calls the chips "Changed · Measured · Kept the same". The bench's own words are the longer ones above, so match those on screen.
- **Run buttons** carry the lab's own word: "▶ Run" (1.1A, 1.2 Part A, 1.3A, 1.3B Parts A–B, 1.4 Part C), "▶ Check" (1.1B), "▶ Measure" (1.2 Part B, 1.5 Part C), "▶ Test" (1.3B Part C, 1.4 Parts A–B), "▶ Trace" (1.2 Part C, 1.5 Part B) and "▶ Try" (1.5 Part A).
- **Repeat and average.** Each setting takes up to 3 runs (`MAXRUNS`). Most parts need 3 settings × 2 runs (1.1B needs 4 or 3 settings × 1 run). The results table shows Run 1–3 and the Average, with a dash in empty cells. About 1 repeat run in 20 is an odd reading (`OUTLIER_RATE` 0.05), and the button says "Reject the odd reading first" until the pupil throws it out.
- **A graph the pupil finishes.** The points appear first. The pupil then taps the trend from "increases / stays the same / decreases", with a hint after each wrong tap ("Compare the first point with the last point…"), and the line draws. The BRIEF says "rises / falls / stays level", but the built benches use "increases / stays the same / decreases"; use the bench words on screen. Category variables get a bar chart instead (1.1B Parts A and C, 1.3A Part A, 1.3B Part C).
- **Tap-task parts have no variables box and no graph.** These are 1.2 Part C (tracing cards), 1.5 Part A (choosing the arrow) and 1.5 Part B (tracing paths). Here the bench times the pupil, which is not a science result, so `variablesShown` is false and `graph` is null in the JSON.
- **The Challenge has 3 multiple-choice questions, then 3 typed questions.** Each multiple-choice question carries a cyan topic badge. **On every P6 bench one of these badges reads "The trap"** (for example labs/p6-1-1a/index.html:834). The typed questions are one meaning [1] and two explanations [2]. The bench sends them to the app (`lab:mark`, lab.html:1486–1490), and the server marks them against the record's `written` points (`api/lab.js`, which never sends the points to the page). The BRIEF asks for "five quiz questions"; the built benches have 3 + 3.
- **Steps-page depth.** The six `labs-review.js` labs have, per part: the set-up line, Predict, the bench task, one "What did you see?" graph question and a "Worth knowing" note. Their written questions are only in the bench Challenge. **1.2 is fuller.** It has an explain question in Part A, four fill-in blanks under "Let's conclude" and one "Now use it" question, plus its three typed Challenge questions.
- **Timing.** The records say "about 30 minutes". The BRIEF asks for "three parts, forty to forty-five minutes".
- **Folders.** BRIEF-P6 §5 names `labs/photo/`, `labs/convert/` and so on. The built folders are `labs/p6-1-1a/` … `labs/p6-1-5/` and `labs/sunline/`.

**Part labels.** The app shows "Part A", "Part B", "Part C"; the videos write "Part (a): <title>". `labs_p6.json` gives both (`label` and `appLabel`).

- Big idea (app): All living things need food. Food is a source of energy. Energy from food is used for life processes such as moving, growing and repairing. This is true even for a living thing that stays still, like a mould.
- **Part (a): On the wheel** (bench pill "🐹 On the Wheel"; eyebrow "Part A · predict, then test it on the bench"). Predict: "If the mouse is given more food, what happens to the distance it runs?" Graph: line graph: Distance run · m against Food given · g. Observe: "Look at the points. As the food given increases, the distance run…" (increases). Takeaway: Food gives the mouse energy. - **Part (b): Growing up** (bench pill "🐛 Growing Up"; eyebrow "Part B · predict, then test it on the bench"). Does: Give the caterpillar different amounts of leaf each day and measure the mass it gains after three days. Graph: line graph: Mass gained · g against Leaf given each day · g. Observe: "Look at the points. As the leaf given each day increases, the mass gained…" (increases). ## 5. The bench Challenge questions as a mix-up source (the seven built labs)

Positive-language note: every P6 bench shows a cyan badge **"The trap"** on one of its three multiple-choice Challenge questions (labs/p6-1-1a/index.html:834, p6-1-1b:835, sunline:840, p6-1-3a:832, p6-1-3b:833, p6-1-4:829, p6-1-5:849). Never film or quote that badge. Say "Lots of students mix this up". Each item below gives the question, the right option, the wrong options (each a real mix-up) and the bench's "why". The typed questions' do-not-accept lines are mix-ups as well.

### 1.1A Fuel for Life
- **Why do all living things need food?** (badge "Energy") Right: "Food is their source of energy for life processes." Mix-ups: "Food keeps them from feeling bored." / "Food is needed only by animals that move." / "Food makes them heavier, and that is its only use.". Why: Food is a source of energy. Living things use that energy for life processes such as moving, growing and repairing.
- **A pupil says, "Only living things that can move need food for energy." Is the pupil right?** (badge "The trap") Right: "No. A mould never moves, but it still needs food for energy." Mix-ups: "Yes. Only living things that can move need food for energy." / "Yes. A mould grows without needing food." / "Yes. Growing does not use energy, only moving and running do.". Why: The mould covered more area when it was given more food. Every living thing needs food for energy, whether it moves or not.
- **Why was each amount of food tested more than once?** (badge "Fair test") Right: "Repeating and averaging makes the result more reliable." Mix-ups: "To use up all the food in the bowl before the next test." / "To tire the animal out." / "So that each run gives a different conclusion.". Why: One reading can be a little high or a little low. Repeating and taking the average gives a more reliable result.
- Wrong prediction reasons (each is a real mix-up): "Running does not use energy." · "A full mouse feels too sleepy to move." · "Caterpillars grow by themselves." · "Too much food makes it too full to grow." · "A mould makes its own food from air and water." · "A mould only needs moisture, not food."
- Do-not-accept lines from the typed questions: "food (an example only, with no meaning given)" · "something that uses energy" · "something that makes living things heavier" · "energy (only repeats the words of the question)" · "the mouse was happier or less hungry, so it ran more" · "food gives the mouse strength or power (the word energy is needed)" · "it ran further because it had more food (only repeats the question)" · "the food made the wheel turn faster" · "the leaf stays in its stomach, so it is heavier" · "it ate more so it got fatter (no mention of growth)" · "it gained more mass because it had more leaf (only repeats the question)" · "caterpillars grow by themselves"

### 1.1B Energy Labels
- **A pupil says, "A food that weighs more always has a higher energy value than a lighter food." Is the pupil right?** (badge "The trap") Right: "No. The type matters: 10 g of peanut has more energy than 10 g of apple." Mix-ups: "Yes. A heavier food always has a higher energy value than a lighter food." / "Yes. The sweeter food always has the highest energy value." / "Yes. A food label shows only the total mass of the food, not its energy value.". Why: Different foods store different amounts of energy in each gram. Mass alone does not tell you the energy value.
- **The same snack is served as a 20 g portion and a 40 g portion. Which has more energy in total?** (badge "Portion") Right: "The 40 g portion, because there is more of the same food." Mix-ups: "The 20 g portion, because a smaller portion is packed with energy." / "Both are the same, because it is the same food." / "Neither. Portion size has nothing to do with energy.". Why: A bigger portion of the same food has more energy in total. The value for each gram stays the same.
- **To compare two foods fairly from their labels, what must you do?** (badge "Fair comparison") Right: "Read the same column, such as "per gram", on both labels." Mix-ups: "Read whichever number looks biggest on each of the two labels." / "Compare the colours and the pictures on the two labels." / "Taste both foods first.". Why: A fair comparison keeps everything the same except the food. Reading the same amount, such as each gram, on every label does that.
- Wrong prediction reasons (each is a real mix-up): "The heaviest-looking food always has the highest energy value." · "All foods have the same energy value. Only their taste differs." · "Energy value depends only on the type of food, not the portion size." · "A bigger portion is more filling, so it has less energy in each gram." · "A sweeter food always has a higher energy value, so taste is a reliable guide." · "A guess is as good as a measurement, so there is no need to read a label."
- Do-not-accept lines from the typed questions: "how heavy the food is" · "how sweet the food tastes" · "how healthy the food is" · "the number on the label (no mention of energy)" · "a bigger portion has more energy in each gram" · "a bigger portion is more filling" · "it had more energy because the portion was bigger (only repeats the question)" · "a bigger portion tastes stronger" · "a sweeter food always has more energy" · "a label is easier to read than tasting" · "the biscuit is bigger than the banana" · "a label is better than a guess (only repeats the question)"

### 1.2 Back to the Sun
- **A pupil says, "Animals get their energy from the food they eat, not from the Sun." What is missing from this idea?** (badge "The trap") Right: "That energy can be traced back to the Sun, so animals depend on it too." Mix-ups: "Nothing. Animals get all their energy from food, so the Sun does not matter." / "Animals that eat other animals do not depend on the Sun at all." / "Animals get their energy from the soil and the water instead.". Why: An animal gets energy from its food, but that energy first came from the Sun, trapped by a plant. Every trace leads back to the Sun.
- **In the chain grass → grasshopper → frog, which has the least energy available?** (badge "Food chains") Right: "The frog, because only part of the energy passes on." Mix-ups: "The grass, because it gives all its energy away." / "They all have the same, because energy only moves along." / "The frog has the most, because it is the biggest.". Why: About 10 units at the grass became about 3 at the grasshopper and about 1 at the frog. Only part passes on at each link.
- **A shark never leaves the sea and never touches a plant. Does its energy still trace back to the Sun?** (badge "Tracing") Right: "Yes. Its food chain starts with living things that trap light energy." Mix-ups: "No. The shark makes its own energy, so it does not need the Sun." / "No. The shark gets its energy from the sea water itself, not from food." / "No. Only land animals depend on the Sun, because plants grow on land.". Why: The shark eats fish, which eat smaller living things, back to those that trap light energy from the Sun. Every chain starts there.
- Wrong prediction reasons (each is a real mix-up): "Plants always store the same amount of energy, whatever the light." · "Bright light stops a plant from making food." · "Energy only moves along, so the same amount is at every link." · "Each animal gets all the energy of the one before it, plus its own." · "All living things get what they need from the ground in the end." · "The biggest living thing holds the most energy."
- Do-not-accept lines from the typed questions: "a list of animals" · "a chain that holds food" · "animals standing in a line" · "the grass gets hotter" · "the lamp gives the grass food" · "the grass eats the light" · "the energy disappears" · "the energy goes back to the Sun" · "the energy goes into the soil" · "the frog is smaller"

### 1.3A Leaf Factory
- **A pupil says, "Plants get their food from the soil, the same way they get water." Is the pupil right?** (badge "The trap") Right: "No. Plants make their own food. From the soil they take in water and mineral salts." Mix-ups: "Yes. Plants get their food from the soil, in the same way that they get their water." / "Yes. The roots absorb food that is already made." / "Yes. Soil is the food of a plant.". Why: A plant makes its own food in its leaves, using light energy, carbon dioxide and water. Soil supplies water and mineral salts, not food.
- **How does carbon dioxide mainly enter a leaf?** (badge "Getting in") Right: "Through the tiny openings on the underside of the leaf." Mix-ups: "Through the roots, with the water." / "Straight through the thick waxy top surface of the leaf." / "Through the stalk that joins the leaf to the stem.". Why: With the openings open about 3.0 units entered each minute. Sealed, it fell to about 0.2 units.
- **The tiny openings under a leaf are sealed, but it has light and water. How much starch does it make?** (badge "Sealed openings") Right: "Almost none, because almost no carbon dioxide can get in." Mix-ups: "The same as before, because the leaf still has light and water." / "More than before, because the sealed leaf keeps all its light in." / "Some, because carbon dioxide can come in through the roots.". Why: With the openings sealed only about 0.2 units of carbon dioxide entered. Without carbon dioxide the leaf made almost no starch.
- Wrong prediction reasons (each is a real mix-up): "Water and carbon dioxide alone can make some food." · "The leaf can use light it stored earlier in the day." · "Carbon dioxide can pass straight through the surface of the leaf." · "The leaf pulls harder for the gas when it is blocked." · "A leaf can only give out a fixed amount of oxygen." · "The leaf uses itself up as time goes on."
- Do-not-accept lines from the typed questions: "plants taking in food from the soil" · "plants breathing" · "plants taking in sunlight (no mention of making food)" · "plants growing towards the light" · "the leaf was too cold without the lamp" · "plants rest or sleep in the dark" · "the leaf used light that it stored earlier" · "there was no starch because there was no light (only repeats the question, no mention of making food)" · "the lamp makes the oxygen" · "the leaf gives out carbon dioxide" · "the oxygen is air that was already in the water" · "a longer time gives more oxygen (only repeats the question)"

### 1.3B Light and Gas
- **A pupil says, "The gas in the bubbles is carbon dioxide, which the plant breathes out." Is the pupil right?** (badge "The trap") Right: "No. The bubbles are oxygen, given out during photosynthesis." Mix-ups: "Yes. The gas in the bubbles is carbon dioxide." / "Yes. Carbon dioxide slows photosynthesis down, so it is pushed out." / "Yes. Plants only give out carbon dioxide.". Why: During photosynthesis a plant takes in carbon dioxide and gives out oxygen. The bubbles collected are oxygen.
- **Why does more dissolved carbon dioxide give more bubbles?** (badge "Carbon dioxide") Right: "Carbon dioxide is needed for photosynthesis, so more of it speeds it up." ### 1.5 Energy Chain

Why: An arrow points from the eaten to the eater, showing the direction in which energy passes. The chain must start with the producer.
- **A chain has 10.0 units of energy at the grass, 2.8 at the grasshopper and 0.8 at the frog. Why does the frog have so little?** (badge "Energy") Right: "Only part passes on at each link. The rest is given out as heat." Mix-ups: "The frog is small, so it cannot hold much energy." / "The frog did not eat enough grasshoppers to fill up." / "The energy should be the same at every link, so the readings are wrong.". Why: Each living thing uses energy for its life processes and gives energy out as heat. Only what is left can pass to the next link.
- **Why does every food chain start with a producer?** (badge "Producers") Right: "A producer makes its own food, using light energy." Mix-ups: "A producer is the smallest living thing." / "A producer has less energy than the consumers that eat it." / "A producer eats the consumers.". Why: Plants are producers. They trap light energy and store it in the food they make, and that energy then passes along the chain.
