# Style Guide — "TheOneApp, Explained" · Primary 6 series

This guide carries over **every** rule of the P5 style guide (`../P5/plan/01_style_guide_p5.md`, which carries the P3 and P4 rules) and the teacher's standing rules, then sets the P6 adjustments for 11–12-year-olds in their PSLE year. Where a P6 rule changes a P5 rule, the P6 rule wins for P6 episodes. The real-photo convention (P5 guide §11) applies unchanged: photos for the real thing, drawings for the mechanism; teacher's photos first, then public-domain or CC-licensed photos with credits; the `photo()` helper and the per-brief photo shot list.

---

## 1. Voice

- A warm, exact teacher talking to ONE 11-year-old who will be marked on how they explain. Sentences of one idea, up to about 20 words. Cause → process → result in every explanation: "The box moves across the rough floor. Frictional force between the box and the floor opposes its motion, so the box slows down and stops."
- **Say the science word, then use it.** No baby words for P6: "kinetic energy" (never "moving energy"), "gravitational force" (never "gravity pulls" without the full term once), "frictional force", "elastic spring force", "potential energy", "producer / consumer / predator / prey", "photosynthesis", "respiration", "chlorophyll", "population / community / habitat / environment", "adaptation".
- **Forms of energy are named as the textbook names them:** kinetic energy, potential energy (due to height, due to stretching or compressing, in chemicals), light energy, electrical energy, sound energy, heat energy. The book adds the long names once ("also known as gravitational potential energy / elastic potential energy / chemical potential energy") and the teacher's recitation table uses them, so a script may say each long name **once**, with the book's plain name first; the Exam corner and key words use the plain names (the 2023 syllabus says the specific long terms are not required).
- Never: particles, molecules, atoms, newtons or any unit of force (say "the reading on the force meter" if a number is needed), acceleration, "energy is used up" (it is converted), "cold gets in", "plants breathe", "plants eat sunlight".
- Hedge words stay where the book has them ("may", "usually", "some").
- Numbers are real: calories per gram from the book (4 for carbohydrate or protein, 9 for fat), 95% of Singapore's electricity from natural gas, 90% of food imported and the 30 by 30 goal, the spring table (100 g → 10 cm, 200 g → 15 cm, 300 g → 20 cm), the launcher table (surface Y 6, 5, 6 cm; surface Z 1, 2, 1 cm). Never invent a reading.

## 2. Positive language (unchanged)

No "cheat", "trap", "stupid", "kill", "dead", "die", "gross". Science terms stay in their real meaning: extinct, predator, prey, decomposer (only if the textbook has it), pollution, deforestation, depletion, "remains of plants and animals" (fossil fuels), "wear and tear". "Die out" → "disappear forever"; "kills" → "harms"; "dead leaves" → "fallen leaves".

## 3. Pacing and narrator (unchanged)

Kit timings; Heart voice 3; one visual change every 2–3 s; three touches per idea. P6 watch-list words for the aligner: *photosynthesis, chlorophyll, respiration, iodine, calories, carbohydrate, protein, kinetic, potential, elastic, frictional, gravitational, lubricant, streamlined, satellite, trampoline, plunger, slingshot, uranium, biofuel, renewable, deforestation, depletion, reforestation, mangrove, mudskipper, plover, hatchling, quadrat, Galileo, van Helmont*.

## 4. Structure (3:45–5:00, about 520–680 authored words)

Same skeleton: Hook (≤ 35 words) → welcome with **three goals** → body beats → Try-it → Pause and predict (**four options**, 4-second countdown) → Exam corner (≤ 3 lines) → atomic habit (≤ 30 words) → mind map → one offline ask → goodbye.

P6 changes:
- **One "Explain like the marker wants" beat per episode:** a real structured-question answer, built live on screen in three chips: *cause → process → result*, in the teacher's recitation wording where one exists.
- **Data beats read like the exam:** a table or graph from the book's Let's Apply (bubbles against distance, spring length against mass, distance moved on two surfaces, electricity against blade length), read aloud with the trend and its range ("as the mass increases from 100 g to 300 g, the length increases from 10 cm to 20 cm").
- **Variables chips** (*changed · kept the same · measured*) on every investigation, plus "why repeat three times" once in the forces chapter.
- **Energy-conversion chains are drawn as the book draws them:** boxes joined by "is converted to" arrows, the object named under each box ("potential energy (stretched rubber band) → kinetic energy (moving toy jumper)"), with "+ heat energy / + sound energy" where the book adds them.
- **Food chains:** arrows point from the eaten to the eater and mean "energy is transferred to"; a chain always begins with a producer; the Sun is drawn at the start but outside the chain.
- **Tail length:** exam + habit1 + habit2 ≤ 130 spoken words. Hook ≤ 35. Episode ≤ 680 authored words.
- Close: one offline ask fit for a P6 ("Write the energy conversion for one thing in your kitchen", "Find one example of frictional force being useful at home and one where it is a nuisance").

## 5. The teacher's standing rules (checked before every render)

1. **Definition accuracy:** FULL wording from `definitions_p6.md`; prefer the textbook (Chapters 1–3 are verified against the book's text) → the teacher's recitation table → the syllabus outcome → standard wording flagged "textbook unverified" (Chapters 4–5, whose textbook pages could not be read).
2. **Plain label lines**, no arrowheads, no numbered markers. Arrows only for motion, force direction, energy conversion ("is converted to"), food-chain transfer and the Earth–Moon pull.
3. **Wrong statements:** ✗ at the start only, no strikeout, only on statements that are actually false ("✗ Energy is used up").
4. **Positive language.** 5. **Realism** (§8) and **photos** (P5 guide §11).
6. **Every named skill or term becomes a key word, full name** ("KINETIC ENERGY", "POTENTIAL ENERGY", "ENERGY CONVERSION", "FRICTIONAL FORCE", "GRAVITATIONAL FORCE", "ELASTIC SPRING FORCE", "MAGNETIC FORCE", "PHOTOSYNTHESIS", "RESPIRATION", "CHLOROPHYLL", "PRODUCER", "CONSUMER", "PREDATOR", "PREY", "FOOD WEB", "POPULATION", "COMMUNITY", "HABITAT", "ADAPTATION", "DEFORESTATION", "GLOBAL WARMING", "RENEWABLE").
7. **4-second "Think!" countdown.** 8. **School Lab card + spoken link** where a built P6 lab matches (`app_p6.md`: only seven P6 benches are built today, all in Chapter 1 and all in review; the rest ship with `lab: null` and a home try-it). 9. **Mind map matching the Mind Lab poster** (P6 posters: Photosynthesis, Energy Conversion, Forces, Living Things & Their Environment; speak "&" as "and"). 10. **Bigger objects.** 11. **Welcome with three goals and a goodbye.** 12. **Free tools, files split over about 14 MB.**

## 6. P6 adjustments for 11–12-year-olds

- **PSLE answer frames** are practised: "state the force", "explain why the block stopped", "describe the relationship", "give a reason why the experiment was repeated", "do you agree? explain". Each episode's Exam corner holds at least one such answer in full.
- **Both themes link back:** every Interactions episode names the energy conversion involved once (friction → heat), and every Energy episode names the force involved once (the falling apple and gravitational force), as the book's Let's Connect boxes do.
- **Mass and weight stay apart:** mass is the amount of matter and does not change with location; weight is the gravitational force on the object and may change from place to place; a heavier object (greater mass) has greater gravitational force acting on it and so greater weight.
- **Photosynthesis and respiration are taught side by side** with the book's comparison table (where, when, gases in and out), and "plants respire all the time" is said plainly.
- **Fossil fuels trace back to the Sun;** "non-renewable" means cannot be replaced once used up; renewable sources depend on the weather and need land and money (the book's balanced view).
- **Human impact is taught with Singapore's own responses** (Zero Waste Nation, recycling bins, reforestation, conservation of endangered species, 30 by 30 food goal) and with the sea-turtle hatchling and mangrove stories from the labs and the theme closure.
- **Concrete Singapore anchors:** a hawker-centre plate and its calories, the MRT's regenerative energy (description only), the Marina Barrage, a school field on sports day, bicycle brakes on the park connector, anti-slip strips at the void deck, a spring rider at the playground, Sungei Buloh mangroves and mudskippers, the mangrove plovers, Pulau Ubin, the Zero Waste Nation bins, NEWater.
- **US bridges:** Grade 5–6, a food label on a cereal box, a bald eagle food chain, a skateboard ramp, a baseball bat and ball, hiking boots on a smooth rock, a trampoline, Yellowstone wolves and elk (populations), a sea-turtle beach in Florida, recycling day.
- **Safety line and "Ask an adult" badge:** iodine solution (stains, adult handles it), the Bunsen burner and boiling water (teacher demonstration), slingshots and launchers ("never aim at anyone"), bicycles and brakes ("helmet on"), any home test with batteries only.
- **Characters:** never the textbook's cast (Ali, Belle, Chen, Devi, Mr Bala, Mrs Lim, Siti, Elias). Use "a boy in class", "your friend", "you".

## 8. Drawing quality and realism (P6 additions in bold)

All P3–P5 rules hold. P6 additions: **a leaf shows its tiny openings only when the beat is about gases; chlorophyll is "the green substance in the leaf", not a drawn organelle; iodine solution is brown and turns blue-black on starch; the bubble-count set-up is a water plant in a flask with a torch at distance d; the willow tree of van Helmont grows while the soil mass stays the same; a battery-bulb circuit is a closed loop; the falling apple keeps its potential energy decreasing and kinetic energy increasing with height labels; a slingshot's band is stretched then released; a fan's blades spin; a skateboard ramp has points X, Y, Z marked; forces are drawn as one arrow each, labelled by name, from the point they act on, in the direction they act (gravitational force always straight down; frictional force opposite to the motion along the surface; elastic spring force toward the spring's original shape; magnetic attraction between unlike poles and repulsion between like poles); a spring's length is measured with a ruler beside it; a feather falls slowly with the air's frictional force drawn upward; tides: the Moon's pull bulges the water; a satellite orbits the Earth; a food chain is a row of pictures joined by arrows from eaten to eater, the producer first; a food web is several chains sharing organisms; a habitat scene (pond, mangrove, garden) shows its living and non-living factors; adaptations are drawn on the real animal (a long beak, webbed feet, a cactus spine); deforestation shows stumps and bare soil, pollution shows litter or smoke, never a dead animal.**

## 9. Pre-render checklist (P6)

1. Every definition line matches `definitions_p6.md` word for word; "textbook unverified" lines (Chapters 4–5) checked by the teacher.
2. Forms of energy named as the book names them; long names at most once each; never "energy is used up".
3. Cause → process → result explanations in recitation wording; forces named in full; mass and weight kept apart.
4. Variables chips on every investigation; data beats read with trend and range.
5. No numbered markers, no arrowheads on label lines, no strikeout, ✗ only on false lines.
6. Positive language sweep.
7. Key words with full names; cue words in stripped form.
8. Three goals, welcome, four-option prediction quiz passing the four rules, lab card + spoken line (or `lab: null`), mind map matching the Mind Lab poster, one offline ask, goodbye.
9. Realism sweep (§8) and photo sweep (P5 guide §11).
10. Layout audit clean. 11. Safety badges (§6). 12. Lab on the card is built and signed off. 13. Tail ≤ 130 words; hook ≤ 35; episode ≤ 680. 14. One Singapore anchor and one US bridge spoken in a named beat.
