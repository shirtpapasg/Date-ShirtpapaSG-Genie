## Big idea
Magnetic force is the force exerted by magnets on magnetic materials and other magnets; magnets attract magnetic materials such as iron and steel; unlike poles attract and like poles repel; magnetic force acts even when the objects are not in contact.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "Magnets attract all metals." → Magnets attract magnetic materials such as iron and steel; copper is not attracted (p. 53; P3 Magnets). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- "Magnetic force acts only when the magnet touches the object." → Magnetic force can act even when the objects are not in contact (p. 53). **Becomes quiz option C.**
- "Unlike poles repel / like poles attract (mixed up)." → Unlike poles attract, like poles repel (p. 53). **Becomes quiz option B.**
- "A floating magnet has no gravitational force on it." → Gravitational force acts on all objects all the time (p. 61, preview of EP 15). **Becomes quiz option D.**
- "Only one magnet pushes or pulls." → Each magnet exerts a magnetic force on the other (p. 53: "each magnet exerts…").

## Exam wording box
1. The force exerted by magnets on magnetic materials and other magnets is known as magnetic force.
