## Big idea
Gravitational force is the pull that the Earth exerts on all objects; it acts all the time, on still objects, objects moving up and objects in water; objects have weight because gravitational force pulls them downwards; mass is the amount of matter and does not change with location, weight may change from place to place; greater mass, greater gravitational force, greater weight; the Moon's pull causes tides.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "Mass changes in space." → Mass does not change with location; only weight may change (p. 64). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- "A still object has no force on it." → Gravitational force acts on objects all the time, including objects that are not moving (p. 61). **Becomes quiz option A.**
- "The throw keeps pushing the ball upwards after it leaves the hand." → After release, gravitational force acts downwards on it and it eventually falls (p. 61). **Quiz option C.**
- "Gravitational force can pull upwards." → It pulls objects downwards, towards the Earth (p. 60, p. 63). **Quiz option D.**
- "Mass and weight mean the same." → Mass is a measure of the amount of matter; weight is caused by gravitational force (p. 64).
- "Gravitational force only acts on falling objects." → It acts on objects moving upwards and in water too (p. 61).

## Exam wording box
1. Gravitational force is the pull that the Earth exerts on all objects.
2. Mass does not change with location, while weight may change from place to place.
3. Structured answer (cause → process → result): The watermelon has a greater mass than the apple, so a greater gravitational force acts on it. Hence, the watermelon has a greater weight.
