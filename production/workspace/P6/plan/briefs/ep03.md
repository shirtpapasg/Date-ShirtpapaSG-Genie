## Big idea
Photosynthesis is the process whereby plants use water, carbon dioxide and light to produce sugar and oxygen; light is absorbed by chlorophyll, the green substance usually found in leaves; leaves take in carbon dioxide and give out oxygen through tiny openings; excess sugar is stored as starch in leaves, roots or stems.

## Misconceptions to bust
- Plants get their food from the soil → the roots take in water from the soil; the plant makes its own food, sugar, in its leaves (rows 1.1, 1.2; lab 1.3A Challenge mix-up). **This is the `tip` beat.**
- Water alone lets a leaf make food → photosynthesis needs water, carbon dioxide and light together (row 1.2; lab 1.3A Part (a)). **Quiz option C.**
- A leaf can make food without taking in carbon dioxide, or gives out no oxygen when blocked → leaves take in carbon dioxide and give out oxygen through tiny openings; blocked openings stop photosynthesis (row 1.22). **Quiz options A and D.**
- Photosynthesis produces starch → it produces sugar; the excess sugar is converted to starch (row 1.24a; app mix-up 41; `labs_p6.json` 1.3A caution).
- Plants eat sunlight / plants breathe → chlorophyll absorbs (traps) light; never "eat" or "breathe" (style §1).
- Chlorophyll is one of the things photosynthesis "needs" alongside water, carbon dioxide and light → the book's needs are water, carbon dioxide and light; chlorophyll absorbs the light (row 1.3; app mix-up 41 note).

## Exam wording box
1. Photosynthesis is a process whereby plants use water, carbon dioxide and light to produce sugar and oxygen.
2. Structured answer: How does a leaf make food? The chlorophyll absorbs light (cause), the leaf uses light energy, water and carbon dioxide to make sugar (process), oxygen is given out and excess sugar is stored as starch (result).
