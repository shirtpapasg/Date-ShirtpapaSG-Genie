# EP 10 — The ramp test: height, speed and the energy that "disappears"

## Big idea
On a ramp, potential energy converts to kinetic energy on the way down and back on the way up; some kinetic energy is converted to other forms (heat, sound) because of frictional force, so the skateboarder cannot reach the same height; the wound-up toy's potential energy becomes kinetic and sound energy; a fair test changes one variable (ramp height) and measures distance.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "She used up all her energy." → Energy is converted, not used up: some kinetic energy was converted to other forms (2.45; mix-up 33; answer key Set 2 Q39b). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- "Change several things at once and compare." → Change only one variable (Let's Apply Q4). **Becomes quiz options A and D.**
- "To test mass, use balls at different heights." → Keep the height the same. **Quiz option B.**
- "All the kinetic energy becomes potential energy while she is still moving up." → Only some, until she stops for a moment at Z (mix-up 34). Shown in `ramp` and `graph`.
- "A wound-up toy has kinetic energy before release." → Potential energy until it is released (lab 2.1 planned mix-up). Shown in `toy`.
- "A heavier toy car always travels further." → Not what this test investigates; the car is kept the same (lab 2.2B planned mix-up). Description note.
- "Raise the ramp" (vague). → Say exactly what to change: the height of the ramp where the car starts (mix-up 37).

## Exam wording box
1. When an object is moving, some kinetic energy is converted to heat energy due to the frictional force between the object and the surface it moves on.
2. Structured answer (cause → process → result): Frictional force acted on her as she moved. Some of her kinetic energy was converted to heat and sound energy, so less was converted back to potential energy, and she could not reach the height of X.
