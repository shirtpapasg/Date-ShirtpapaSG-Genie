## Big idea
A population is all the organisms of the same kind living in a habitat; a community is the different populations living together in the same habitat; organisms in a community depend on one another (interdependence).

## Misconceptions to bust
- A population is the number of organisms ("twenty mudskippers are twenty populations") → a population is all the organisms of one kind; count kinds, not individuals. **Becomes quiz option C.**
- Plants are not part of a population or community → plant kinds are populations too (the grass, the mangrove trees). **Becomes quiz option D.**
- A habitat with a low count has no community → a small population still forms part of the community (lab 4.3 planned mix-up). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- Population and community mean the same → one kind vs many kinds living together.
- Every habitat supports the same community → different habitats support different communities (lab 4.3 data: 3.3 to 5.3 populations).
