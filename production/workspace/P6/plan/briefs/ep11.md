## Big idea
The energy we use comes directly or indirectly from the Sun: fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas) store energy from the Sun and are non-renewable (cannot be replaced once used up; 95% of Singapore's electricity comes from natural gas); renewable sources (moving water, wind, solar) do not run out but depend on the weather and need land and money; we should use energy efficiently and sustainably.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "The energy for a fan comes from the wall socket, and nothing further back." → Trace it back through the power station and its fuel to the Sun (2.38; lab 2.3 planned mix-up). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- "Solar energy is non-renewable / the panels used up their energy." → Solar energy is renewable but depends on weather conditions (2.42, 2.43; energy is converted, not used up, 2.45). **Become quiz options A and B.**
- "Solar panels work by heat." → Light from the Sun is converted to electrical energy by solar panels (2.36). **Quiz option D.**
- "Fossil fuels can be replaced quickly / within a lifetime." → They take millions of years to form (2.32; lab 2.3 planned mix-up). Taught in `nonrenew` and `marker`.
- "Wind has nothing to do with the Sun." → Wind occurs due to the uneven heating of the Earth's surface by the Sun (2.37). Shown in `renew`.
- "Renewable sources have no drawbacks." → Dependent on weather; need large amounts of land; expensive to build (2.43). Shown in `limits`.
