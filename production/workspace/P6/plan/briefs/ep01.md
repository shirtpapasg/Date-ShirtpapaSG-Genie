## Big idea
Living things need energy to carry out life processes (moving, growing, reproducing, and staying alive); the energy in food is measured in calories; carbohydrates and proteins give 4 calories per gram, fats 9; food labels help plan a balanced diet.

## Misconceptions to bust
- We need energy only when we move → you need energy even when you are sleeping, sitting or doing nothing; energy is required to keep us alive (row 1.6). **This is the `tip` beat.**
- A heavier food always gives more energy → the energy depends on what the food is made of: each gram of fat provides 9 calories, each gram of carbohydrate or protein 4 (row 1.11; lab 1.1B Challenge mix-up "A food that weighs more always has a higher energy value"). **Quiz option B.**
- You can judge a food's energy by its taste → read the label (lab 1.1B Part (c) takeaway). **Quiz option D.**
- Only animals need energy → plants need energy too; it comes from the sugar produced during photosynthesis (row 1.9).
- The energy in our body is "used up" → never say it; say the body needs energy for life processes (style §1).

## Exam wording box
1. Living things need energy to carry out life processes such as moving, growing and reproducing.
2. Each gram of carbohydrate or protein provides 4 calories, while each gram of fat provides 9 calories.
3. Structured answer: Why does a sleeping boy still need food? A sleeping boy needs energy to stay alive (cause: energy is required to keep us alive, even during sleep), and that energy comes from food (process), so he still needs food (result).
