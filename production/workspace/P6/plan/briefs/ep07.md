## Big idea
Potential energy is the energy an object has because of its position or state: due to height above the ground (more height or more mass, more potential energy), due to being stretched or compressed, and in chemicals (batteries, food, fuels).

## Misconceptions to bust
- "A battery on its own has electrical energy." → Its chemicals have potential energy; electrical energy is present in a closed circuit (2.16, 2.20, 2.25; app lab 2.2A planned mix-up "the energy comes from the switch, not the cells"). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- "A still object has no energy." → A still object can have potential energy because of its position or state (2.10). **Becomes quiz option C.**
- "A squashed spring has kinetic energy." → It has potential energy until it is released (2.14; lab 2.4 planned mix-up "a fully compressed spring rider has kinetic energy before it is released"). **Quiz option A.**
- "Gravitational energy." → The word *potential* is needed; "potential energy" on its own is accepted (Mix-up buster #35; row 2.11). Description note.
- "Chemical energy is a separate form." → The book says potential energy in chemicals (2.15; poster conflict, `app_p6.md` §4c item 2).

## Exam wording box
