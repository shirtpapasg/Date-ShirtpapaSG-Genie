## Big idea
Frictional force is the force that opposes motion when two objects rub against each other; it slows moving objects and stops them; it is less on a smooth surface; it is useful (walking, holding, braking) and a nuisance (wear and tear, heat); it is reduced with lubricants, wheels and streamlined shapes; a moving object's kinetic energy is converted to heat energy because of friction.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "The box stopped because the push ran out." → It stopped because frictional force opposed its motion (p. 54; p. 56 I'm Inspired; recitation). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- "A smoother surface grips better." → On a smooth surface the frictional force is less (p. 57); worn, smoother soles slip more (p. 58). **Becomes quiz option A.**
- "Smooth means no frictional force at all." → Less, not none. **Quiz option C.**
- "Frictional force is always a nuisance." → It is useful for walking, holding and braking (p. 57, p. 55). Shown in `useful`.
- "Energy is used up by friction." → Some kinetic energy is converted to heat energy (p. 59). Shown in `nuisance`.
- "More heat means more stopping." → Heat is a result of frictional force, not a stronger brake. **Quiz option D.**
