## Big idea
Organisms have adaptations that help them survive in their environment: structural adaptations (body features such as a beak shape, webbed feet, a waxy leaf coating) and behavioural adaptations (what the organism does, such as being active at night to avoid heat).

## Misconceptions to bust
- Body features and behaviour are the same kind of adaptation ("webbed feet are a behavioural adaptation") → structural = what the body has; behavioural = what the organism does. **Becomes the `tip` beat and quiz options A–C.**
- Naming the feature is enough ("the shell protects it") → name the feature or behaviour, then how it helps (answer key Set 1 Q36a rejects "protect" without how).
- One adaptation is the best fit for every situation → a long-thin beak picked the most grains from narrow slots (14.0), but a broad-flat beak did best with wide slots (13.0) (lab 5.1 planned mix-up; description).
- Leaf coating does not affect water loss → waxy 0.8 ml vs non-waxy 3.2 ml (lab 5.1 Part (b)).
- An animal grows an adaptation because it wants or needs to in its lifetime → adaptations are features the organism already has (⚠ check: the book's wording on how adaptations arise is unknown; keep this out of the video, description only).
- A mudskipper is not a fish because it leaves the water → it is a fish with adaptations for life on mud.

## Exam wording box

2. Structured answer (cause → process → result): The waxy coating reduces water loss from the leaf, so the plant keeps more water and can survive in a hot, dry place. (lab 5.1 Part (b) note, ⚠ textbook unverified)
