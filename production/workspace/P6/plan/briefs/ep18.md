## Big idea
An environment is made up of living things and non-living factors (light, temperature, water, air); a habitat is the place where an organism lives and gives it air, water, food, space and shelter; non-living factors affect which organisms are found where.

## Misconceptions to bust
- More light always means more living things → organisms gather where several non-living factors suit them together; the shaded tree base had the lowest light but more living things than the lawn (lab 4.2 planned mix-up; lab 4.1 part (b)). **Becomes the `tip` beat.**
- Shade means nothing can survive there → moss, ferns and many small animals live in shade (lab 4.1 part (a) wrong reason).
- Animals need light to make food → only plants make food, using light; earthworms need moist soil, not bright light. **Becomes quiz option D.**
- Water only supports fish → water in soil supports plants, earthworms and insects too (lab 4.1 planned mix-up question).
- An environment is only the non-living surroundings → it includes the living things too (TLG KI1, 4.2).
- A habitat is just "a home" → it provides air, water, food, space and shelter (4.6).
