# EP 07 — A mushroom is NOT a plant: fungi and bacteria

## Big idea
Fungi and bacteria are living things too: fungi do not make their own food (they grow on another object, break it down and absorb it) and reproduce by spores; bacteria are tiny living things of different shapes; both can be useful or harmful; the full classification tree closes Chapters 1–2.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "A mushroom is a plant." Fungi do not make their own food.
- "Spores mean fungus." Ferns make spores too; the food test decides (the quiz table).
- "All bacteria are harmful." Useful bacteria help break down food; yoghurt.
- "Mould on bread is just dust." It is a living fungus.
- "All fungi are too small to see." Bacteria need a microscope; mushrooms and mould patches do not (never say all fungi are microscopic).
- Never draw bacteria as tiny animals with faces, arms or legs.

## Exam wording box
1. Fungi (singular: fungus) are living things that do not make their own food. Fungi grow on another object. They produce a substance to break down the other object into simpler substances and absorb them as food.
2. Bacteria (singular: bacterium) are tiny living things and they come in different shapes and sizes.
3. Living things can be classified based on common observable characteristics. Scientists have classified living things into groups such as plants, animals, fungi and bacteria.

## Description note (not in the video)
In 1928 Alexander Fleming noticed that mould in one of his dishes had stopped bacteria from growing. From that mould came penicillin, a medicine that stops harmful bacteria from growing (never "bacteria killer"). Kept out of the video so "mould is harmful" and "mould helps us" do not land a few seconds apart.
