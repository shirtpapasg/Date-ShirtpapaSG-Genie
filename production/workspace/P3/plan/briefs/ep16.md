## Big idea
Some animals have three stages in their life cycles (egg, young, adult); some young look like the adult (chick, nymph) and some do not (tadpole); young insects moult as they grow.

## Misconceptions to bust
- In P3, the frog's life cycle has three stages: the egg, the young (tadpole) and the adult (5.2, 5.11). In P3 answers, call the tadpole the frog's young. (Positive line only: do not put a ✗ on "a tadpole is a larva"; biologists do call the tadpole the frog's larva, and secondary biology uses that word.)
- "A tadpole looks like a small frog." It has a tail, no legs at first, and lives in water.
- "The young of a three-stage insect is a larva." It is a nymph that looks like a smaller adult without wings (app M-29).
- "All insects go through a pupa." Grasshoppers and cockroaches do not (app M-28).
- No stage numbers on the loops.

## Realism notes
- Chick: yellow, fluffy, two legs, small beak; adult chicken: comb, feathers, two legs.
- Frog eggs: a mass of clear jelly with dark dots; tadpole: round body, long tail, no legs at first; adult frog: four legs, webbed hind feet, moist skin, no tail.
- Nymph: like the adult grasshopper but smaller and wingless; adult with wings folded along the back; six legs on the thorax, feelers on the head.
- Moulting: the old skin is a pale, hollow copy left behind.
- Cockroach egg case: a small brown purse shape.
- Cicak: a small pale house lizard with four legs and a tail; the baby a smaller copy.
