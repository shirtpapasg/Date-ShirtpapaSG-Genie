# EP 21 — Hang a magnet and it points North–South. Every time.

## Big idea
A freely suspended magnet comes to rest in the North–South direction because Earth behaves like a magnet with its own North and South poles; a compass needle is a magnet and works the same way, so it can be used to tell direction.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "The magnet points North." It comes to rest in the North–South direction: one end faces North, the other South.
- "A compass needle points wherever you push it." It swings back to North–South.
- "The magnet points North because North is 'up'." It is the Earth's magnet interacting with it.
- "Only a compass does this." Any freely suspended magnet does (hanging, floating).
- "A nearby magnet cannot move a compass needle." The needle is a magnet, so it is attracted or repelled (the quiz).
- Use the words "freely suspended" and "comes to rest" in the exam.

## Exam wording box
1. A freely suspended magnet comes to rest in the North–South direction.
2. Earth behaves like a magnet with its own North and South poles. It interacts with a freely suspended magnet.
3. When the needle inside the compass stops moving, it always points in the North–South direction of the Earth.
