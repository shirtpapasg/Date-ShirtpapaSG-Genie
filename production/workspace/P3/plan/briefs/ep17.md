# EP 17 — The caterpillar that becomes a butterfly: 4 stages

## Big idea
Some animals have four stages (egg, larva, pupa, adult) and their young do not look like the adult: the larva eats a lot and moults many times, the pupa does not eat and develops inside the pupal case; knowing the mosquito's cycle helps stop dengue.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "The four stages are egg, young, pupa, adult." The book says egg, larva, pupa, adult (the lab's big idea uses "young"; the video uses "larva").
- "The pupa is sleeping." It is alive and developing inside the pupal case.
- "A caterpillar is a worm." It is an insect larva with six legs (plus prolegs).
- "The caterpillar wraps itself in a case." Its skin splits and a hard pupal case appears.
- "All mosquitoes spread dengue." Most do not; infected Aedes mosquitoes do.
- No stage numbers on the loops.

## Exam wording box

## Realism notes
- Caterpillar: three pairs of true legs on the thorax (front), prolegs on the abdomen; head with small eyes; it chews a leaf from the edge.
- Pupa: hangs from a stem or leaf; the caterpillar's skin splits to reveal it (no silk wrapping); closed case, no eating; glow inside for "changing".
- Mealworm larva: segmented, golden-brown, six tiny legs at the front; the beetle adult dark with wing covers.
- Mosquito: eggs on the still water surface; the wriggler hangs head-down from the surface with its tail tube at the surface; the pupa is comma-shaped at the surface; the adult has six legs and two wings.
- Stagnant water drawn flat and still; the tipped plate and the quiz pail pour water out.
- Loops: curved arrows, names, no numbers.
