## Big idea
Insects have six legs, a pair of feelers and three body parts; reptiles have dry, scaly skin, breathe through lungs and are cold-blooded; mammals have hair or fur, mostly give birth to their young alive, feed them milk and are warm-blooded; the shared characteristic tells you which animal does not belong, and a comparison always gives the reason.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "A spider is an insect." Eight legs; it belongs to a different group.
- "Wings make it an insect / a bird." Wings vary in insects; a bat has wings but fur.
- "Cold-blooded means it feels cold." It does not have a constant body temperature; its body temperature changes with the surroundings.
- "It swims, so it is a fish." Scaly skin and lungs mean reptile (the sea turtle; the lab's mystery creature).
- "Odd one out is a guess." Find the characteristic the other three share, then say it.
- Keep "most", "many" and "usually": a few mammals lay eggs; snakes have no legs; young insects have no wings.

## Exam wording box
1. Insects have six legs and a pair of feelers. The body of insects is divided into the head, thorax and abdomen.
2. Reptiles are cold-blooded animals. This means that they do not have a constant body temperature like humans. Their body temperature changes with the environment that they are in.
3. Living things such as plants and animals can be classified based on common observable characteristics.
