## Big idea
We compare life cycles by observable characteristics (the number of stages, and whether the young looks like the adult); a cycle has no first or last stage; life cycles let living things of the same kind continue.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "Comparing means only finding what is alike." Same AND different.
- "Both start as an egg, so they have the same stages." Count the stages.
- "Two four-stage animals cannot be different." Compare where the stages live (the mosquito larva and pupa near the water surface; the beetle larva on land).
- "The egg is stage one." No first or last stage; never number the loop (monograph C3).
- A comparison answer must state the basis (stages, or whether the young looks like the adult).

## Exam wording box
1. When we compare, we can use observable characteristics to identify similarities and differences between the life cycles of the animals. Observable characteristics include the number of stages and the physical features of the animals.
2. The stages in a life cycle are repeated. There is no first or last stage.
