# EP 09 — Snap it or bend it: strong vs flexible

**Topic tag:** Strength and Flexibility · **Icon:** 💪 · **Chapter:** Ch 3 pp. 53–55 (+ Let's Apply Q2, p. 68) · **Mind Lab chapter:** Diversity of Materials · **School Lab:** 3.2 The snap test · Part (a): Hold the load (alternative: 3.3 The bend test · Part (b): Bend the plastic) · **NGSS:** 2-PS1-1 · **Narrator:** Heart voice 3 · **Target:** 3:45–4:15, about 560 authored words · **Output:** `StrongFlexible`

## Big idea
A physical property is something you can measure or observe; strength is a material's ability to be pulled or pushed without breaking or tearing (a fair strength test uses strips of the same thickness and length), and flexibility is its ability to bend without breaking.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "Bending a lot means strong." Two different properties.
- "Any bend at all means flexible" (glass). Flexible means bends easily without breaking.
- "Hard and soft are the P3 properties." They are everyday words; the five properties are the list.
- "Any strips will do for a strength test." Same thickness and length, or the comparison is not fair.
- Ceramic: teach the positive lines only ("A ceramic plate breaks easily when it is dropped or knocked: it is fragile. A ceramic tile breaks when you bend it: it is rigid."). Do not put a ✗ on "ceramic is strong": the book itself calls ceramic floor tiles strong (p. 62).

## Realism notes
- Hook bags: identical size; three cans each; the paper bag's bottom tears (a clean pull failure, no sagging or soaking); the plastic bag holds.
- Strips in the snap test are clamped at both ends and sag downward as weights are hung from the middle; the snap is a clean break; all four strips are the same thickness and length.
- Bending: a smooth curve for rubber and plastic, a tiny curve for metal; the ceramic tile cracks straight across.
- Hands pulling the wire and thread pull in opposite directions; the thread frays and snaps.
