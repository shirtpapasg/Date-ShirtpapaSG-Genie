# EP 15 — One mango seed to a tree full of mangoes: 3 stages

**Topic tag:** Plant Life Cycle · **Icon:** 🥭 · **Chapter:** Ch 4 pp. 76–79 (+ Let's Apply Q1, p. 94) · **Mind Lab chapter:** Life Cycles · **School Lab:** 4.2 Seed to plant · Part (b): Build the life cycle · **NGSS:** 3-LS1-1 · **Narrator:** Heart voice 3 · **Target:** 3:30–4:00, about 500 authored words · **Output:** `PlantLifeCycle`

## Big idea
A plant's life cycle has three stages: the seed, the young plant and the adult plant; the adult has flowers and fruits with seeds inside, so the cycle repeats, and seed banks keep seeds safe.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "The stages are seed, seedling, plant" or "seed, flower, fruit". Use the book's names: seed, young plant, adult plant (seedling is a correct science word, but the exam name is young plant; the lab bench says "seedling").
- "The cycle starts at the seed and ends at the adult." It repeats; no first or last stage; no numbers (monograph C3).
- "New seeds come from the roots or the soil." They form inside the fruit.
- "A young plant has flowers." Flowers and fruits belong to the adult plant (the quiz).
- Keep it light on how a seed starts growing (water, air, warmth is P5 content; never name soil as a need).
