## MOE MONOGRAPH CROSS-CHECK (2025 Primary Science Monograph, read in full 11 Oct 2026)

**What the monograph is.** *Curricular Resource Integration and Innovation for Singapore Primary Science: Teachers as Pedagogical Designers for Implementation Integrity* (MOE and NIE, 2025; its author list includes «Tan, A.-L.», which matches P3 textbook author Tan Aik Ling). Section A sets out the 2023 syllabus (five themes, essential takeaways, key inquiry questions, the Conceptual Flow Diagram and the 3C lesson frame: Capture, Construct, Consolidate). Section B describes the resource suite (Textbook, Activity Book, SPARKLE kits, SLS, Young Scientist Badge Scheme, Teaching and Learning Guide). Section C tells nine classroom stories; four are P3 topics (C1 bacteria and fungi, C2 materials, C3 life cycles, C4 magnets). It has **no glossary and defines almost no P3 terms**, so the textbook stays the wording source for every definition above. The monograph's value is (1) the official theme-level wording and (2) the alternative conceptions it names.

### Conflicts and differences found (all also noted in the table rows)

1. **Comparing (2.4)** ⚠ CONFLICT. Book p. 20: similarities only. Monograph: always «similarities and differences» (C3 p. 31; Table 4 p. 19). The book's own p. 90 sentence (5.12) also says similarities and differences. **Rule:** teach comparing as finding what is the same and what is different.
2. **Classification wording (D0.1, 1.3)** ⚠ The book's pp. 3 and 5 lines leave out differences. Monograph Table 1 and book pp. 20 and 43 include them. **Rule:** use 2.33.
3. **Fungi and microscopes (2.27, 2.30)** ⚠ CONFLICT in scope. Monograph C1 (p. 21): «Bacteria and fungi are microscopic organisms that are not visible to our unaided eyes.» The book's fungi include mushrooms and bread mould you can see. **Rule:** never say *all fungi are microscopic*; bacteria need a microscope, mushrooms do not.
4. **Cycles question C1 (C0.4)** ⚠ minor wording difference (*makes a cycle* vs *makes up a cycle*). Use the book's.
5. **Physical properties (3.10)** ⚠ the monograph's classroom uses *hard* and *soft* as property words. Hardness is not one of the five P3 properties; keep it as an everyday word only.
6. **Mammals (2.22–2.23)** no conflict: the monograph's general statement is followed by the platypus exception, which is exactly why the book says *Most mammals give birth to their young alive*.

### Alternative conceptions the monograph names (use them in Exam corner and myth-buster beats)

| Topic | Monograph wording | Where in the book it is answered |
|---|---|---|
| Life cycles | «the common alternative conception that the stages of a life cycle can be numbered» | 5.16 (p. 93): no first or last stage. Production rule: no Stage 1/2/3 numbers on cycle diagrams. |
| Magnets | «all magnetic materials are magnets» | 6.3–6.4: magnets attract magnetic materials; only magnets repel. |
| Magnets | «objects are magnets if they can be attracted» | 6.13 (p. 109): repulsion is the test. |
| Bacteria and fungi | Children often link microbes only with disease and picture them as tiny animals with arms and legs (C1, p. 21, citing research). | 2.29–2.31: useful and harmful fungi and bacteria; shapes of bacteria (rod, sphere, spiral). |
| Flowering plants | Grass is a flowering plant whose flowers are usually cut short, so they are seldom seen (C7, p. 58, a P4 lesson). | 2.6–2.7: flowering plants bear flowers. |
| Generalisations | «Given that variabilities exist in nature, there are exceptions to generalised scientific knowledge.» (C7, p. 55) | Keep *usually*, *most* and *some* in 2.5, 2.13, 2.15–2.16, 2.19, 2.20, 2.23. |
