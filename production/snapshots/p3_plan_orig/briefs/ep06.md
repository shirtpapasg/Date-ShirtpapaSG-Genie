## Big idea
Insects have six legs, a pair of feelers and three body parts; reptiles have dry, scaly skin, breathe through lungs and are cold-blooded; mammals have hair or fur, mostly give birth to their young alive, feed them milk and are warm-blooded.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "A spider is an insect." Eight legs.
- "Wings make it an insect / a bird." Wings vary in insects; a bat has wings but fur.
- "Cold-blooded means it feels cold." Its body temperature changes with the surroundings.
- "It swims, so it is a fish." Dry scaly skin and lungs mean reptile (the lab's mystery creature).
- Keep "most" and "usually": a few mammals lay eggs; snakes have no legs.

## Exam wording box
1. Insects have six legs and a pair of feelers. The body of insects is divided into the head, thorax and abdomen.
2. Reptiles usually have dry and scaly skin. They breathe through their lungs. Most reptiles reproduce by laying eggs.
3. Reptiles are cold-blooded animals. Their body temperature changes with the environment that they are in.
4. Mammals are animals that have hair or fur as their outer covering. Most mammals give birth to their young alive. Mammals are warm-blooded, which means that they can maintain a constant body temperature.
