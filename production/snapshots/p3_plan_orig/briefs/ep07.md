# EP 07 — A mushroom is NOT a plant: fungi and bacteria

## Big idea
Fungi and bacteria are living things too: fungi do not make their own food (they grow on another object, break it down and absorb it) and reproduce by spores; bacteria are tiny living things of different shapes; both can be useful or harmful.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "A mushroom is a plant." Fungi do not make their own food.
- "Spores mean fungus." Ferns make spores too; the food test decides.
- "All bacteria are harmful." Useful bacteria help break down food; yoghurt.
- "Mould on bread is just dust." It is a living fungus.
- "All fungi are too small to see." Bacteria need a microscope; mushrooms and mould patches do not (never say all fungi are microscopic).
- Never draw bacteria as tiny animals with faces, arms or legs.

## Exam wording box
1. Fungi (singular: fungus) are living things that do not make their own food. Fungi grow on another object. They produce a substance to break down the other object into simpler substances and absorb them as food.
2. Fungi can reproduce very quickly through spores.
3. Bacteria (singular: bacterium) are tiny living things and they come in different shapes and sizes.
4. Some bacteria are harmful, while others are useful to us. Fungi and bacteria are also living things.
