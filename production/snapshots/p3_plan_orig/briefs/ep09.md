# EP 09 — Snap it or bend it: strong vs flexible

**Topic tag:** Strength and Flexibility · **Icon:** 💪 · **Chapter:** Ch 3 pp. 53–55 · **Mind Lab chapter:** Diversity of Materials · **School Lab:** 3.2 The snap test · Part (a): Hold the load (alternative: 3.3 The bend test · Part (b): Bend the plastic) · **NGSS:** 2-PS1-1 · **Narrator:** Heart voice 3 · **Target:** 3:45–4:15, about 540 authored words · **Output:** `StrongFlexible`

## Big idea
A physical property is something you can measure or observe; strength is a material's ability to be pulled or pushed without breaking or tearing, and flexibility is its ability to bend without breaking.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "Bending a lot means strong." Two different properties.
- "Any bend at all means flexible" (glass). Flexible means bends easily without breaking.
- "Ceramic is strong." Default to fragile and rigid (the book calls floor tiles strong only in one example).
- "Hard and soft are the P3 properties." They are everyday words; the five properties are the list.
- Fair comparisons: strips of the same thickness and length (Let's Apply Q2).

## Exam wording box
1. The physical property of a material is something that you can measure or observe.
2. The strength of a material is its ability to be pulled or pushed without breaking or tearing.
3. The flexibility of a material is its ability to bend without breaking.
4. Materials that bend easily without breaking are flexible. Materials that break easily when bent are rigid.
