# EP 19 — Why a magnet ignores a copper coin

**Topic tag:** Magnetic Materials · **Icon:** 🧲 · **Chapter:** Theme Opener Interactions (p. 99) + Ch 6 pp. 100–104 · **Mind Lab chapter:** Magnets · **School Lab:** 6.1B Magnetic materials · Part (a): Test the metal objects · **NGSS:** 3-PS2-3 · **Narrator:** Heart voice 3 · **Target:** 3:30–4:00, about 500 authored words · **Output:** `MagneticMaterials`

## Big idea
A magnet is an object usually made of iron or steel; it attracts objects made of magnetic materials (iron and steel) and not objects made of non-magnetic materials, which include the metals aluminium and copper.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "A magnet attracts all metals / shiny metals." Only magnetic materials such as iron and steel; aluminium and copper are not (app M-35).
- "Say stick." Say attract (pull); the book's word.
- Do not name nickel or cobalt (syllabus: not required).
- "Anything attracted is a magnet." Not yet: EP 20 shows that only repulsion proves it.

## Exam wording box
1. A magnet is an object that is usually made of iron or steel.
2. Magnets attract objects made of magnetic materials. Magnetic materials include iron and steel.
3. Objects that are not attracted to magnets are made of non-magnetic materials. Non-magnetic materials include ceramic, fabric, glass, plastic, rubber and wood. Metals such as aluminium and copper are also non-magnetic.
4. A magnet can interact with objects that are made of magnetic materials.

- The lodestone is a dark rough rock with small nails clinging to it.
- The floating globe hovers with a visible gap above its base.
- Six magnet shapes drawn true: U-shaped and horseshoe have poles at the two tips; the ring has poles on its flat faces; the button is a flat disc.
