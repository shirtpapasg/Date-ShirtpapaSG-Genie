# EP 18 — Frog vs mosquito: same or different? How to compare

**Topic tag:** Comparing Life Cycles · **Icon:** ⚖️ · **Chapter:** Ch 5 pp. 90–98 (+ Let's Apply Q2–Q3, pp. 95–96) · **Mind Lab chapter:** Life Cycles · **School Lab:** 5.2 Compare life cycles · Part (b): Different number of stages · **NGSS:** 3-LS1-1 · **Narrator:** Heart voice 3 · **Target:** 3:45–4:15, about 540 authored words · **Output:** `CompareLifeCycles`

## Big idea
We compare life cycles by observable characteristics (the number of stages, and whether the young looks like the adult); a cycle has no first or last stage; life cycles let living things of the same kind continue.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "Comparing means only finding what is alike." Same AND different.
- "Both start as an egg, so they have the same stages." Count the stages.
- "The egg is stage one." No first or last stage; never number the loop (monograph C3).
- A comparison answer must state the basis (stages, or whether the young looks like the adult).
- Mosquito vs mealworm beetle difference (Let's Apply Q2): the mosquito larva and pupa live near the water surface; the beetle larva lives on land (do not claim all beetle stages are on land).

## Exam wording box
1. When we compare, we can use observable characteristics to identify similarities and differences between the life cycles of the animals. Observable characteristics include the number of stages and the physical features of the animals.
2. The life cycles of the frog and the mosquito are similar because the young do not look like the adults. However, the life cycles are different because the frog has three stages in its life cycle, while the mosquito has four stages in its life cycle.
3. The stages in a life cycle are repeated. There is no first or last stage.
4. Life cycles ensure that living things of the same kind can continue to live on Earth.
