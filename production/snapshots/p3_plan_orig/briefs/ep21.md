## Big idea
A freely suspended magnet comes to rest in the North–South direction because Earth behaves like a magnet with its own North and South poles; a compass needle is a magnet and works the same way, so it can be used to tell direction.

## Misconceptions to bust
- "A compass needle points wherever you push it." It swings back to North–South.
- "The magnet points North because North is 'up'." It is the Earth's magnet interacting with it.
- "Only a compass does this." Any freely suspended magnet does (hanging, floating).
- Use the words "freely suspended" and "comes to rest" in the exam.

## Exam wording box
1. A freely suspended magnet comes to rest in the North–South direction.
2. Earth behaves like a magnet with its own North and South poles. It interacts with a freely suspended magnet.
3. Hence, a freely suspended magnet can be used to tell the direction.
4. When the needle inside the compass stops moving, it always points in the North–South direction of the Earth.

## Realism notes
- The hanging magnet: string tied at the magnet's middle, magnet level (horizontal), free to turn; it slows and stops, red end toward N.
- Compass: round, rose with N at the top, E right, S bottom, W left; needle red end pointing to N.
- Earth: draw N and S labels at the top and bottom; say "behaves like a magnet" and do not name which magnetic pole is where (beyond P3).
- Spoon compass: bronze spoon on a flat square plate; it settles along a N–S line; do not mark which end points South.
- The globe floats with a gap above the base; push arrows between like poles.
