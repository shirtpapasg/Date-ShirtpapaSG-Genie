# TheOneApp, Explained: the new format (proposal, 19 Nov 2026)

Built from four independent Fable brainstorms (game theory, learning science, kids' media, wildcard; their reports are in this folder)
and the teacher's goal: a channel that explodes in views and that teachers around the world praise and go to.

## The idea in one line
**You shrink into the science, then you play against a rival to prove you understood it.**
Explored's first-person journey (our signature) + a game the viewer plays with their hands + exam-exact "Mark Sentences".

## Why this can beat the big explainer channels
Popular explainer shows for kids are a host explaining to a sidekick: two characters talk, the child watches. Ours adds the two
things they don't have:
1. **The child is a player, not an audience.** Every episode is a match: You vs the rival. The child commits to an answer before
   every reveal and keeps score.
2. **The child is inside the science.** The camera IS the water molecule, the electric current, the pollen grain.
3. **Teachers get exam-grade accuracy and a free classroom kit**, which is what turns teachers into fans who share it.

We take lessons from what works (a recurring duo, banter, a catchphrase, short episodes), never anyone's characters, names or look.

## The cast (three, simple to draw consistently)
- **Heart**: the warm, fair host (voice). She never says anything wrong.
- **Chop**: a cheeky Singapore mynah (black oval body, yellow beak and eye patch, stick legs). Very sure, often wrong, always a good
  sport. His wrong answers are the real misconceptions pupils bring to exams. He is right about one round in three, and sometimes
  right for the wrong reason (no point), so "pick the opposite of Chop" never works.
- **You**: a pair of hands and a "YOU" tag, as in Explored's first-person camera.
- Running gag: a tiny cicak hidden somewhere in every episode.

## The lock-in (the answer to "is pausing even possible?")
A YouTube video can't stop or check. So we never ask children to press pause and never leave dead air. We design a lock-in:
- a closed question with 2 or 3 choices, answered with the body: fingers (1, 2, 3) or point left/right;
- 5 to 6 seconds, a draining ring, music drops to a speeding-up tick, a bell, "Locked in!";
- the child locks first, THEN Chop reveals his pick (so children think instead of copying or opposing him);
- the score is kept on the other hand (one finger per point; a perfect game is a high five);
- "No lock-in? You ride with Chop." (doing nothing is a choice, and usually a losing one);
- the reveal speaks to every choice, so a child who never played still learns everything.
In a classroom a small bell icon marks the teacher's pause points (eyes shut, vote, pair talk). In TheOneApp the video truly waits
for a tap.

## Episode skeleton (about 6 minutes)
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Silent cold open: a real Singapore problem with a deadline |
| 0:12 | The dare: Chop boasts, the rules, the season score (You vs Chop), "a cicak is hiding in this video" |
| 0:30 | Round Zero: Chop's revenge question from last episode (5 s lock-in) |
| 0:45 | "Are you ready? Three! Two! One! Go!" The first-person journey: the one big idea (about 60 s) |
| 1:45 | Round 1 (winnable): set-up as a fair test, lock-in, Chop's pick, the race, the Mark Sentence |
| 2:45 | Round 2: Chop's big fail (the laugh lands where viewers usually drift) |
| 3:45 | FOUL!: Chop runs an unfair test; cross your arms when you spot it; Heart fixes it |
| 4:30 | Showdown (double points): the hardest idea, then Chop's written exam answer with one mistake to spot |
| 5:20 | Payoff: the problem is solved; the Mark Sentences fly past as a win strip; show your score hand |
| 5:45 | Did you spot the cicak? Chop's Revenge (next episode's dare). "Now you know!" Hard cut. |
Each round is a ready-made Short.

## Worked example: P5 evaporation, "Dry by 7.30?"
An HDB flat in Tampines, 9 pm, raining. Ming's only PE shirt is soaked; PE is at 7.30 am.
- Journey: you are a tiny bit of water on the shirt; you gain heat from the surrounding air and change into water vapour, which no one
  can see. It happens at any temperature, no boiling needed.
- Round 1, exposed surface area: bunched on the chair vs spread on the bamboo pole. Mark Sentence: "The larger the exposed surface
  area, the faster the rate of evaporation."
- Round 2, wind: fan vs still air. Chop: "Fan air is cool, cool is slow!" He loses. "The presence of wind increases the rate of
  evaporation."
- FOUL!: Chop's test puts a spread shirt in the sun against a crumpled one in the shade: two things changed. Re-run fairly: sun vs
  shelter. "The higher the temperature of the surroundings, the faster the rate of evaporation."
- Showdown, humidity: same corridor, same temperature, a dry day vs just after rain. "The higher the humidity, the slower the rate of
  evaporation." Chop's exam answer: "The fan made the water disappear." Spot it: water does not disappear; it evaporates into water
  vapour.
- Payoff: shirt spread out, near the fan, by the open window once the rain stops. 7.25 am: dry.
- Chop's Revenge: why is there water on the OUTSIDE of a cold kopi-peng cup?
(All Mark Sentences to be checked against the P5 textbook before scripting; results shown as order and real teacher-run data, never
invented drying times.)

## Growth plan: views and teachers worldwide
1. **Titles that children and teachers both search**: the dare first, the topic last. "Can You Dry a PE Shirt by 7.30? | Evaporation
   for Kids". The same thumbnail layout every time (the problem, Chop's face, three words).
2. **Shorts engine**: three Shorts per episode (a round, "Spot Chop's mistake", a misconception buster). Every Short ends on a correct
   statement and points to the full episode.
3. **"Learn science the Singapore way"**: Singapore pupils are consistently top of the world in international science tests (TIMSS).
   Each description lists the Singapore syllabus topic and the matching topic in other curricula (e.g. US NGSS, England KS2, Cambridge
   Primary), so teachers abroad find it and trust it.
4. **A free teacher kit per episode** in TheOneApp: the pause points, the misconception, the fair test to run for real, the answer
   frame, the marking points. This is what makes teachers share it.
5. **TheOneApp as the game's home**: real lock-in where the video waits, tracked score and streaks, a stamp book (the cicak code), a
   class mode with the live vote on the teacher's screen, "Fix Chop's answer" from keyword tiles.
6. **Later: more languages**, as extra audio tracks where YouTube allows it.
7. A steady weekly rhythm and theme playlists.
Honest note: nothing guarantees a viral channel. These are the levers that the biggest kids' channels pull, plus interaction and
classroom value, which most of them lack.

## Where it doesn't fit, and the fix
Topics with variables (evaporation, heat, light, forces, electricity, plant growth) fit the race perfectly. For classification,
life cycles and body systems, the rounds become "Sort it" (put it through the right gate), "Order it" (put the stages in order) and
"Trace it" (pick the path through the body), with the same lock-in, Chop and score.

## How we'll know it works
- YouTube: retention at 0:30 and at each lock-in, average % viewed vs the current Explained episode on the same topic, rewatch bumps,
  returning viewers, Shorts → long video.
- Classroom: two classes, two topics, swapped formats; a quiz straight after and one week later, including an open-ended question.
- App: code words entered, lock-in rate, quiz scores.

## Decisions for the teacher
1. The rival: Chop the mynah (recommended), or a robot, or a pupil character.
2. Catchphrase: "Lock it in!" (global) or a Singlish touch such as "Confirm plus chop!".
3. Pilot: P5 evaporation as one full episode + three Shorts + the teacher sheet, scripted first for approval.
