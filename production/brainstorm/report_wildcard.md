# Lens 4 (wildcard): "One Dial" — summary of the Fable agent's report (19 Nov 2026)
- Each episode is a game level: a real Singapore problem with a clock; a control panel of the topic's variables as dials; ONE RULE: turn only one dial per move (the game rule IS the fair test).
- 60 s move loop x4: two lanes with one dial different (12 s) → three on-screen players lock in with reasons (8 s; Bo always holds the misconception) → "Point at your lane. 5,4,3,2,1. Locked!" (5 s) → side-by-side race (15 s) → zoom lens: why (10 s) → keyword chip earned + exam sentence said once slowly (10 s). Move 3 twist: a player turns two dials, panel buzzes "Not a fair test". Move 4: the locked dial (boss stat). Boss round: PSLE question, earned chips drop into the model answer. Level code (pictures) → unlocks the dial lab in TheOneApp. Each move = a ready-made Short.
- Evaporation example "Dry by 6:30": Toa Payoh HDB, rainy December night, Wei's soaked PE shirt; dials: exposed surface area, wind, temperature, humidity (padlocked HIGH because it's raining). Players Mei (careful), Arjun (bold), Bo (gut/misconception). Think about it: why does laundry dry in an air-conditioned room?
- Pause answer: asking a child to press pause is mostly hoping; never ask for it in the YouTube cut. Instead: near-free response (point left/right), both outcomes always play so the child sees their own choice win/lose, side with a player ("with Mei or Bo?"), 5 s loud beat with "Locked!", the loss belongs to Bo. Checked interaction lives in the app's stop-and-wait player (same mp4, app pauses at each countdown with two big buttons) and in class (left half of room stands for left lane).
- Cost: first episode ~2x Explored (reusable kit), then about the same. Risk: only fits topics with variables (evaporation, heat, light, forces, electricity, photosynthesis, plant growth); needs sister mechanics for classification, life cycles, body systems. Never show invented drying times (order only, or real teacher-run data). Zoom lens labelled "extra" beyond syllabus.
